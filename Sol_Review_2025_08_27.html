Sol Review: recall, drift, quest sprawl, and efficiency

Evidence review of v1.714 at 03fdfeeb · run 2026-08-27 · requested filename retained verbatim as Sol_Review_2025_08_27.html
Bottom line: the game is going in the right direction. Its strongest systems treat recall as durable state, keep volatile campaign truth out of cached prompt bytes, retrieve old scenes on demand, preserve the sacred transcript, and make many failures loud. The arc wall also targets the right measured cause of quest sprawl: emergent side threads surviving beyond the arc that created them. But the implementation is not yet a closed lifecycle. ACT_COMPLETE can bypass the wall and strand a child forever, unstamped or renamed quests can escape it, and the wall bounds neither within-arc spawning nor indefinite continuation. Keep the direction; harden the lifecycle before declaring quest sprawl solved.
Review posture: this is a Sol evidence report and prioritized todo list, not a formal Fable verdict. The repository’s drift-surface workflow reserves final adjudication and implementation of parser, prompt, memory, and state changes for Fable. Every drift-surface item below is therefore marked as a review candidate with an executable acceptance condition.

P0 — protect canon and close quest bypasses

P0-01 · Make act completion atomic with its child arcs

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · tag_table.js:876-955 · engine-tests

Why [ACT_COMPLETE:Totally Wrong] closes the first active act because the handler ignores its operand. It leaves that act’s arc active, opens the next act, and performs no arc-wall sweep. The abandoned child can never be swept later because ARC_COMPLETE searches active acts only. A non-final ARC_COMPLETE + ACT_COMPLETE response also leaves the newly activated old-act arc alive inside a completed act.

Accept when wrong/malformed act titles mutate nothing and warn loudly; an act cannot complete with a live child arc unless the transition explicitly resolves that arc and its stamped descendants; no completed act contains an active arc; valid final co-emission remains safe; non-final co-emission is mutation-tested.

Direct runtime repro; tag_table.js:938-955. This is the highest-confidence quest-wall bypass.

P0-02 · Never replace corrupt recall stores with blank state silently

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · state.js:467-478

Why corrupt sessionLog JSON becomes [], and corrupt memory JSON becomes blankMemory(), without warning or rescue. Transcript inflation already follows the correct pattern: retain the original, warn the player, and expose recovery. These two paths can erase the layers that make accurate recall work while making the campaign appear healthy.

Accept when malformed stored JSON is reproduced first; the original bytes are preserved in a bounded rescue record/export; load fails loudly or enters an explicit degraded state; no blank replacement is persisted until the player chooses recovery.

Code-path audit contrasted with transcript rescue at state.js:101-147.

P0-03 · Preserve NPC death-correction receipts across .tnd import

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · ui-files.js:449-480 · import contract

Why death retraction stores reversible canon in memory.archive.npcDeathCorrections, but the import archive whitelist omits that key. Export/import can silently discard the preimage needed by later correction logic. The “full archive” test repeats the same stale static allowlist, so the loss ships green.

Accept when a failing round-trip fixture proves the key is lost; current and future archive categories round-trip through one schema/registry rather than copied allowlists; an unknown category is preserved or rejected loudly; the repaired fixture is byte/meaning equivalent.

tag_table.js:503-518, ui-files.js:449-480, dev/run-tests.js:281-298.

P0-04 · Validate the quest state machine and protect archived identity

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · tag_table.js:542-599 · pending Fable entry 21

Why arbitrary status text such as paused is accepted into the live store, then vanishes from every active/offered prompt reader. A declined title can be resurrected because the reopen guard covers abandoned/completed/failed but not declined; a later archive overwrites the title-keyed history row. That is silent quest-canon loss, not merely UI polish.

Accept when all writers dispatch through one explicit status-transition table; invalid status is rejected loudly; declined/reoffered semantics are owner-decided and history-preserving; every stored live status is visible to prompt, health, UI, export, and Table Talk readers; sabotage proves the rejection and history cases.

Runtime probes reproduced invisible paused and declined resurrection. Current transitions span parser and journal UI.

P1 — recall completeness and runtime reliability

P1-01 · Make long-tail facts visible to at least one recall lane

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · memory.js:1027-1033, 1722-1785

Why a runtime probe ranked the correct old turn, but the fact lived beyond the 700-character GM excerpt and was trimmed out. The same fact was beyond the summarizer’s 500-character player / 4,000-character assistant heads, so neither RAG rendering nor durable filing exposed it. The full transcript retained the truth but both active recall lanes hid it.

Accept when a failing fixture places the only distinguishing fact at each current boundary; at least one injected lane returns it within a bounded budget; controls prove ordinary excerpts and cache keys remain stable; retrieval metrics report selected-but-trimmed hits.

P1-02 · Make active-NPC detail activation case-insensitive and identity-aware

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · api.js:1665-1668

Why hot NPC memory checks use case-sensitive recent.indexOf(name/alias). Lowercase or case-varied mentions can fail to inject durable details even though RAG name detection already handles case.

Accept when canonical name, alias, case variants, and safe word boundaries activate the same NPC; partial-name false positives remain rejected; the implementation reuses the identity resolver rather than adding a second recognizer.

P1-03 · Replace display-title quest parentage with durable identity and provenance

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · helpers.js:353-379 · tag_table.js:548-582, 887-910

Why both quest identity and bornArc parentage are title strings. Renaming an arc or rephrasing a quest creates orphans/duplicates. Wall closure is stored as generic abandoned; the reopen message then falsely says the player dropped it. Archive projection also discards bornArc.

Accept when quests and arcs have migration-safe stable identities (or an equally strong reconciliation design); display renames preserve linkage; archive rows retain parent and closure author/reason; player abandonment and arc closure render differently; import/migration/sync round-trips are tested.

P1-04 · Close unstamped quest escape routes

FABLE REVIEW REQUIRED · quest/arc lifecycle

Why after the last arc closes but before an act transition, a reoffered quest is accepted with no active parent and remains immune forever because acceptance/updates never backfill bornArc. Two-live parallel acts also deliberately omit a stamp, while a parallel act with one remaining arc unexpectedly stamps. Legacy and spine-titled quests are immune too.

Accept when every allowed exemption is explicit and documented; no-active-parent creation either waits, attaches to an explicit scope, or is rejected loudly; acceptance can safely backfill eligible legacy offers; parallel semantics are decided by act.parallel, not an accidental live-count side effect.

P1-05 · Measure and govern within-arc quest hydra growth

DESIGN + FIELD TELEMETRY · #231 follow-through

Why the wall bounds intended cross-arc lifetime only. A probe created 120 stamped live quests in one arc, producing a 36,084-character quest block; one quest accepted 80 objectives. ARC_CONTINUE can keep an arc and all descendants alive indefinitely. Rephrased titles remain independent.

Accept when a post-v1.714 field campaign records per-arc peak live/offered counts, creations, age, continuations, prompt bytes, and semantic-near-duplicate rate; the owner then chooses a soft nudge, hierarchy, or hard bound from evidence. Do not trim canon merely to improve a metric.

P1-06 · Certify late-game model behavior on a frozen mature fixture

OPEN TODO #226 · provider/model validation

Why fresh 50-turn sweeps do not represent a 2,000-turn prompt. Prior late-game evidence showed prose shrinking and voice/mechanics drift even while engine contracts stayed green. The t2097 prompt is about 126k characters before user input, making this a separate product surface.

Accept when a sanitized frozen mature save drives a fixed 20-turn scenario across the supported model ladder; prose length, canon recall, mechanics, tag compliance, latency, and cache behavior are compared to an accepted baseline; failures are stored as replayable artifacts.

P1-07 · Give TTS/STT full-operation deadlines and generation ownership

NORMAL FILES · tts.js:2400-2558 · stt.js:438-660

Why TTS clears its abort timer after response headers, before json()/arrayBuffer(); a mocked stalled body stayed pending after the accelerated deadline with controller.aborted=false. STT upload has no deadline. Multiple uploads share mutable _baseText, so an older completion can overwrite or auto-send a later recording.

Accept when timers cover header and body consumption; timeout aborts release playing/busy state and drain or preserve the queue explicitly; STT results carry a recording generation and stale generations cannot write or send; stalled-body and out-of-order probes fail first, then pass.

P1-08 · Make reroll rollback transactional

NORMAL FILE · game.js:1901-1952

Why reroll replaces the session pair and transcript before display/TTS/save. A later synchronous throw enters catch and pushes the originals without undoing the replacement, creating doubled session history and a mismatched transcript. Main turns have a committed latch; reroll does not.

Accept when a forced failure after each mutation boundary leaves transcript, sessionLog, story DOM, speaker map, and persisted state exactly at the pre-reroll snapshot; success commits once; retry cannot double-apply.

P2 — efficiency, observability, and documentation

P2-01 · Collapse each committed turn to one UI commit and one final save

CROSS-SURFACE DESIGN · api.js:2283-2347 · game.js:1699-1703, 1858-1863

P2-07 · Harden low-frequency browser I/O failures

NORMAL FILES · ui-files.js · sw.js

These are lower canon risks than P0/P1, but they violate the no-silent-failure rule and accumulate reliability debt.

Checked — defenses to keep

Keep the single tag registry and strip contract

103 handlers and 110 stripped names were censused; no handler-without-strip or accepted-but-visible leak was found. Unknown tags warn and are removed from player prose. The focused tag coverage/derivation suite passed.

Keep the stable/volatile prompt split and STYLE authority order

A fixed-world probe held stable bytes identical while turn, sheet, quest, and session mutations changed volatile bytes. STYLE remains after identity/recall content, with only the documented multiplayer/person override following it. The 10 focused cache-split assertions passed.

Keep the two-lane RAG design and one-entry memo

Rare-word retrieval and an old alias after merge both returned the correct origin scene. On t2097, ten cold queries took 3.1–18.1 ms and memo hits 0.079–0.138 ms with identical output. RAG is not the present performance bottleneck.

Keep the sacred transcript and byte-identical compression memo

The transcript remains append-only in memory/export and compressed only at storage/wire boundaries. The memo changes cost, not bytes. The 9 focused transcript-LZ assertions passed. The fix target is synchronous work placement, never transcript deletion.

Keep health/provenance rings and loud wall output

Tag and health logs are bounded at 40; refused operations have bounded verbatim receipts with sentinels. Arc-wall closure produces mutation, toast, system message, and console output. These make field failures attributable.

Keep recent monotonic-resource fixes

Story DOM caps, voice-model LRU/delete, sync compression/telemetry, listener cleanup, NPC live-fact bounds, campaign render-pointer cap, and Piper realm recycling are present. Do not reopen them without field evidence.

Quest-sprawl deep dive

Direction supported. The historical failure was not merely “quests take too long.” The measured campaign accumulated emergent side threads whose lifetimes crossed the campaign spine. Stamping a side quest to its creating arc, warning near the wall, and archiving unfinished descendants when that arc closes is a targeted response to that mechanism.

Closure not supported yet. The wall is conditional on a new quest receiving an exact title-string stamp and a later matching ARC_COMPLETE running while its act is active. That makes the mechanism a useful guard, not a hard lifecycle invariant.
