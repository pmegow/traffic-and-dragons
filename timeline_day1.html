Renumbered  This report was written while the engine labelled a campaign’s first day Day 0. As of v1.498 day labels are 1-based, so the stretch analysed here — turns 1050–1265 — now displays in-game as Day 2. No measurement changed: it is the same clock range (1440–2483 elapsed minutes) and only the label moved. The filename and headings below are left as written so existing links keep working.

Read this before trusting the numbers. The engine does not store per-turn time. [TIME_ADVANCE:] is stripped before the transcript is written, and the "Time +Xm" line the parser builds is discarded — applyMuts() returns it and the call site never captures the return. The only value that persists is the single running total worldState.clock.min.

So the per-turn minutes below are reconstructed, not recorded: every turn’s narration was read and priced under the exact reference scale the GM itself is given. The 1043-minute actual figure IS real — it is the difference between the Day 1 dawn boundary (clock 1440) and the stored clock (2483). Treat individual rows as estimates; treat the totals as directional.

Two separate causes, both real

Cause 1  The scale is priced per action, not per scene. Conversation accounts for 100 of 216 turns — nearly half of all play — and the reference scale prices it at 1–5 minutes. At ~4 minutes a turn, a 16-hour waking day needs roughly 200 turns to fill, which is exactly what you are seeing. The scale times the words spoken, not the scene that contains them: the walk over, the waiting, and the aftermath are all free.

Cause 2  The clock silently loses time it is never told about. The narration describes 2332 minutes; the clock recorded 1043. Even allowing for error in the reconstruction, a 2.24× gap is structural rather than noise. The mechanism is the first line of the handler: if(!ts.length)return; — a turn carrying no [TIME_ADVANCE:] tag advances zero minutes, with no warning and no floor. clockAdvance has exactly one caller, so nothing backstops it.

The visible symptom: the stored clock reads Day 1, 17h23m elapsed — about 11pm — while world.time says “early morning” and the t1263 narration has “Sandpoint waking up around you both.” The story slept; the clock did not. The five jump turns alone carry 555 narrated minutes the clock had no way to receive.
