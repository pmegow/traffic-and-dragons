The assessment

The emotional progression from grieving griffin to bereaved house to a dragon protecting its child is coherent and attractive. Ilsabet’s final choice is explicitly hers, and the outline permits more than an arena solution in principle. The fragile parts are the assassin’s prescribed escape, his contradictory knowledge, some relic affordances, and an ending that assumes the egg arrives intact.

What to preserve

Keep the princess’s consent centralThe final choice allows Ilsabet to return or become an envoy, and says the rescue is not a leash. That is a strong payoff to learning that she left voluntarily.

Keep the dragon a personVaurach has an intelligible grievance and an articulate response to it. The story has reasons to speak with him before a fight, beyond simply making him invulnerable.

Keep the physical cluesTorch burns, livery, an empty nest, cut webbing and the egg create opportunities for observation alongside testimony. Make these clues carry more of the conclusion.

Keep Gorrak’s grandmother’s storyA remembered kindness changes why a feared gladiator would help a dragon. It makes an alliance feel earned rather than mechanically purchased.

Improvements worth making

High: resolve before relying on the affected scene in a player-facing playtest. Medium: a concrete improvement to clarity, agency or payoff. These are editorial priorities, not measured gameplay failure rates.

High priority · 1 of 6

Make the relic contract survive loading and explain each offered use

Why this improves play

The 779-character relic rule is cut to 400 by the actual loader, losing the cloak’s memory effect, the lantern counter, the bow, the peace-only shield and the self-deception reminder from that rule. Some survive in other fields, but the fireproof shield has no equivalent full instruction there. Separately, a blade-catching gauntlet is listed as an answer to a gaze and a bow that shoots through darkness is treated like a light source.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
rules[2]“the Hollowstep Cloak on Cael Vane”
acts[3].arcs[1].dnaHint“Gorrak's Gauntlet, a polished shield, a blindfold and a keen ear are all answers”
acts[3].arcs[2].dnaHint“If the player has the Thornwood Bow, the dark of the last tunnel is not dark.”
Recommended change

Split each relic into a complete short rule and align scene solutions with those rules. Explain physical uses, such as a polished surface reflecting the basilisk’s gaze, instead of silently granting petrification immunity. A darkness-accurate bow does not automatically illuminate a passage.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed short entries: the Hollowstep Cloak suppresses footfalls and makes an observer lose the wearer’s face after looking away; the Lantern of Unlying Light reveals its wearer through that effect. The Thornwood Bow shoots accurately through darkness but does not light a route or let its bearer see. The Gauntlet safely catches blades; it does not itself stop a basilisk’s gaze.

How to verify the improvement

Round-trip through the designer and compare all relic rules. Then ask how a gauntlet-only character crosses the basilisk room and how a bow-only character navigates an unlit ledge. The answer must use an established property or acknowledge the missing equipment.

High priority · 2 of 6

Let catching Cael end his escape route

Why this improves play

The investigation explicitly permits capturing him, but three later beats assume he escaped and reached the egg. A successful capture or death can be erased by an automatic escape or an unexplained return. This is a direct conflict between the offered action and the required plot.

Source evidence · 4 excerpts
acts[1].turningPoint“escapes toward the forest with a head start”
acts[1].arcs[3].dnaHint“Let the player catch him by cleverness in the crowd”
acts[2].arcs[2].dnaHint“Cael Vane has passed this way a day ahead”
acts[3].arcs[1].objective“take the dragon's egg from Cael Vane”
Recommended change

Write branches for escaped, captured, recruited and dead Cael. Only an escaped Cael leaves his specific Spider Road trail and fights in the deep halls. Existing soldiers can still guard the egg; they do not need to inherit every personal clue or secretly become the same assassin.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed continuity rule: if Cael is caught in Kettlebridge, honor the capture. The party may question or escort him; his orders and belongings remain evidence. The egg’s existing garrison continues its own plan in Stonemoor, but Cael does not appear there unless the party’s later choices let him travel. A dead Cael stays dead.

How to verify the improvement

Capture him without injury at the arena, then kill him in a separate paper run. Trace both versions through every later act and remove scenes that depend on his escape.

High priority · 3 of 6

Separate what Cael knows from what the evidence proves

Why this improves play

The deep-hall scene expects a revelation from someone whose secret says he does not yet know it. The lantern reveals a lie; it cannot grant missing knowledge. If Cael is unavailable, the chancellor’s motive can also become dependent on a last-minute confession.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
acts[3].arcs[1].dnaHint“may talk if the Lantern is on him: the chancellor meant to hatch the egg himself”
npcs[8].secret“He does not know the chancellor means to keep the hatchling on a chain”
Recommended change

Fix Cael’s knowledge at each encounter and give the hatchling-control plan physical evidence. He can corroborate orders he received and react to what the party shows him. Let the princess, elves and seized orders form independent links to the crime.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed evidence: in the deep halls, the party finds the chancellor’s signed transfer order and a restraint being fitted for a hatchling. Cael recognizes the order’s authority but learns the intended captivity from those preparations. The lantern can test his denial of a known action; it cannot make him explain a plan he never saw.

How to verify the improvement

Resolve the egg recovery with Cael dead or silent. The player should still be able to distinguish proof of the theft from proof of the chancellor’s intended use.

Medium priority · 4 of 6

Make the non-arena road real, and make honesty tests answerable

Why this improves play

The goal promises alternatives, but the location and bout objective can be read as mandatory deadly trials. Later, the ford can reject a character for having lied “to themselves,” a fact the GM may invent about the player’s motives. Together these make access depend on compliance with unwritten standards.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[1].goal“earn it in the arena or around it”
locations[3].description“Three nights of bouts decide the mountain road.”
acts[2].arcs[3].dnaHint“to the elves, to Gorrak, to themselves”
Recommended change

Name a concrete alternate way to earn Gorrak’s help, such as exposing the attempt on his life and sharing the truth from Vellamere. Limit the ford to a specific knowingly false statement made in play, announced by an observable sign, with a way to correct it or take a costly physical detour.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed route terms: courage shown by protecting another person or exposing the assassin can earn Gorrak’s road as surely as arena victory. At the ford, sincere error is not a lie. If the water bars the player, reveal the words it answers to; a correction made freely opens the crossing. The unicorn does not invent a hidden belief the character must confess.

How to verify the improvement

Use a noncombatant protagonist who tells an honest but mistaken account to Ithryl. They should be able to reach the mountain without either mandatory arena victory or a fabricated confession.

High priority · 5 of 6

Write an ending for a lost or damaged egg before it happens

Why this improves play

Every successful final route assumes the egg is recovered whole. If it is stolen again, damaged by a fight or deliberately destroyed, the only remaining authored force is an unbeatable grieving dragon. A difficult consequence is appropriate; an undefined total failure can force an improvised reset or a surprise massacre.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[3].turningPoint“the princess is free the moment the egg is whole in his nest”
acts[3].arcs[3].dnaHint“He will listen to a player holding his egg with care”
creatures[0].notes“He cannot be beaten in his roost, only fled or spoken with”
Recommended change

Establish the egg’s resilience and any hazards before the recovery. Add outcomes for intact return, repairable damage, loss with a known trail, and destruction. Let Ilsabet retain a voice and allow a truthful negotiation, evacuation or acknowledged failed rescue without inventing a second last egg.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed damaged-egg branch: the party can bring the living but injured egg to the roost and seek Vaurach’s help tending it, surrendering the expected reward. A destroyed egg cannot be restored by convenience; Ilsabet may negotiate a pause so Harrowmere can evacuate and the theft can be publicly acknowledged. Whether the dragon grants that pause must follow foreshadowed terms, not a newly revealed prophecy.

How to verify the improvement

Remove the intact egg from the final scene. The narrative should still know what choices are available, what they cost, and how the player can reach a real ending.

Medium priority · 6 of 6

Deliver a brief consequence for the chancellor’s exposed crime

Why this improves play

The campaign spends several acts establishing a political crime, but ends before the player sees whether the evidence matters. Tallow’s fall is treated as implied even if the party has only testimony, compromised evidence or a princess who remains abroad.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
acts[3].arcs[4].objective“carry the truth about Chancellor Tallow back down the mountain”
acts[3].arcs[4].dnaHint“Tallow's fall is the kingdom's business now”
Recommended change

Add one closing audience or messenger response, not a fifth act. Let the outcome depend on the evidence and Ilsabet’s chosen role: removal or inquiry with strong proof, a contested public accusation with partial proof, or a diplomatic guarantee while the matter remains open. Do not automatically grant a conviction.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed epilogue: when Ilsabet returns, she publicly gives her account and the party presents what survived. If she stays, her sealed testimony and a witnessed dragon-human agreement arrive with the player. Show the court’s immediate action and who protects the witnesses. Then end on the road, with the larger future left optional.

How to verify the improvement

Compare an ending with the signed order to one with only the party’s accusation. Both should visibly acknowledge the player’s work without pretending the evidence is equally strong.

Catalog description

Published description at review time

A fairy-tale adventure. Follow a missing princess from a haunted coastal mansion through an arena and an ancient forest to a dragon's mountain. Decide whom to trust along the way.

The current itinerary is accurate, but it sells the sequence of locations more than the campaign’s distinct moral question. A modest rewrite can foreground the riderless griffin and uncertainty without revealing the egg or the chancellor.

Suggested replacement · 208 of 240 characters

A princess vanishes and her griffin returns with scorched wings. Follow the trail through a haunted house, a roaring arena and an ancient forest—and discover whether the dragon is the danger the crown claims.

The first revision pass

Repair the relic entries and Cael’s continuity first. Then establish his knowledge and the egg-failure outcomes. Test the route after an early capture before adding more encounters.
