The assessment

This version already anticipates the common ways a rescue campaign goes wrong: forced capture, a missing clue, a dead witness, compulsory romance, duplicated rewards and a doorway home held hostage by the plot. Those problems should not be logged again as if the text had ignored them. The remaining improvements concern visible escalation, the land after a permanently lost heir, and the terms of Grist’s negotiated defeat.

What to preserve

Keep the explicit alternativesThe empty prison, matching letters, mine outflow, tracks and independent guides make discovery resilient. Killing every ambusher does not erase the route to the mine.

Keep both heirs activeAuden studies guards; Willow maps water routes. Their cooperation makes the romance an earned relationship rather than a reward handed to the rescuer.

Keep consent separate from magicThe true-name, Ring of Truth, bargain and wedding rules distinguish informed agreement from coercion. Sincere mistakes remain possible under the ring.

Keep the homeward promise independentThe Willow Mother’s alternative rite works without royal approval or a wedding. Failure can be acknowledged without trapping the player in an unwanted campaign.

Keep Grist’s responsibility fixedThe courier can be caught early, the forgery can be exposed early, and the blueprint explicitly forbids inventing another mastermind to protect the outline.

Improvements worth making

High: resolve before relying on the affected scene in a player-facing playtest. Medium: a concrete improvement to clarity, agency or payoff. These are editorial priorities, not measured gameplay failure rates.

  1. Give the visible escalation a small, actionable schedule
  2. State what happens to a realm if its heir dies permanently
  3. Give negotiated surrender a concrete peace dividend
Medium priority · 1 of 3

Give the visible escalation a small, actionable schedule

Why this improves play

The rule correctly rejects a hidden turn countdown, but the next attack’s target, departure trigger and consequences are not fixed. Two runs could reward the same delay very differently. The player needs to know what they are racing to prevent, not just that the forest looks worse.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
rules[8]“Announce impending advances with time to act; honor delays the player earns.”
acts[2].arcs[2].dnaHint“Stop the courier's next forged attack”
Recommended change

Add three observable stages linked to established events and in-world warnings. Name a target for the next false-flag attack, the evidence that announces it, and what changes if it succeeds. A ceasefire or intercepted dispatch should genuinely postpone the relevant stage.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed pressure note: first, border patrols prepare a retaliatory march; matching letters can win a pause. Second, Grist’s courier carries an attack order against a named crossing, with departure announced by a witness or found dispatch. Third, an unopposed attack starts a limited border clash. Warn before each transition and keep travel or questioning from automatically advancing it.

How to verify the improvement

Run one route that spends time helping refugees and another that secures a royal pause. Both should receive usable warnings; the earned pause must change what is possible.

Medium priority · 2 of 3

State what happens to a realm if its heir dies permanently

Why this improves play

The loss rules are honest and passage home remains available, but ecological recovery is tied specifically to safe return. If an heir dies, the text does not establish whether that land is permanently doomed, slowly recoverable, or awaiting another source of stewardship. The player can finish the campaign without understanding the largest consequence of the failed rescue.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
rules[7]“each heir's safe return begins recovery in their own land”
rules[17]“A lost heir is not secretly replaced or resurrected”
acts[3].arcs[2].dnaHint“If rescue failed, this exchange still offers a way out, with loss acknowledged.”
Recommended change

Choose an explicit ecological aftermath for each combination of surviving heirs. My recommendation is that death prevents immediate restoration, while patient care by the surviving community can eventually renew the bond; this preserves grief without retroactively making rescue failure an unannounced extinction sentence. A harsher outcome is possible, but must be foreshadowed before the rescue.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed rule: a safely returned heir begins prompt recovery in their own realm. If an heir dies, their land remains wounded through the ending; surviving kin and willing inhabitants can renew its stewardship over seasons. This does not restore the dead, complete the failed rescue or create another mandatory quest before the mortal may leave.

How to verify the improvement

Resolve a rescue where only Willow survives, then one where only Auden survives. State the political, ecological and passage outcomes separately, without replacing either heir.

Medium priority · 3 of 3

Give negotiated surrender a concrete peace dividend

Why this improves play

The campaign deliberately supports negotiation, worker revolt and exposing Grist instead of killing him. The final settlement, however, does not say what prevents a surrendered Grist from retaining the machinery of the same conspiracy, or how the people helped in the mine obtain a benefit beyond a general promise of representation.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
npcs[7].notes“bargained into an explicit surrender”
acts[2].arcs[2].dnaHint“Grist offers passage and treasure for silence”
acts[3].arcs[1].dnaHint“representation for the mine settlements”
Recommended change

Offer a few concrete surrender terms that can be negotiated and later checked: return the heirs, surrender seal dies and orders, stop the raiders, protect civilians, and accept witnesses from both courts. Tie Tallow’s requested settlement to a visible first action. Do not make peaceful resolution secretly inferior by default.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed settlement: Grist gives up the counterfeit workshop and disarms the raiders under a mixed delegation’s witness. Mine households keep their homes and reopen trade through a supervised crossing; Tallow or another willing representative speaks at the first boundary council. If Grist rejects these terms, the party can still expose or displace him. Record the terms actually won rather than granting all of them automatically.

How to verify the improvement

Compare killing Grist, securing surrender and helping his workers displace him. Each ending should have a different, concrete consequence while preserving the inhabitants’ agency.

Catalog description

Published description at review time

A fairy-tale mystery. Stranded between rival fae courts, seek a missing prince, untangle dangerous bargains, and find your way home before the borderlands fall silent.

Keep the current pitch. It sets up the two courts, the missing prince, the bargains and the threatened land without revealing Grist or the heirs’ romance. Its single-prince opening matches what the mortal initially knows.

The first revision pass

Do not rebuild this blueprint. Add the pressure note and the two aftermath clarifications, then playtest an early courier capture and a rescue that loses one heir. Those tests are more valuable than another full rewrite.
