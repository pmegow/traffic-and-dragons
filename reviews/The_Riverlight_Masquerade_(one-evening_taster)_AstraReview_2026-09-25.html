The assessment

A single house, a public deadline, and suspects who need something from one another make this an excellent introduction to the game. The problem is not a shortage of events. The blueprint has two incompatible versions of the crime and leaves the murder and several clue links for the GM to invent. A mystery is fairer when the truth is fixed before the player begins investigating.

What to preserve

Keep the contained settingThe terrace, ballroom, library, counting-house, conservatory and water-gate give players distinct places to investigate without an expedition-sized map.

Keep Petrell and Threnna complicatedPetrell owes the culprit money; Threnna commissioned an innocent copy. These are useful reasons for innocent people to look suspicious.

Keep the evidence ruleThe rule against proving guilt by accusation is exactly right. Support it with a complete clue chain rather than replacing it with automatic confession.

Improvements worth making

High: resolve before relying on the affected scene in a player-facing playtest. Medium: a concrete improvement to clarity, agency or payoff. These are editorial priorities, not measured gameplay failure rates.

  1. Choose one crime, and make midnight resolve that crime
  2. Fix the victim, fifth guest and messenger roles before play
  3. Make proof survive losing the contact
  4. Give the taster an opening job and an observable clock
  5. Allow an early solution and a culprit who refuses to confess
  6. Write four short endings, including being wrong
High priority · 1 of 6

Choose one crime, and make midnight resolve that crime

Why this improves play

Following Act Two changes the plot to a frame-up, but following Act Three changes it back to a document swap. Players can correctly solve one scheme and still be told they have not stopped the other.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
premise“meant to be swapped for the real one at the midnight toast”
acts[1].turningPoint“the forgery isn't meant to replace the treaty at all — it's meant to be found”
acts[2].goal“the false treaty is publicly sealed as truth”
Recommended change

Keep the frame-up: it gives Devrin a public performance to interrupt. Rewrite the premise and final act so the toast is when he intends to accuse Petrell. State what political power the accusation gives him, who physically carries the planted forgery, and where the authentic treaty remains.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed GM truth: Devrin has planted the forgery in Petrell’s coat. At the midnight toast he will produce it and demand Petrell’s exclusion from the tariff alliance. The authentic treaty remains with Ossvane; it is never swapped. Stopping the accusation protects Petrell, while exposing its author determines whether Devrin keeps his standing.

How to verify the improvement

Ask a reader to describe the crime, document custody and deadline after reading each act separately. All three answers should agree.

High priority · 2 of 6

Fix the victim, fifth guest and messenger roles before play

Why this improves play

The roster names four apparent suspect-guests, plus the host and steward. It does not settle whether the fifth guest is the murder victim, who killed that person, or whether the go-between is the Masked Forger. These gaps affect who can be interviewed and whether the opening murder ever receives an answer.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[0].turningPoint“A guest is found dead”
acts[0].arcs[0].objective“the five masked guests”
acts[1].arcs[3].objective“the nervous go-between”
Recommended change

Write one short cast-and-custody note assigning all five guest slots, the corpse, and the contact. A compact option is to make the fifth guest a murdered witness and the Masked Forger the go-between; choose a fixed killer and motive. Give each living guest a distinct mask so names are not the only memory aid.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed cast addition: the fifth masked guest is an independent treaty witness who recognized the forged seal. Devrin killed them before the receiving line ended. The Masked Forger carried his messages and now fears becoming his next loose end. Her testimony corroborates the murder evidence but is not its only source.

How to verify the improvement

Run introductions, the body discovery and the final gathering on paper. No person should need to be alive in one scene and dead in another, and the murder should have an evidenced answer.

High priority · 3 of 6

Make proof survive losing the contact

Why this improves play

Several locations offer activity, but the commission is explicitly revealed only through one contact. If that person escapes or dies, the player may know a forgery exists without a supported way to attribute it. Possessing an authentic seal also does not, by itself, establish who commissioned a false document.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
npcs[2].notes“surfacing only when the go-between is cornered”
acts[1].arcs[1].objective“financial records proving which guest recently paid a forger's price”
npcs[4].notes“the real treaty's original wax seal, which can prove the forgery instantly”
Recommended change

Define three independent routes to Devrin: a payment trail that Brix can explain, an instruction fragment with a verifiable provenance, and voluntary forger testimony. Separate authenticity evidence from evidence of authorship. Do not require visiting all three routes.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed clue chain: Brix’s dated payment record links Devrin to the scribe; a discarded draft contains his instruction to plant the copy on Petrell; the forger can identify that instruction and the seal alteration. Two independent links justify a public accusation. One link can justify delaying the toast while witnesses check it.

How to verify the improvement

Remove the contact entirely. A careful player who searches two other locations must still have a fair route to expose Devrin without a replacement NPC arriving to explain everything.

Medium priority · 4 of 6

Give the taster an opening job and an observable clock

Why this improves play

The character has no explicit invitation, commission or reason to risk accusing powerful strangers. Midnight is central, but startingTime is absent and the eight arcs have no short-session pacing guidance. A new player can spend most of the taster learning names before receiving a useful objective.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
acts[0].arcs[0].objective“Move through the receiving line and first dances”
rules[0]“all major revelations and consequences must resolve before or at the midnight toast”
Recommended change

Open with an invitation and one simple request from Threnna. Set an evening starting time in the blueprint. Use public event cues, not a secret count of player messages: reception, final dance, guests summoned, toast. Let two productive investigation routes be enough to reach the confrontation.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed opening: Threnna meets you on the Lantern Terrace, explains why her small hold needs this treaty, and asks you to check an unsettling discrepancy before the toast. The musicians announce the final dance well before midnight. Each time-costly detour is stated before the player commits.

How to verify the improvement

Give a first-time reader only the opening scene. They should be able to state their task, why they care, and what signals that time is running short. Measure real session duration later; the label alone does not establish it.

Medium priority · 5 of 6

Allow an early solution and a culprit who refuses to confess

Why this improves play

These success-shaped objectives can encourage the GM to defer an earned early accusation, or to require a confession even after the documents prove the case. They also confuse Devrin, who commissioned the crime, with the scribe who physically forged the copy.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
acts[2].arcs[0].objective“using gathered evidence to force a confession or surrender”
acts[2].turningPoint“The party exposes the real forger”
Recommended change

Name the commissioner in the finale. State that sufficiently corroborated evidence resolves the investigation even if Devrin denies it or escapes. If the party solves the case before the final dance, move to negotiation and consequences instead of withholding the result until every arc is visited.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed finale rule: When the party proves Devrin commissioned the planted forgery, resolve his exposure immediately. A confession is useful, never mandatory. The midnight gathering may ratify the settlement, hear a disputed case, or proceed under agreed safeguards.

How to verify the improvement

Present conclusive evidence immediately after the counting-house scene. The GM should advance to consequences without inventing a second culprit or a missing mandatory confession.

Medium priority · 6 of 6

Write four short endings, including being wrong

Why this improves play

The finale delegates the outcomes but the taster promises a self-contained evening. A mistaken accusation, a missed toast or a quiet bargain can otherwise end in a vague invitation to continue investigating next session.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
acts[2].arcs[1].objective“determined by what was uncovered and how it's used”
rules[0]“no plot threads requiring a future session”
Recommended change

Add a brief outcome table for public exposure, negotiated containment, a false accusation, and no intervention. Resolve the treaty, Petrell, Devrin and the murder in the closing narration to the extent the party established them; distinguish what happened from what they can prove.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed late ending: Devrin makes his accusation and the alliance fractures for now. Threnna protects the party from immediate retaliation but names the households that will suffer the renewed tariffs. A final glimpse shows what became of the forger. Close the evening; offer any repair campaign only as an optional sequel.

How to verify the improvement

Walk through an incorrect accusation and a missed deadline. Each must produce a concrete ending without secretly making the player correct.

Catalog description

Published description at review time

A single evening of intrigue: find which of five masked guests carries the forged treaty before the midnight toast. Short by design — a taste of the game.

Keep the promise of a compact evening, but update the pitch once the crime is made consistent. Do not name Devrin or reveal the frame-up in the player-facing copy.

Suggested replacement · 204 of 240 characters

Five masked guests, one forged treaty, and a midnight toast. Follow gossip and stolen glances through a riverside manor before a lie tears its fragile alliance apart. A self-contained evening of intrigue.

The first revision pass

First settle the crime and cast. Then write the clue links and outcome table. Test the opening and one shortened investigation route before adding any more scenes.
