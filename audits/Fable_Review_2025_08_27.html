① The headline question: is the arc wall the right path against quest sprawl?

Fable adjudication · #231, #229, #230 · owner ruling 2026-08-24

Verdict: right rail, incomplete wall. Keep the design — and close three holes before trusting it.

The diagnosis behind #231 is genuinely rigorous. Two intuitive explanations were measured and falsified on the t2324 save (duration — the favourite quest ran 161 turns; epistemic-vs-terminal goals — zero-box rates were flat), and the confirmed axis was proportion through anchoring: the giants thread at 7 quests was healthy precisely because it died with its arc, while the closed-eye thread hit 18 quests across ~8 arc boundaries because nothing tied it to anything. The wall enforces exactly the confirmed axis — an emergent thread is subordinated to the spine unit that bore it. Stamping at quest creation is the only deterministic join point (acceptance may never come; objectives may never be tagged). And the hard-wall-with-no-promotion ruling holds up better than it first looks: resurrection through a fresh |offered is player-gated — the reopen guard converts unbounded GM momentum into per-arc player consent, which is the agency mechanism the "player never gets a voice" worry asks for. The design panel that probed alternatives (promotion ceremony, proportional budget, player-facing choice at the wall) concluded the same: nothing on that list beats the shipped shape on the evidence available, and building promotion machinery now would be speculation ahead of data.

But as shipped, the wall would have only partially caught the campaign that motivated it. Three holes, in order:

1. Parallel acts are the wall's blind habitat (P0 item). When two or more arcs are live, currentArcTitle() returns null and nothing is stamped — deliberately, to avoid guessing. But the owner's own Runelords campaign spent ~45% of its turns (t878–t1922) inside a parallel act, and that stretch is where the sprawl lived. Everything born there is permanently immune. The fix that preserves the no-guessing principle: when the arc is ambiguous but the ACT is not, stamp the act (bornAct) and sweep at act completion.

2. ACT_COMPLETE is an open door (P0 item). Closing an act while one of its arcs is still active orphans that arc's progeny into permanent immunity — the act-level exit reintroduces exactly the sprawl the arc-level wall kills. These two fixes are one design.

3. The wall is blind after it fires (P0 item). Post-sweep, the crisis line still demands the GM register any pursued goal, the blocked |active re-creation is silent to the GM, and no note says the thread was closed — so the GM population the wall targets lands in an untracked-thread state, the exact green-indicators blindness #231 exists to end. A wall-variant 2-turn note (not the #229 "abandoned by the player" wording, which would be false) closes the loop.

What NOT to build yet: thread fingerprinting. The measured pathology was a thread of 18 retitled rows, and the wall polices rows — a well-behaved hydra that closes each row inside its arc never trips it (P1 item). That is a real granularity gap, but detecting "the same thread under new titles" is heavy, fuzzy machinery, and the honest move is the one the TODO row already names: gate it on the first armed campaign's field data. That measurement is currently impossible — wall-abandons and player-abandons write identical archive records — so the tiny by field (P2/P2) is not cosmetics, it is the instrument the next design decision depends on. Land it now.

The rest of the family: the same-response newborn-quest kill (P1), the Suggest-completion blast radius that can now fire the sweep from a utility click (P1, folds into the review-call whitelist P1), the resurrected-quest objective wipe (P1), and the small records in P2/P3. Entry 23's four probe points are all answered — see the queue disposition table (section ⑦).
