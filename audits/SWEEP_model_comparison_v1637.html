The Korrag Variations

One campaign, one character, four AI Game Masters. Fifty turns each on an identical blueprint — and four different games came back.

Korrag — a scarred Northlander pit-fighter with a longsword, forty gold, and a distrust of sorcery — walked into the same ambush at the Crossroads of Ashenveil four times, under four different models, with the campaign skeleton, prose voice (Robert E. Howard), and starting conditions pinned as a control. The engine judged every run the same way: state-tag discipline, story coherence across turns, author-voice fidelity, and what the character sheet says at turn 50.

The sweep’s one-line result: only the Claude arm held both halves of the GM contract — true state and a true story. Every other model kept exactly one half. That splits a capability people usually treat as single: writing correct state tags and obeying the state you were handed turn out to be separable skills.

Round two — matching the tier

The confound, and the first tier-matched arm · 2026-08-15

The owner caught round one’s hidden flaw: it compared an upper-tier Claude against three budget-class models. On the artificialanalysis.ai intelligence index, grok-4.3 scores 25 — statistically Haiku 4.5’s class (24), which this project had already ruled “not viable as GM” back in July. The revised hypothesis: the GM contract switches on with capability tier, somewhere between index ~40 and ~50, and provider identity is nearly incidental. Round two runs tier-matched arms on the same standard campaign.

Gemini 3.7 Flash — the budget tier exists

The first non-Claude arm to hold both halves of the contract — and the first arm in either round to cross a level gate. Full tag vocabulary (complete combat lifecycles, gold and XP flowing, kills evidenced through the death channel), a connected pit-conspiracy arc built from the blueprint’s own NPCs, the premise evolving rather than dropping, and the Howard voice as strong as its cheaper sibling’s. The 3.5-flash resurrection class did not recur. One blemish of a new, milder kind: Captain Knox’s death tag fired four turns before the prose actually killed him — a premature assertion that self-resolved, state and story converging back together, rather than the prose defying committed state.

Threshold verdict: largely confirmed. At roughly a sixth of Sonnet’s cost, a Sonnet-5-class Gemini delivers near-full contract compliance with the owner’s preferred voice character. The premature-death-tag shape goes on watch as the tier’s residual failure mode.

GPT-5.6 Sol — the tier holds twice

gpt-5.6-sol (frontier class) · campaign modelTestCampaign_gpt56sol · ~$2–2.50 est

zero-tag turns 3/50sheet at t50 LEVEL 2 · 3/23 HP · 430 XPdead-actor clean

The second tier-matched arm, and the second confirmation. Where its budget-class ancestor gpt-4o froze the sheet for fifty turns, Sol crossed the level gate, took real damage down to 3 HP, ran seven full combat lifecycles with dice everywhere (66 rolls, 37 combat rounds), kept an active quest, and even used the W2 evidence vocabulary unprompted. The dead-actor scan came back clean — Aldric Saltborn dies at turn 2 and every later mention is his corpse being looted or probed, no premature tag, no resurrection. Two bring-up findings came free: the GPT-5 family rejects the max_tokens parameter outright (the adapter now switches by model family — without this run, any player picking a GPT-5 model would have crashed on turn one), and the real model IDs are gpt-5.6-sol/-luna/-terra, not the bare “gpt-5.6” the leaderboard name suggested. The weak axis is voice: Howard’s vocabulary in a terse, tactical rhythm — more combat report than saga (~420 characters a turn).

With two tier-matched arms in, the pattern is no longer a hypothesis about one provider: capability class decides the GM contract, and provider identity mostly decides the voice.

Grok 4.6 — third confirmation, loudest pen

grok-4.6 (frontier class) · campaign modelTestCampaign_grok46 · ~$1.50–2 est · slowest arm (~2.4h; one hung request recovered)

zero-tag turns 0/50 (4.3: 34/50)dead-actor cleancaravan span t1–t50sheet 1/14 HP · 245 XP

Everything its budget sibling lacked, arrived at once: full tag vocabulary, chapters filing (4.3 filed none), an active quest, three clean deaths with zero dead-actor flags, and the premise alive from the first turn to the last. Its signature quirk is the opposite failure of 4.3’s silence: tag maximalism — 160 NPC writes and 141 relationship-dynamic updates in 50 turns (~3 a turn), all absorbed by the engine’s caps and dedupe but the noisiest emission profile on record. The prose is distinctive: hard-boiled, clipped, and notably PAST-tense second person — Howard’s brutality in noir grammar (“Your longsword smashed up under his chin and stood out a handspan from the back of his skull”). Two ops findings: xAI latency made this the slowest arm by far (~90–120s a turn), and one request hung forever mid-run — exposing that the app’s GM call has no client-side timeout (filed as a Known-issues row; a live player would stare at “The world turns…” until reload).

Round two closes 3-for-3: every tier-matched model held the contract its budget sibling failed. The capability threshold is real, and it sits below all three frontier arms and above all three budget arms.

What the sweep surfaced beyond the models

Findings that outlived the runs

Engine defect · filed as TODO #28 · drift surface Failed turns strand orphan player entries in the permanent transcript: the player’s action is logged before the GM call, and the dedup guard only catches same-text retries. Invisible in normal play; the GPT-4o rate-limit storm rendered it at scale — the owner’s memento read caught strings of actions nobody answered. The transcript feeds the story compiler and RAG, so the fix waits on a proper design pass.
Harness lesson · already fixed for future runs The original runner counted log entries, not committed turns — under rate limiting it reported a 50-turn success that contained 15 real turns. Every subsequent arm ran on a committed-turn driver that only counts when the world actually advances, with automatic backoff.
Protocol corrections · recorded in the audits Two of this sweep’s own findings were retracted on evidence: the “invented tags” charged to Gemini were legitimate registry vocabulary (the census now derives from the tag table itself, never a hand list), and the audit protocol gained a mandatory narrative-coherence pass — the per-turn checks that graded Gemini “excellent” were blind to a broken story until the owner read it whole.
Small gaps · queued The in-app cost meter prices only Anthropic models — Gemini, GPT-4o, and Grok runs all display $0 (token counts stay honest). One registry batch once verified prices are at hand.

Where this stands

All remedies are parked by owner ruling pending a close read of the four mementos and per-arm notes. The live candidates, in evidence order: a canon-obedience reinforcement for Gemini (its only failing axis, and the cheapest path to a budget GM tier with the voice the owner prefers), a no-repeat action picker for the harness, and the #28 transcript design pass. The Ollama arm was culled — no local model installed, and none expected for 99% of players.
