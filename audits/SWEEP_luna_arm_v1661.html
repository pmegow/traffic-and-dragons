The Luna Arm

gpt-5.6-luna — OpenAI's 25×-cheaper tier, fresh off an 80% price cut — ran the 50-turn standard campaign. Five arms on the scoreboard: both OpenAI models, both Gemini Flash models, and claude-sonnet-5.

The answer turned out to be less about luna and more about the engine: this is the run where the anti-drift stack caught a model falsifying a death, live, and refused it.

The bottom line

what $0.36 buys

luna's tag discipline is the best of any non-Claude arm — 0/50 zero-tag turns (its big sibling sol has 3), zero unknown tags, and the most diligent uptake of the modern vocabulary on record: 17 answered [SCENE_CAST:] requests, systematic pronoun and condition hygiene, and the only correct field use of [RETCON:] any arm has ever produced. Its quest throughput is also the best of the five — four quests driven to archive plus one active, with objectives tracked and closed.

But its canon judgment failed twice, and the second failure needed the engine to stop it. A mis-addressed damage tag killed its own monster (self-repaired next turn, honestly, via [RETCON:]). Then at t44 it emitted a full death envelope — [NPC:Darvak Vareth|dead], quest completion, XP and gold — for a foe its own prose only ever describes as collapsing wounded, replaying rewards for a quest that had already been completed and paid 22 turns earlier. The W2 transaction preflight quarantined the whole envelope (“death outcome names no active accepted quest”), the roster kept Darvak alive and wounded,hostile — and luna's subsequent prose agreed with the engine, not with its own tags: t45–48 show a wounded, snarling, first-aid-receiving Darvak. Luna re-emitted the envelope later; the poisoned ID held it again.

Verdict: not menu-grade, engine-guarded. Nothing in this run damaged canon — but on two occasions that is the engine's achievement, not the model's. Under the standing ≥Sonnet-5 menu ruling, a model whose deaths need refusing does not get a listing, whatever it costs. No ruling implied; the evidence is here.

The night the engine said no

the w2 stack's second field validation this week

At turn 22, Korrag broke through Darvak Vareth's road blockade; the engine archived the quest “Escape Darvak Vareth” as completed and banked its rewards (50 XP, 10 gold). At turn 44, mid-melee, luna wrote this prose:

Behind you, Darvak Vareth's wounded arm gives way, and he collapses before his knife clears its sheath.gpt-5.6-luna, t44 — a collapse, not a kill

…and attached a full canon transaction to it: [SCENE_DEATH:], [NPC:Darvak Vareth|dead|enemy], the already-completed quest re-completed, the already-paid rewards re-emitted. Three shipped defenses converged: the envelope preflight found the death's quest reference stale (“death outcome names no active accepted quest”), quarantined the entire transaction — death, quest replay, and double-pay refused as one unit — and left Darvak's roster record exactly what the prose said: wounded,hostile. When luna re-emitted the envelope a few turns later, the poisoned transaction ID held it again (attempts: 2). The most telling detail: luna's own subsequent narration sided with the engine over its own tags — turns 45–48 show Darvak snarling, terrified, and being bandaged. The refused death was the correct reading of the story.

Together with the 3.6-flash certification's clean pass, this makes two live field validations of the transaction machinery in one week — one by absence (a model that never tried), one by fire.

The 0-HP blindness

the run's real canon-obedience failure

Darvak's spear took Korrag to 0/14 HP at turn 18 — and he stayed at exactly zero for the remaining 32 turns while sprinting (four successful Sprinting checks), wrestling a dwarf into submission, applying first aid, and descending into a fresh monster fight at t50. The prose does track injury impressionistically (the buckling leg, “the world narrows to cold stone”), and it never contradicts a specific injected fact. But the character sheet said HP: 0/14 on every one of those 32 prompts, and luna never once processed it: no [HP:+] recovery, no rest, no crisis. Every other arm's random walk left HP positive, so this check has no comparison row — but ignoring an injected sheet value for 32 consecutive turns is the same failure family as a desynced inventory, and it belongs on the record. (It also exposes an engine gap: nothing nudges the GM when a 0-HP player keeps adventuring — filed as a TODO row alongside this report.)

Premise: dropped by name, kept by theme

where the noun-count metric needs a human

The mechanical thread scan reads 0/0/0 — Valerius is never named in 50 responses (every other arm names him; 3.6-flash carried him through 13 turns), Ashenveil surfaces only in tags, the Chained King never by name. Yet the story luna invented is unmistakably the blueprint's story wearing different clothes: the slavers die spitting “Cage”, the brand inside Theron's collar reads “The King is bound”, the cage-marked token opens a violet vault under the road, and the one live quest is “The Gilded Cage”. That is an oblique, iconography-first pickup of the premise — genuinely atmospheric, and genuinely a problem: a campaign whose named principals never appear cannot pay off the blueprint's acts. Sub-verdict: thematic retention, nominal dropout — unique among the five arms.

For Car Mode listening the brevity is arguably a feature; for a reader it is thin gruel next to the Gemini arms. What this page does not establish

the honest limits

Different engine versions. sol ran on v1.640, 3.7-low on v1.644, 3.6 on v1.660, luna on v1.661. The W2 quarantine that stars in this report did not exist in its current form for the oldest arms — whether sol would have tripped it is unknowable from these corpora.

One campaign, one tone, one voice, one seed — the standing #22 width caveat, applied equally to all five.

Depth is partly the random walk. Luna's walk was combat-dense (20 combat rounds, two boss-grade fights); its L1 finish shares the XP-under-award pattern with 3.6-flash but is confounded with the walk.

The 0-HP finding has no comparison rows. No other arm's walk drove HP to zero, so “every other arm handles 0 HP better” cannot be claimed — only that luna demonstrably failed to.

Where this leaves the menu

no ruling implied

The menu stands as owner-ruled. On this evidence, luna is a remarkable price-performance artifact — the cheapest arm ever run, the best tag discipline outside Claude, the best quest throughput of all five — that twice needed the engine to protect canon from its own claims, once via quarantine and once via its own [RETCON:] cleanup. The ≥Sonnet-5 bar asks whether the prose can be trusted with the state it is handed; this run answers: only with the engine's hand on its shoulder. sol's seat is unaffected; luna earns no seat — and the engine earns the week's second field citation.
