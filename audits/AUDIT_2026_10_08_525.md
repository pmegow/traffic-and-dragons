# #525 — ending narration and portable record ownership

Date: 2026-10-08. Isolated checkpoint v1.1183, cache `tnd-v3-20261008j`; integration assigns its own markers. Baseline `5751f292`.

The later owner ruling controls this change: narration addresses the hero as “you”; the campaign voice decides the register. The earlier ban on game words was superseded. Level and the selected voice remain available to the ending model.

## Cause and implementation

The old prompt did not specify grammatical person. The engine then copied the prose's closing paragraph directly into a defining moment on every witness's sheet. A companion could therefore carry another hero's “I” or “you” as an unattributed memory.

Both ending prompts now request second person and a separate hidden third-person RECORD sentence. One shared parser handles the label, supported matched wrappers and multilingual sentence stops. Only prose reaches transcript, chapter, frame, audio replay and memento. Missing or punctuation-less metadata produces one warning and uses the whole attributed closing paragraph; punctuation is a conservative delimiter check, not proof of semantic completeness. A record-only response throws before any ending is filed, leaves the ending owed and shows the existing retry error.

Old ending moments are repaired through their own `who`, never the holder or current hero. The repair runs at migration and before all three prompt entry boundaries, so a mid-session library replacement is covered. It includes the archive, leaves ordinary memories unchanged, and never mutates the incoming external library copy. Exact ownership-prefix recognition prevents repeated growth. Generated attribution words cannot raise held memories or feed motif matching. First identifying words are title-aware and use Unicode-safe delimiters; surnames and known namesakes cannot steal a fate. Existing fate records without reliable original ownership are deliberately not rewritten.

## Review and failure evidence

The first five engine assertions failed on the original implementation: missing person, missing prose/record boundary, missing attribution, raw metadata in history, and absent normalization. Further failure-first probes caught dotted titles and one-token known namesakes. Independent review found decorated labels leaking and multiline records swallowing prose after non-Latin punctuation. The shared wrapper registry and shared completion predicate resolve those failure classes; no consumer-specific stripping was added.

Independent reviewer `/root/astra_nine_review` approved the final runtime after 222 parser/attribution assertions and 14 ownership, archive, witness, idempotence and fallback assertions. The reviewer also ran the actual controlled ending pipeline. This approval does not claim a live model call.
