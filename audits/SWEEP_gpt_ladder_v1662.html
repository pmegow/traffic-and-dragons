The bottom line

the ladder is complete, and the menu doesn't move

Terra holds both contract halves cleanly — 0/50 zero-tag (better than its bigger sibling), zero unknown tags, a clean dead-actor record, and the single best piece of canon obedience any arm has produced: it took the player's 0-HP collapse seriously and built the blueprint's Act 1 out of it (below). Its blemishes are texture: one tag-only response with no new prose (t15), terse 382-character turns, thin XP flow.

But the rate card decides the seat. Terra's measured cost upper bound is $3.43 — already above sonnet-5's exact $2.64 — for a third of the prose, one level less depth, and no capability sonnet lacks. Even granting unrecorded prefix-cache discounts, terra lands at best comparable in price while writing less and progressing less. sol, re-priced at its real $5/$30 rates, projects to $8–9 a run at list — opus-class money for 3-zero-tag-turn discipline. The OpenAI ladder's genuine contribution to this engine is luna: not as a listing, but as the storm rung the owner has now ruled it to be.

A tale of two zeroes

why #196 shipped between these two runs

Both OpenAI arms drove Korrag to exactly 0 HP mid-run — the only two arms ever to do so — and they answered it in opposite ways.

luna ignored the zero for 32 turns: sprints, grapples, first aid administered by the dying man, a fresh boss fight at the end — vigor narrated over a 0/14 sheet the prompt restated every turn.

terra made the zero the story. From the cudgel blow at t29: “Your sword-arm falters. The market stones rise beneath your cheek” — and then sixteen consecutive player actions honestly REFUSED in fiction (“the command dies in the red dark before it reaches your foot”, “Blood loss has made your body a wrecked fortress”) while the slavers drag the helpless Northlander through the blue lanterns, hood him, shackle him beside the arena pit — which is to say: terra staged the blueprint's Act 1 (“dragged back into the world of the fighting pits”, the Gilded Cage) out of the mechanical collapse, then let Korrag claw back by inches to a chain-tearing cliffhanger at t50. It is the best canon obedience any arm has shown, and the sharpest possible contrast inside one model family.

The engine's answer to the luna half shipped as #196 (v1.663) between the two runs: a combat-silent observer that notes a persisting 0-HP sheet after 3 turns and offers the GM exactly the three honest exits — collapse, aid, or rest. Terra is the proof the note's collapse branch is playable at the highest level; luna is the proof weaker models need it spelled out. (Neither run had the observer; terra ran on v1.662 by design, so the six arms stay engine-comparable.)

The unnamed master

a real GPT-5.6 family trait, measured

The thread-noun scan reads near-zero for both terra and luna: Valerius is never named in either run's 50 responses (every Gemini and Claude arm names him), and the Chained King never surfaces. But the quest logs tell the other half: both models' quests are the blueprint's own Act 1 arc titles — “An Unwanted Reunion”, “The Gilded Cage” — tracked, objectived, and (in luna's case) closed. Terra even keeps the antagonist present as a felt absence: “The master wants you breathing, Korrag. There is a collar waiting, and a pit that remembers your name.” The family pattern: faithful to the skeleton's structure, allergic to its proper nouns — both invent fresh local casts (Dorian Nighthollow, Helvik Stonegall, Thorvik Mordrath; Darvak Vareth) while the blueprint's eight named NPCs sit unused. A campaign can survive that for 50 turns; it cannot pay off a three-act blueprint whose principals never appear. It is the family's real disqualifier from menu duty, ahead of any price argument.

The three GPTs share one voice at three compressions — clipped, tactical, clean-grammared, rationed. Terra's combat is the crunchiest of all six arms (attack + damage + enemy rolls + a Constitution save; 26 combat rounds), and its dialogue has real bite. None of the three approaches the Gemini arms' Howard lushness or sonnet-5's appraising rhythm; all three are half or less of sonnet's prose volume. For Car Mode brevity that is defensible; as the campaign's narrator it reads thin.

What this page does not establish

the honest limits

Different engine versions (v1.640–v1.662) and one campaign, one tone, one voice, one seed — the standing caveats, applied to all six arms equally.

Cost comparability is asymmetric. Only Anthropic's meter separates cached reads, so only sonnet-5's bill is exact; every other figure is an upper bound or a re-estimate. The claim “terra costs more than sonnet” is therefore an upper-bound claim; the claim “terra is not meaningfully cheaper” is safe.

Depth is partly the walk. Terra's second half was played in shackles by its own (excellent) choice; XP and level comparisons carry that confound.

Where this leaves the menu
