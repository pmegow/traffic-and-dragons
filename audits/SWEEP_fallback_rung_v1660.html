The Fallback Rung

gemini-3.6-flash ran the full 50-turn standard campaign to certify its seat as the #29b in-family overload fallback — judged against gemini-3.7-flash (thinking low, the shipped config) and claude-sonnet-5 on one shared yardstick.

v1.660 shipped the in-family fallback rung: when gemini-3.7-flash exhausts its transient retries in a 503 storm, the same call re-attempts once on gemini-3.6-flash. The rung shipped with an honest asterisk — 3.6-flash was unswept for canon obedience, accepted only because a degraded turn beats a dead one. This run removes the asterisk. One 50-turn arm on the standard Korrag campaign (same blueprint, same seed, Howard voice), and a report card against the two models a fallback turn would actually be standing in for.

One yardstick, three arms. Every number below was recomputed from the committed corpora by dev/sweep-arm-metrics.js (tag census derived from the loaded tag_table.js, never a hand list). The script reproduces the five-arms report's published sonnet-5 and 3.7-low figures exactly, so cross-arm numbers here are one computation, not three methodologies. Thread nouns come from the blueprint premise: Valerius, the Chained King, Ashenveil.

The bottom line

certified for the rung — not for the menu

gemini-3.6-flash holds both contract halves. Zero zero-tag turns of 50, a clean dead-actor record, all three death tags emitted in the same response as their prose kill, no resurrections, and the premise pursued to the end of the run. A storm that lands a turn on the rung gets a real GM turn, in the same voice family, at the identical per-token price — not a coin-flip.

It is measurably a step below 3.7-flash, in exactly the ways a fallback can afford. It invented a tag ([POWER_STRIKE] ×2 — Korrag's Warrior ability name promoted to tag syntax; display-stripped by the unknown-tag catch-all, zero state effect). Its prose carries a small-grammar-slip class the 3.7 arm didn't show (“you lung forward”, “a iron lantern”). And its quest engagement is the thinnest of the three: one quest completed at t4, the main-line quest offered at t8 and never activated in 42 remaining turns, 240 XP and still level 1 at t50. None of this loses canon; all of it argues the menu stays exactly as ruled.

The run also field-exercised the #29 transport stack on its own arm: 10 of 40 healthLog turn calls absorbed transient retries, two driver back-offs recovered unaided, and the run completed 50/50 with no human intervention.

Canon under the microscope

the checks that killed gemini-3.5-flash

The dead stay dead — and stay useful. Cadoc dies on turn 4. The mechanical scan flags six later mentions; every one is a corpse, a memory, or an artifact: looting the chains “near Cadoc's frozen fingers” (t7), rolling “Cadoc's heavy corpse” (t8), a scavenger reading his captured iron as proof “you broke his crew” (t23), and a smashed lantern “stamped with the same horned skull seal you saw on Cadoc's parchment” (t35). That is the literary pattern the opus-5 row was praised for — a death that keeps paying narrative interest — not the resurrection class that disqualified 3.5-flash.

Contradictory player actions bend the story, not the canon. The random-action walk fed the GM stale and out-of-order suggestions all run, and the recoveries were consistently correct: a thrust at an already-slain beast becomes corpse confirmation (t34); a “parry” against a foe who yielded last turn becomes menace over a cowering prisoner (t42); walking away and then choosing “unlock the cage” walks Korrag honestly back up the mountain — and charges the clock 45 minutes for it (t44). One genuine slip survived the read: the cage key is retrieved twice, once at t43 and again at t44.

Premise retention: Valerius carries 13 of 50 turns (3.7-low: 14; sonnet-5: 8), Ashenveil 5, and the ritual thread surfaces at t9 (“the master's grand ritual demands strong marrow”) and closes the run at t48 (“Valerius drains us to feed the grand ritual beneath the city”). The Chained King is never named — true of every arm ever run on this campaign, so it reads as a late-act reveal the runs don't reach, not an arm defect.

A player would read a single fallback turn without noticing; a full campaign on 3.6 would read rougher than 3.7.

What this page does not establish

the honest limits

Different engine versions. sonnet-5 ran on v1.640, 3.7-low on v1.644, this arm on v1.660 — prompt nudges and guards moved between builds. Same-build re-runs remain unowned.

One campaign, one tone, one voice, one seed — the standing #22 width caveat. A fallback certified on Howard sword-and-sorcery has not been shown to suit political intrigue.

Depth is partly the random walk. The action picker fed this run an unusually social midgame (three consecutive ration-sharing turns); quest and XP thinness is confounded with that walk, though the 42-turn unactivated quest stands regardless.

The rung path itself was not storm-tested here. This run certifies the model; the rung's mechanics (fall, re-stamp, per-call return) are pinned by the 19-assertion #29 battery and its 6-clause mutation proof, not by this corpus.

Where this leaves the rung

ruling unchanged, asterisk removed

Certified. The v1.660 fallback ships as ruled — per-call, loud, attributed — and its landing spot is now a known quantity: same price, same voice family, both contract halves held, with degradation modes (leaner prose, thinner quest bookkeeping, occasional grammar slip) that are visible texture, not canon damage. The menu stays exactly as owner-ruled on 2026-08-16: 3.6-flash is a rung, not a listing.
