The assessment

The box is valuable to three parties for three different reasons, and the company’s need to cross the Flats supplies immediate pressure. Hask’s ignorance is especially useful leverage. The campaign needs firmer custody, bargaining and travel rules so its final choice grows from decisions the player made rather than from a collision the outline requires.

What to preserve

Keep Hask mistaken rather than omniscientHis belief that he is seeking royal bones can be challenged with evidence. That creates a real reason to negotiate with a dangerous person.

Keep a genuine upside in every bargainThe rule that the God must honor a real benefit prevents the familiar “all deals are fake” trap. Pair that upside with discoverable limits.

Keep Corr and Ren vulnerable for different reasonsCorr’s illness and Ren’s fading memories can strain loyalty without making both secretly evil. Their needs should create choices, not compulsory betrayal.

Improvements worth making

High: resolve before relying on the affected scene in a player-facing playtest. Medium: a concrete improvement to clarity, agency or payoff. These are editorial priorities, not measured gameplay failure rates.

  1. Give the reliquary a clear custody chain and opening condition
  2. Make a successful intervention stop the cult’s awakening rite
  3. Make sorcery’s extra cost visible before a player commits
  4. Clarify the player’s authority over the Red Ledger
  5. Give the hundred-mile crossing a few concrete resource choices
  6. Bound the God’s promises and write the consequences of each custody decision
High priority · 1 of 6

Give the reliquary a clear custody chain and opening condition

Why this improves play

The key has no assigned carrier, the theft has no defined route into the hiring scene, and resealing has no specified requirements. The player cannot make an informed early decision to inspect, secure, abandon or open the cargo when its basic handling rules are left to the moment.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
premise“bargain with anyone carrying the key”
npcs[0].notes“she stole it from a rival consul's tomb”
acts[2].turningPoint“reseal the Coffered God”
Recommended change

Write a short chain of custody: where Ysbet stole the box, why it is now at Kalgra’s Rest, who holds the key, and which vault is the actual destination. Distinguish the mundane lock from the magical seal. Seed the resealing method in Ren’s knowledge and a second existing source.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed custody note: Ysbet stole the reliquary from her rival’s frontier tomb and routed it through Kalgra’s Rest to disguise its return into her own vault. Corr carries the outer lock’s key; opening that lock exposes the seal but does not release the entity. Ren can explain how to renew the salt binding, and Sister Ash-Tongue’s stolen sealing text supplies an independent account.

How to verify the improvement

Before leaving Kalgra’s Rest, the player should know what they can physically do to the box. Before the final choice, they should know what resealing requires and where to obtain it.

High priority · 2 of 6

Make a successful intervention stop the cult’s awakening rite

Why this improves play

The investigation invites the player to find cult infiltrators, yet the turning point requires their rite to happen anyway. The final gates also assume both forces converge even if the party has neutralized one faction. These fixed events can erase the point of successful planning.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[1].turningPoint“The Cinder Chorus performs a rite that partially wakes the Coffered God”
acts[1].arcs[1].objective“Investigate the cult's presence among the company's own hired hands”
acts[2].arcs[0].objective“the converged forces of Hask Vane and the Cinder Chorus”
Recommended change

Write conditional turning points. If the rite is prevented, the God remains less able to project and the cult must use a different, weaker approach. If Hask withdraws or joins the company, remove his hostile gate encounter and show the consequences of that agreement.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed transition: an unopposed rite lets the God speak into the company’s dreams. If the party stops the rite, it can speak only through the established crack or a willing key-holder, and Ash-Tongue reaches Two Thrones without the intended following. The finale uses only forces that actually survived and remained hostile.

How to verify the improvement

Expose the cult before its rite and persuade Hask that the box is not royal bones. The final confrontation must be materially easier or different because both successes mattered.

High priority · 3 of 6

Make sorcery’s extra cost visible before a player commits

Why this improves play

This rule applies to every use of magic, not just the box or Ren’s practices. A character created with ordinary spells can unexpectedly lose memories or lifespan for using an established ability. Ren’s thematic cost is specific; the universal rule has no amount, choice or limit.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
rules[0]“always extracts a cost paid in the caster's body, memory, or years”
rules[0]“whenever magic is used”
npcs[5].notes“a cost paid in her own memories”
Recommended change

Decide explicitly whether this is a campaign-wide change to character magic or a property of the setting’s extraordinary rites. My recommendation is to preserve established spell costs and put bodily, memory or lifespan bargains on named rituals whose price is revealed before consent. If universal costly magic is essential, disclose it at the opening and define the costs before the first cast.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed rule: spells and abilities retain their established game costs. Sorcery involving the reliquary, Ren’s divinations or a new bargain may demand an additional named price. Explain that price before the character chooses; ordinary casting never silently consumes years or a personal memory.

How to verify the improvement

Try healing an injured companion in the first ambush. The result must use a cost the player could know before pressing the action, rather than an improvised permanent penalty.

Medium priority · 4 of 6

Clarify the player’s authority over the Red Ledger

Why this improves play

The player is expected to manage the company, while the commander is not identified and the starting relationship is unspecified. A solo protagonist may be treated as commander in one scene and as a disposable hire with no voice in the next.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
npcs[3].notes“The Red Ledger's second-in-command”
acts[1].arcs[3].objective“Manage the company's internal loyalties”
premise“The company must decide whether they are guards, thieves, or unwitting midwives”
Recommended change

State the starting role and the company’s decision process. A useful default is a newly appointed field leader with Corr as the experienced deputy, while retaining an alternate role for a player who wants to be a hired specialist. Make companions’ reasons to follow or refuse explicit.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed opening agreement: the player is contracted to lead this crossing, not to own its members. Corr handles routines and can challenge a plan that endangers the company. Major choices about the box are argued openly; dissent may split the company, but no absent commander can silently overrule an earned agreement.

How to verify the improvement

Ask who can order a route change, who decides whether to open the box, and what happens if Corr disagrees. Answer consistently for the chosen player role.

Medium priority · 5 of 6

Give the hundred-mile crossing a few concrete resource choices

Why this improves play

The environment promises that water and route choice matter, but the outline gives neither a travel schedule nor meaningful alternatives. Repeated thirst narration can become a punishment the GM applies whenever tension falls, while Ren becomes an unlimited or arbitrarily costly refill.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
premise“a hundred miles of salt where water is currency and shade is a weapon”
locations[2].description“hidden cisterns make them both a refuge and an ambush ground”
npcs[5].notes“magic lets her read weather and water”
Recommended change

Define a compact itinerary with known replenishment points, carrying limits appropriate to the company, and a few announced choices. Use existing inventory and travel accounting where available; do not add a new hidden counter for every scene. Give Ren’s divination a fixed kind of question and an explicit price.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed route choice: the exposed direct road is shorter but consumes the company’s reserve; the canyons offer a known cistern and ambush risk; a diversion to Ren’s cistern-house costs time and yields sealing knowledge. State the expected travel time and water consequence before the company chooses.

How to verify the improvement

Walk the crossing twice using different routes. Supplies, allies or arrival conditions should differ for traceable reasons, with no unannounced double-charge for the same travel.

High priority · 6 of 6

Bound the God’s promises and write the consequences of each custody decision

Why this improves play

Its ultimate nature can remain uncertain, but a bargain is unfair if its powers and price change to fit the player’s latest theory. “Reseal, deliver or open” is a strong menu of decisions only when their practical effects are seeded. There is no stated outcome for handing it over early or walking away.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
npcs[4].notes“the GM should let players' theories shape how it manifests”
rules[1]“a real, honored upside alongside the hidden cost”
acts[2].turningPoint“deliver it to whichever faction the company has thrown in with, or break it open themselves”
Recommended change

Keep its ontology ambiguous while fixing a small set of powers, constraints and examples of costs. Give the player ways to investigate a bargain before accepting. Add aftermaths for resealing, giving it to Hask, helping the Chorus, opening it independently and abandoning the contract. Early decisions should move the ending, not force a replacement escort job.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed boundary: the God can restore Corr’s health but claims a defined future service, not an unspecified additional life. It cannot possess an unwilling person merely because they listened. Resealing limits its reach; delivering it gives the named recipient control of a real danger; opening it changes the Flats in a consequence demonstrated by a smaller prior manifestation. Record one unsettled bargain per person and close it when paid.

How to verify the improvement

Offer the same bargain to two skeptical players, allow them to investigate separately, and keep the promise and price consistent. Then resolve an early handover without undoing it.

Catalog description

Published description at review time

Sword and sorcery. A mercenary company escorts a sealed reliquary across a salt waste, hunted by a warlord, a cult, and the thing in the box that wants out.

Keep the current pitch. It accurately presents the crossing, the competing pursuers and the cargo’s dangerous agency. The God’s desire to escape is the central premise, not a late mystery that needs concealing.

The first revision pass

First define the box’s custody and seal, and clarify magical costs. Then write conditional faction transitions and a compact ending table. Test one early bargain and one prevented rite.
