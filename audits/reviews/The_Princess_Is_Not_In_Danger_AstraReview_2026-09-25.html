The assessment

The ghosts’ theatre becoming an audience for the dragon is an excellent setup-and-payoff. Keith’s regretted remark, the princess’s letters and the postal loophole also fit the same comic world. The published version nevertheless loses the end of two important rules, and several scenes prescribe the only acceptable solution while the campaign promises ingenuity. Preserve the dry seriousness; make the playable terms equally clear.

What to preserve

Keep the theatre-to-memoir payoffA location visited early becomes the solution to someone else’s need later. That is more satisfying than introducing a final magic cure.

Keep Wilhelmina’s plain accountHer letters tell the player what she actually wants, puncturing the kingdom’s story without turning her into another secret villain.

Keep the relics modestA doubtful sword and an invisible left elbow are useful comic constraints. Their limitations are part of the reward, not defects to upgrade away.

Keep everyone earnestThe rule to play the world straight gives the player room to supply the surprise. Explaining each joke would make the campaign less funny.

Improvements worth making

High: resolve before relying on the affected scene in a player-facing playtest. Medium: a concrete improvement to clarity, agency or payoff. These are editorial priorities, not measured gameplay failure rates.

High priority · 1 of 6

Restore the two rules already clipped in the published version

Why this improves play

Both published rules are exactly 400 characters and end mid-sentence. The local sample retains their complete 415- and 876-character versions. The live text therefore lacks the end of the death policy and most of the relic limitation rule. Loading the already-clipped publication cannot recover those missing words.

Source evidence · 2 excerpts
rules[0]“and no one dies in Gorse unles”
rules[3]“the Boots of Modest Speed on loan from Hobb's ”
Recommended change

Split the tone policy into complete short rules and give relics separate entries below the existing 400-character limit. Use the full local sample as a recovery reference, then review the intended death policy rather than assuming its old wording is precise enough. Keep the final prohibition against upgrading joke relics.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed short rule: named relics retain their stated, limited effects and are found only at their established locations. The GM must not turn a comic limitation into a stronger power. Put each relic’s location and effect in its own short rule so opening and saving the blueprint cannot silently remove the later items.

How to verify the improvement

Publish a revised test copy, open it in the designer and export it. All sentences should be complete, and the Helm, Sword and Cloak limits should still be present. The current published snapshot passes structural validation despite the loss.

High priority · 2 of 6

Reconcile embarrassing danger with lethal responses to rudeness

Why this improves play

A player trying ordinary social experimentation can cross from embarrassment to an apex dragon’s lethal response on a subjective judgment of rudeness. The story repeatedly frames threats as comic, while the bestiary makes several of them genuinely deadly. The missing end of the death rule amplifies the ambiguity.

Source evidence · 4 excerpts
rules[0]“Danger is real but its consequences are embarrassing before they are fatal”
creatures[0].notes“lethal if anyone is rude first”
creatures[1].notes“impossible to provoke”
creatures[2].notes“His spines are real and venomous”
Recommended change

Write one consistent escalation rule. My recommendation is that ordinary social mistakes cause social or temporary physical consequences, while persistent, deliberate violence can become lethal after an unmistakable warning and a chance to disengage. Keep the hydra and basilisk dangerous, but establish rescue, surrender and recovery conditions.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed rule: awkwardness, criticism and a wrong answer are not consent to lethal combat. Escalate from offense to warning to a reversible consequence. A character who deliberately continues a clearly warned violent attack can face normal lethal danger. The Trials stop on surrender; petrification caused by Doris has an established recovery route.

How to verify the improvement

Try a blunt criticism of the memoirs, a failed performance and a deliberate sword attack. These should not all trigger the same punishment, and the player should understand the distinction before it becomes irreversible.

High priority · 3 of 6

Make Thursday’s itinerary possible on its own terms

Why this improves play

The party learns it has less than a day, then is told to register for trials tomorrow morning, complete them, climb the mountain and return before Thursday. Ten-minute boots do not explain that gap. Because nobody knows the day until the reveal, the deadline can feel like a joke at the player’s expense rather than a problem they can solve.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[2].arcs[4].dnaHint“Thursday is tomorrow. The mountain is a day away.”
acts[3].arcs[0].objective“register for the Rescue Trials by tomorrow morning”
acts[3].arcs[4].objective“Bring the princess home to Little Piddle-on-the-Marsh before the bell that nobody rings”
Recommended change

Choose a workable schedule or explicitly allow the prophecy’s deadline to be negotiated as a comic social rule. Give the calendar reveal a trustworthy basis. State whether Thursday means its start, end, or a ceremony, and make travel options support that interpretation.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed timeline: it is Wednesday morning; the rescue is expected before Thursday’s evening bell. Hobb’s End holds the Trials Wednesday afternoon. The maintained mountain path can be climbed Thursday morning, and Percival’s evening post returns Wilhelmina. The boots save one stated margin; they do not rewind an entire journey.

How to verify the improvement

Lay the five final arcs on a calendar, including rest and travel. A party following the offered route should have a possible success without an unannounced time reset.

Medium priority · 4 of 6

Let the player satisfy social obstacles in more than one way

Why this improves play

The marrow has no judging criteria, the theatre controls the only map, the trees demand exact remembered words, and winning the Trials conflicts with the expected forfeit. These are funny situations, but unspecified correct answers can make the player wait for the narrator to reveal the approved joke.

Source evidence · 4 excerpts
acts[0].arcs[1].dnaHint“Judge wrongly and the town turns”
acts[1].goal“will only reveal to members of the company”
acts[2].arcs[0].dnaHint“work if repeated exactly, including the compliments”
acts[3].arcs[1].dnaHint“the crowd expects the player to do the same, and the crowd is not wrong”
Recommended change

Give each obstacle a need rather than a password. State the marrow’s judging basis, allow honorary membership through backstage help, let a sincere paraphrase work on the trees, and define a respectful forfeit as a recognized Trials qualification if that is the intended solution.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed Trials ruling: the town awards passage for courage and courtesy, not solely for defeating its champion. A skillful surrender, a jointly staged exhibition, or winning without humiliation can all qualify. Bettany explains this before the bout; Wendel’s feelings are expressed in the scene rather than guessed by the GM afterward.

How to verify the improvement

Attempt each obstacle with a reasonable solution that is not the one written in the outline. It should produce a concrete reaction and a route forward, not endless “not quite.”

Medium priority · 5 of 6

Give the Colonel’s mystery a fixed non-murder answer

Why this improves play

The blueprint fixes what did not happen but leaves the actual death and the props that prove it unstated. A player solving the locked-room scene can guess indefinitely while the GM invents an answer that fits the last guess. The lantern detects discomfort around a lie, not a complete historical event.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[1].arcs[1].objective“how Colonel Augustus Frisk actually died in 1712”
acts[1].arcs[1].dnaHint“The answer is undignified (he was not murdered”
npcs[3].secret“she invented the case against her husband's death because it made a better second act”
Recommended change

Choose a small, undignified accident and two or three compatible clues before play. Let a replay, the props and a witness each support it. The player may expose Ottoline’s embellishment without being forced to humiliate her publicly.

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed cause: the Colonel fell from an unsuitable footstool while practicing an entrance and struck his head. A split stool, a torn costume hem and the house’s replay agree; none shows an attacker. Ottoline turned the accident into a murder to improve the play. The company can revise the ending without pretending the accident was noble.

How to verify the improvement

Remove the Lantern of Mild Suspicion. The player should still be able to solve the scene from the fixed evidence, and an incorrect theory should be contradicted by something already present.

Medium priority · 6 of 6

Keep the booking as a rewarding ending, not the only permission to leave

Why this improves play

The princess is physically free and knows the prophecy is nonsense, yet the finale can treat the dragon’s belief as an absolute lock until the player arranges an audience. A player who politely escorts her out has solved her actual problem; forcing the booking afterward would make her freedom conditional on soothing her host.

Source evidence · 3 excerpts
acts[3].turningPoint“he cannot release the princess until the prophecy is fulfilled or the curse is lifted”
acts[3].arcs[3].dnaHint“entirely capable of walking out, which she has not done because it would be rude”
acts[3].arcs[3].dnaHint“The real solution is a booking”
Recommended change

State that the barrier is social reluctance, not a magical prison. Let Wilhelmina choose to leave, speak frankly, invite the dragon to the theatre or propose another reader. The theatre booking should be the warmest earned payoff, not a compulsory answer. The death policy must not make polite departure “rudeness.”

Suggested wording or scene · proposal, not existing canon

Proposed finale: Grunwald fears that their departure confirms Keith’s old remark. He asks them to stay, but does not bar Wilhelmina’s way. A theatre booking gives him a new audience and makes the farewell joyful. If no booking is offered, a truthful, considerate goodbye still returns the princess and leaves a disappointed dragon rather than restarting the rescue.

How to verify the improvement

Arrive without the ghosts’ map or friendship, then ask Wilhelmina whether she wants to leave. The player should be able to complete the rescue while losing the richer theatre payoff.

Catalog description

Published description at review time

Comic fantasy. A prophecy has chosen you and your hat to rescue a princess from a curiously polite dragon. Set off through a kingdom where tradition matters more than common sense.

Keep the current published pitch, including “curiously polite.” It establishes the hat, prophecy and social absurdity without giving away the shopping list or theatre payoff. The campaign title already establishes that this is not a conventional perilous rescue.

The first revision pass

Recover and split the clipped rules first. Establish the death and Thursday policies next. Then test the Trials by gracious forfeit and the finale without a theatre booking.
