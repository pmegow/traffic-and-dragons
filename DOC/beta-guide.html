1What this is

Traffic and Dragons is a sword & sorcery role-playing game that runs entirely in your browser. You create a character, and an AI Game Master narrates a living adventure around you — tracking your health, gold, inventory, quests, and the people you meet, across sessions. There's nothing to install and no account for the game itself; the one thing you must bring is an Anthropic API key, which is what powers the Game Master's brain.

🗝Why sign in? During the beta the game runs on the operator's model account: you sign in with Google or GitHub, the operator pays for the turns, and your campaigns follow your account between devices. There is no key to buy and nothing to paste. (Operators can still run on their own provider credentials from File ▸ Settings.)

2Sign in (required)

  1. Open the game (link in step 4) and press Sign in. Google or GitHub — whichever you already have.
  2. Approve the sign-in in the popup. That is the whole account: no email to verify, no credits to buy.
  3. The operator grants each tester a monthly allowance of turns. File ▸ Account shows how many you have used.
💰What does it cost you? Nothing during the beta. The operator's account pays for every turn; the allowance keeps that honest. If the game ever says your account has no active subscription, ask the operator — your story is safe and waiting.
⚠What the operator can see. When you play signed in, every prompt the game sends to the model passes through the operator's server, so the operator can read your prompts to run the game. Bug reports say exactly what they send before you send them.

3Optional accounts — nice, not necessary

The game works fully with just the Anthropic key. These add extras — you can skip all of them today and add them later:

Optional — scene & portrait images fal.ai — powers the Render button (paints the current scene) and AI character portraits.
  1. Go to fal.ai → Sign up (Google/GitHub works).
  2. Open your dashboard → Keys → create a key (starts with a long token).
  3. Paste it into the game's opening screen in the fal.ai key box (or later, under File ▾ → Render Options).
New accounts typically include free credits — enough to try it properly.
Optional — cloud sync & cross-device play GitHub — lets the game back your campaigns up to the cloud so you can continue the same adventure on your phone, tablet, and desktop.
  1. If you don't have one: github.com/signup (free).
  2. In the game: File ▾ → Connect server → a GitHub login window opens → authorize.
  3. That's it — saves sync automatically from then on.
Without this, your game still saves — just locally to each device.
Optional — voice narration Nothing to sign up for. Tap the 🔈 speaker button in the game and it reads the story aloud using your device's built-in voice. (A premium voice service can be plugged in under File ▾ → Voice Settings, but the built-in voice needs no account.)

4Open the game

🌐 https://traffic-and-dragons.pages.dev
  1. Open the link above in Chrome (Android/desktop) or Safari (iPhone/iPad). Other modern browsers work too.
  2. Press Sign in and approve the popup.
  3. Fastest way in: open the Home page (File ▸ 🏠 Home), pick one of the three pre-made heroes and a story, and press Play. The Game Master opens your first scene. The character wizard is the custom path — it is there when you want a hero of your own.

5Put it on your home screen

The game is a proper web app — installed to your home screen it opens fullscreen with its own icon, like any native app.

📱 iPhone / iPad (Safari)

  1. Open the game in Safari (this only works from Safari).
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with the arrow pointing up, center-bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll down the share sheet and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add (top-right). The T&D icon appears on your home screen — launch it from there from now on.

🤖 Android (Chrome)

  1. Open the game in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu (top-right).
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (on some phones: Install app).
  4. Confirm. Launch from the new icon from now on.

🖥 Desktop (Chrome / Edge)

  1. Look for the install icon (a little monitor with a down-arrow) at the right end of the address bar.
  2. Click it → Install. The game gets its own window and an entry in your Start menu / dock.

6Create your character

First launch drops you into a six-step character builder. There are no wrong answers — lean into whatever sounds fun:

StepWhat you're choosing
1 · ToneThe flavor of the whole world — High Fantasy, Gritty, Sword & Sorcery, Dark Horror, Political Intrigue, or write your own. This shapes how the Game Master narrates everything.
2 · IdentityGender, age, and your ancestry — Human, Elf, Dwarf, Gnome, Half-Blood, Hollow-Born, or Tiefling, each with sub-lineages and their own stat flavor.
3 · ClassYour profession: Warrior, Rogue, Sorcerer, Ranger, Primal, Paladin, Cleric, Druid, or Necromancer.
4 · AttributesYour six ability scores. Roll (fate decides, auto-arranged sensibly) or Point Buy (you budget 27 points). Plus your moral alignment.
5 · Finishing touchesAppearance, backstory, and a portrait — upload a picture, or (with a fal.ai key) generate one from your character sheet.
6 · ReviewName your campaign, pick a starting location and level (1 is the classic experience), optionally add companions, and confirm.
💡Don't overthink the backstory. A sentence or two is plenty — the Game Master weaves whatever you give it into the story, and the interesting stuff happens in play anyway.

7Start your campaign & how to play

Hit confirm and the Game Master opens your story with your first scene. From there:

  • Type what you do in the box at the bottom — plain English, first person. "I ask the innkeeper about the missing caravan." "I draw my sword." "I search the body." Anything. The world reacts.
  • Suggested actions appear as buttons after each scene. Tap one to drop it into the input box (edit it if you like), then send. Long-press (or Ctrl-click) to fire it instantly.
  • The dice are real. When you attempt something chancy, the GM rolls and shows you the result — success and failure both push the story somewhere.
  • Sheet (top bar) shows your full character — health, gold, inventory, spells, skills, relationships. It updates itself as you play.
  • Quests arrive as ⚑ opportunities. Open the quest journal from the sidebar to accept, decline, and track objectives. Nothing is forced on you.
  • Table Talk (tab next to Story) lets you talk to the Game Master out of character — ask rules questions, request a recap, or nudge the tone, without your character saying a word.
  • Rest (top bar) heals the party, recovers spent spells, moves the clock to dawn and makes camp — the place you return to if you die.
  • Dying. At 0 HP you are down, not dead: struggle or yield, and the story resolves it. A true death brings Death, who walks you back to your last camp — ask one question on the way. Three returns per campaign; the fourth death ends the story with a written ending.
  • It remembers. People you meet, places you visit, promises you make — the game files them into long-term memory and the world stays consistent across sessions. Just close the app when you're done; you'll resume exactly where you left off.
🎭Play it like improv, not like a menu. The best sessions come from trying things no button suggests — bribe the guard, adopt the wolf, burn the letter unread. The Game Master keeps up.

8About the UI — a tour of the screen

Two views of the same moment (phone layout — desktop shows a few extra buttons in the top bar). The numbers match the sections below:

1The top bar (your HUD)

Your vital signs at a glance — name, class & level, HP, gold, alignment, and where you are. When companions travel with you, a party row appears beneath it with each member's health bar. The buttons along the top:

ButtonWhat it does
SheetYour full character sheet — stats, portrait, abilities, spells, inventory, skills, relationships, story beats. Everything the game knows about you.
SyncThe "fix it yourself" hatch: directly edit HP, gold, inventory, time, weather and more — handy if the story and the numbers ever disagree.
World StateSlides out a panel with your location, time, weather, everyone you've met, and your quest journal.
RenderPaints the current scene as an image (needs the optional fal.ai key).
↻ Re-rollRe-narrates the last scene in a different telling — same events, fresh prose, no turn spent.
🔈Toggles voice narration on and off.
File ▾Everything else — see the next section.

On phones, some of these tuck into the File ▾ menu to save space.

2The memory bar

The thin strip under the top bar — Session tokens · Chapters · NPCs · Turn — is the Game Master's memory ticker. As you play, the game periodically pauses a moment to "file memories," compressing recent events into long-term memory. That pause is normal and it's why the GM still remembers the innkeeper's debt forty turns later.

3The story area

The main scroll — the Game Master's narration, your actions, and dice results, with a turn number over each scene. Under the latest scene you'll find the suggested action buttons. When a fight starts, a combat tracker — the enemy's health bar, armor, and round counter — appears automatically at the top of this area, and vanishes when the dust settles.

4The quick-reference panel

The right edge of the screen. Collapsed, it's a slim rail of tabs (Pty · Qst · Inv · Ab · Sp); tap to expand it into full drawers — your Party with everyone's health bars, plus Quests, Inventory, Abilities, and Spells. Everything you'd flip to your character sheet for mid-scene, one tap away.

5The voice strip

Appears while narration is being read aloud — ▶ replays the scene, ⏭ skips ahead, and Speaking… tells you the narrator's mid-sentence. (Toggle narration itself with the 🔈 button in the top bar.)

6The input row

The text box where you say what you do, the 🎤 mic for dictating instead of typing, and Send. Above it, two tabs: Story (the adventure) and Table Talk (out-of-character chat with the GM — a blue dot marks unread replies on the inactive tab).

9About the File menu

File ▾ is home to everything that isn't moment-to-moment play. The headliners:

ItemWhat it does

💾 Save / Load ▶Save/Load Game (local) — file-based backups of the whole campaign. Export Character — save your hero as a portable .char file or to your cloud library. Import Character — bring a hero in: play as them in a new campaign, or add them to the current party as a companion. Export as Blueprint — distill your campaign into a reusable template others can play.
☁ Blueprint Library…Cloud-stored campaign templates — pre-built adventures you can open and start playing immediately.
⚙ Admin ▶The settings drawer — details below.
⟳ Clear cache & reloadThe universal un-sticker. If the game ever looks stale or weird, this is the button.
New GameStart a fresh character and campaign. Your current one stays safe under Campaigns.

Inside ⚙ Admin

ItemWhat it does

📖 Narrative options ▶Narrative rules — house rules the GM must always follow ("no spiders," "my dog is immortal"). ✍ Prose inspiration — have the narration channel a favorite author's style. 18+ Adult content — opt into mature language and themes; off by default.

📱On phones, the File menu also picks up a few quick actions (Sync state, World state, Render prompt) that live as top-bar buttons on desktop. The same menu is available on the character-creation screen, with game-only items greyed out until you're playing.

10If something looks broken

SymptomFix
Page looks stale or misbehaves after an updateFile ▾ → Clear cache & reload. This is the universal fix — try it first. (Or a hard refresh: Ctrl+Shift+R on desktop.)
"Invalid API key" or the GM won't respondRe-check the key was pasted completely (starts sk-ant-), and that your Anthropic account has credits remaining.
Images won't renderThe fal.ai key is missing or out of credits — check File ▾ → Render Options.
Campaign didn't follow you to another deviceCloud sync needs the GitHub connection on both devices — File ▾ → Connect server on each.
Something else / it ate your characterTell us! That's exactly what the beta is for. Note what you did, what you expected, and what happened instead — a screenshot helps enormously.
