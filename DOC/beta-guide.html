1What this is

Traffic and Dragons is a sword & sorcery role-playing game that runs entirely in your browser. You create a character, and an AI Game Master narrates a living adventure around you — tracking your health, gold, inventory, quests, and the people you meet, across sessions. There's nothing to install and nothing to buy: you sign in with Google or GitHub, and during the beta the operator's account powers the Game Master.

🗝Why sign in? During the beta the game runs on the operator's model account: you sign in with Google or GitHub, the operator pays for the turns, and your campaigns follow your account between devices. There is no key to buy and nothing to paste. (Operators can still run on their own provider credentials from File ▸ Settings.)

2Sign in (required)

  1. Open the game (link in step 4) and press Sign in. Google or GitHub — whichever you already have.
  2. Approve the sign-in in the popup. That is the whole account: no email to verify, no credits to buy.
  3. The operator grants each tester a monthly allowance of turns. File ▸ Account shows how many you have used.
💰What does it cost you? Nothing during the beta. The operator's account pays for every turn; the allowance keeps that honest. If the game ever says your account has no active subscription, ask the operator — your story is safe and waiting.
⚠What the operator can see. When you play signed in, every prompt the game sends to the model passes through the operator's server, so the operator can read your prompts to run the game. Bug reports say exactly what they send before you send them.

3Optional accounts — nice, not necessary

Signing in is the whole setup. The extras below come with it — nothing to sign up for, nothing to paste:

Optional — scene & portrait images Signed in, the Render button (paints the current scene) and the character portrait come with a weekly allowance of included images — the game tells you when the week's allowance is used up. Nothing to set up.
Optional — cloud sync & cross-device play Your campaigns save to your account as you play and follow you to any device you sign in on — phone, tablet, desktop. Each device also keeps a local copy, and File ▸ 💾 Save / Load can write a file-based backup any time.
Optional — voice narration Nothing to sign up for. Tap the 🔈 speaker button in the game and it reads the story aloud using your device's built-in voice. (File ▸ ⚙ Settings ▸ 🔊 Audio ▸ Voice Settings offers the cloud readers your account includes; the built-in voice needs nothing.)

4Open the game

🌐 https://traffic-and-dragons.pages.dev
  1. Open the link above in Chrome (Android/desktop) or Safari (iPhone/iPad). Other modern browsers work too.
  2. Press Sign in and approve the popup.
  3. Fastest way in: open the Home page (File ▸ 🏠 Home), pick one of the three pre-made heroes and a story, and press Play. The Game Master opens your first scene. The character wizard is the custom path — it is there when you want a hero of your own.

5Put it on your home screen

The game is a proper web app — installed to your home screen it opens fullscreen with its own icon, like any native app.

📱 iPhone / iPad (Safari)

  1. Open the game in Safari (this only works from Safari).
  2. Tap the Share button — the square with the arrow pointing up, center-bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll down the share sheet and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add (top-right). The T&D icon appears on your home screen — launch it from there from now on.

🤖 Android (Chrome)

  1. Open the game in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu (top-right).
  3. Tap Add to Home screen (on some phones: Install app).
  4. Confirm. Launch from the new icon from now on.

🖥 Desktop (Chrome / Edge)

  1. Look for the install icon (a little monitor with a down-arrow) at the right end of the address bar.
  2. Click it → Install. The game gets its own window and an entry in your Start menu / dock.

6Create your character

First launch drops you into a six-step character builder. There are no wrong answers — lean into whatever sounds fun:

StepWhat you're choosing
1 · ToneThe flavor of the whole world — High Fantasy, Gritty, Sword & Sorcery, Dark Horror, Political Intrigue, or write your own. This shapes how the Game Master narrates everything.
2 · IdentityGender, age, and your ancestry — Human, Elf, Dwarf, Gnome, Half-Blood, Hollow-Born, or Cambion, each with sub-lineages and their own stat flavor.
3 · ClassYour profession: Warrior, Rogue, Sorcerer, Ranger, Primal, Paladin, Cleric, Druid, or Necromancer.
4 · AttributesYour six ability scores. Roll (fate decides, auto-arranged sensibly) or Point Buy (you budget 27 points). Plus your moral alignment.
5 · Finishing touchesAppearance, backstory, and a portrait — upload a picture, or generate one from your character sheet (signed in — it comes from your weekly image allowance).
6 · ReviewName your campaign, pick a starting location and level (1 is the classic experience), optionally add companions, and confirm.
💡Don't overthink the backstory. A sentence or two is plenty — the Game Master weaves whatever you give it into the story, and the interesting stuff happens in play anyway.

7Start your campaign & how to play

Hit confirm and the Game Master opens your story with your first scene. From there:

  • Type what you do in the box at the bottom — plain English, first person. "I ask the innkeeper about the missing caravan." "I draw my sword." "I search the body." Anything. The world reacts.
  • Suggested actions appear as buttons after each scene. Tap one to drop it into the input box (edit it if you like), then send. Long-press (or Ctrl-click) to fire it instantly.
  • The dice are real — and yours. When you attempt something chancy the Game Master names the check and you roll; the result is final, and success and failure both push the story somewhere. (Hand the dice back under File ▸ ⚙ Settings ▸ 📖 Narrative options.)
  • Your character sheet — tap your own name in the Party section of the side panel — shows everything: health, gold, inventory, spells, skills, relationships. It updates itself as you play.
  • Quests arrive as ⚑ opportunities. Open the Quests section of the side panel (or the journal in World State) to accept, decline, and track objectives. Nothing is forced on you.
  • Table Talk (tab next to Story) lets you talk to the Game Master out of character — ask rules questions, request a recap, or nudge the tone, without your character saying a word.
  • Rest by saying so in the story — the dashed fourth button offers it when you are hurt. A rest restores party health and mana to full, moves the clock to dawn and makes camp — the place you return to if you die.
  • Dying. At 0 HP you are down, not dead: struggle or yield, and the story resolves it. A true death brings Death, who walks you back to your last camp — ask one question on the way. Three returns per campaign; the fourth death ends the story with a written ending.
  • It remembers. People you meet, places you visit, promises you make — the game files them into long-term memory and the world stays consistent across sessions. Just close the app when you're done; you'll resume exactly where you left off.
🎭Play it like improv, not like a menu. The best sessions come from trying things no button suggests — bribe the guard, adopt the wolf, burn the letter unread. The Game Master keeps up.

8About the UI — a tour of the screen

Two views of the same moment on a phone, in the default dark palette (turn Dark mode off for parchment). Desktop lays the same pieces out wider: the side panel ⑥ is always open on the right, and the top bar also carries World State and Render. The numbers match the sections below:

1The top bar (your HUD)

Your vital signs at a glance — name, class & level, HP, MP, gold. File ▾ is everything else — see the next section. On desktop the bar also has World State (a slide-out panel with your location, the time and weather, everyone you have met and the quest journal) and Render (paints the scene); on a phone both live in the File menu.

2Where you are

The place line — the world and the spot within it — and Connected locations, every way out the map knows. Pick one and its sentence lands in the input box for you to send or edit; it never sends by itself. (On desktop the same ways are chips in the top bar.)

3The status strip

The act you are in, the campaign clock (day and hour), the version, and the small dots at the end: green means the Game Master's memory is in good health, and a cloud badge shows while your account syncs. (Desktop shows the memory ticker here — tokens, chapters, people met, turn.)

4The story area

The main scroll — the Game Master's narration, your own lines, dice results, a turn number over each scene. Under the latest scene: 🔊 reads that scene aloud, Render paints it, and Shop appears when there is something for sale where you stand. When a fight starts, a combat tracker — each enemy's health bar and the round — appears at the top of this area and vanishes when the dust settles; tap an enemy's name for what you know about it.

5Suggested actions

Three suggestions from the Game Master and a dashed fourth from the game itself — rest when you are hurt, use an item you carry, take an offered quest, buy something here, or a reckless wildcard. Tap one to edit it; hold it (Ctrl-click on desktop) to send it as it is.

6The side panel

On a phone it hides behind the thin handle at the right edge: drag the handle left to open it as wide as you like, double-tap it to open or close. Down its edge run the tabs — Pty (the party, with everyone's health), Qst (quests: accept, decline, track), Inv, Ab, Sp — and the 🔈 narration toggle. Tap your own name in Party to open your character sheet; tap a companion for theirs. On desktop the panel is always open with every section in view.

7The voice strip

Appears while the narrator reads — ⏸ pauses and resumes, ⏭ skips ahead. Narration itself is switched on and off with the 🔈 (in the panel's tab column on a phone, in the top bar on desktop).

8The input row

The box where you say what you do (× clears it), the 🎤 mic for dictating instead of typing, and Send. Below it, two tabs: Story (the adventure) and Table Talk (out-of-character talk with the Game Master — a dot marks unread replies).
