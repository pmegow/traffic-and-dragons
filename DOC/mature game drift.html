Mature-game drift — the quest ledger at t1782

Campaign: Rise of the Runelords (Ammut) · save testRuns/Rise_of_the_Runelords__Ammut__Ammut_t1782.tnd · window t1750–t1782 (root cause reaches back to t1742) · engine v1.602–v1.605 era · prepared for Fable review · 2026-08-12
TLDR: All three symptoms the owner reported — quests forgotten, misremembered, and uncredited — are one incident with one cause. Mokmurian died at t1648 and the death is in canon. At t1742 the GM tried to close the arc on it, emitting the death re-assertion, the combat close, 2200 XP, 1500 gold and the quest completion inside one CANON_TXN envelope. The envelope's allow-list (identity.js:827) does not contain COMBAT_END — so the preflight refused the entire envelope, rewards included, and quarantined the receipt. Quarantined receipts never retire, so the conflict row it created is still unresolved 40 turns later, having re-fired 14 times with no response the GM could write to satisfy it. That row then acts as a name-keyed blackout (identity.js:858): while it lives, any response containing the string "mokmurian" that completes a quest loses its quest tags and every XP, gold and item grant in that response — which is why the arc's payoff scene at t1760 paid nothing. Two things then happened that a player can see: the GM rationalised the refused death into the fiction — "Mokmurian himself walked out of that mountain alive… the slain leader was only a proxy" (t1781) — and, when the player pushed back out of character, the GM answered by explaining the engine's internals inside the story (t1782). The bookkeeping did not just fail to record the victory; it overwrote it.

2 · The mechanism Confirmed

identity.js:827, _w2TxnOpReason — the allow-list a CANON_TXN envelope is preflighted against:

var allowed = meta.claim==="npc-death"
  ? {SCENE_DEATH:1, NPC:1, QUEST_STEP:1, QUEST:1, XP:1, GOLD:1, ITEM_GAINED:1}
  : {QUEST_STEP:1, QUEST:1, XP:1, GOLD:1, ITEM_GAINED:1};
...
name=_w2TagName(ops[i]);
if(!allowed[name]) return "unsupported operation "+name+" inside "+meta.claim+" transaction";

The W2 contract (CLAUDE.md §"#168 W2 referential integrity") is deliberately all-or-nothing: "applyMuts preflights the whole envelope… and commits state plus a semantic operation receipt only if every handler succeeds." That is the right design for its purpose — it stops a half-applied death leaving a corpse with no quest consequence. But it makes the allow-list load-bearing in a way it was not sized for: any tag outside the list voids the death and every reward with it.

And COMBAT_END is precisely the tag a boss kill produces. The player killed Mokmurian in combat; killing the last living foe is what ends a combat; the GM emitted [COMBAT_END:victory] as it is instructed to. The engine then refused the death, the completion, the XP and the gold because the GM correctly closed the fight.

2b · The cascade — why three more turns lost their rewards Confirmed

The single refusal at t1742 is not the whole loss. The refusal wrote a row into worldState.identityConflicts, and a second rule keys off that row for as long as it stays unresolved. identity.js:858:

var conflict=w2TextTouchesConflict(ordinary),
    refusalSubject=conflict?conflict.subject:refusedVictim;
if(refusalSubject&&(/\[QUEST_STEP:[^\]]+\|(?:true|done|1|yes|x)\]/i.test(ordinary)
                  ||/\[QUEST:[^|\]]+\|(?:completed?|done|finished|failed)/i.test(ordinary))){
  ordinary=ordinary.replace(/\[QUEST_STEP:[^\]]+\]/g,"").replace(/\[QUEST:[^\]]+\]/g,"");
  ordinary=_w2StripRewards(ordinary);   // strips XP, GOLD and ITEM_GAINED
  console.warn("[identity] quest/reward consequence refused - response carries an "
             + "unresolved or just-refused victim "+refusalSubject);
}

And the predicate it depends on, identity.js:783, is a plain substring test against every unresolved conflict:

function w2TextTouchesConflict(text){
  var q=worldState&&worldState.identityConflicts||[],low=String(text||"").toLowerCase(),i;
  for(i=0;i<q.length;i++)
    if(!q[i].resolved && q[i].subject!=="unknown" && low.indexOf(String(q[i].subject).toLowerCase())>=0)
      return q[i];
  return null;
}

So while that one row lives, any response whose text contains the string mokmurian and which completes a quest or ticks an objective has its quest tags stripped and _w2StripRewards applied to the whole response. That is t1760, t1773 and t1782 exactly, and it explains why the arc's payoff paid nothing.

Two properties make this materially worse than a single lost reward:

  • It is not scoped to the poisoned quest. _w2StripRewards removes every XP, GOLD and ITEM_GAINED in the response, so a reward for an unrelated quest that happens to share a turn with the word "Mokmurian" is destroyed too.
  • It is a substring match on a name. Any character whose name contains a conflicted subject's name is swept in with it.

Because the conflict cannot be resolved (below), this is a permanent, spreading credit blackout attached to a proper noun the campaign will keep saying.

2c · Why it never healed

Two properties compound. First, the entry-13 hardening states that quarantined receipts never retire (unlike committed ones, which retire CANON_TXN_RETIRE_TURNS=12 turns behind a successful summary). Second, the refusal writes an identityConflicts row, which drives a per-turn nudge. Forty turns later that row is still live:

{"subject":"Mokmurian","handle":"-",
 "reason":"unsupported operation COMBAT_END inside npc-death transaction",
 "turn":1742,"lastTurn":1742,"attempts":14,"resolved":false,"lastFired":1781}

Open question Fourteen attempts, zero resolutions. The nudge asks the GM to resolve a conflict whose cause is a tag-shape violation in the engine's own preflight. No prose, no tag, and no player action can satisfy it. This is the project's "permanent noise" class in its most damaging form: not merely a note that will not stop, but one the model keeps trying to obey.

3 · What the player saw Confirmed

This is the part that matters most, and it is the reason this is filed as drift rather than as a missing-reward bug. The refusal did not stay in the bookkeeping layer. It reached the fiction.

3a · The victory was narrated away (t1781)

"Jorgenfist is behind you. Mokmurian's head bought Ironbriar's attention and a season's grace for Sandpoint, but the giant lord's death didn't end the threat the way you'd hoped, not cleanly. Mokmurian himself walked out of that mountain alive when the courier's report proved the slain leader was only a proxy, and the horde beneath the Fogscars remains a live question."t1781 — GM narration

Two mutually contradictory facts in one paragraph: Mokmurian's head was delivered to Ironbriar, and Mokmurian walked out alive because the corpse was a body double. That is not a model failing to recall — it is a model reconciling two irreconcilable inputs.

And the engine really did serve it both. Confirmed The death is recorded in canon and has been since long before the refusal:

memory.npcs["Mokmurian"] = {dead:1648, events:[
  {turn:1652, note:"Body posed to look asleep at his desk after death, with three
                    Thassilonian tablets taken from him before the party fled"}]}

So at t1781 the prompt carried "Mokmurian: DECEASED (t1648)" from the roster and, simultaneously, an identity-conflict note in its fourteenth firing saying that same death had no valid record. The body-double is the only story that makes both true. This reframes the incident: the engine did not merely fail to credit a kill — it held two contradictory positions on the same fact and handed the model the job of reconciling them.

It also disposes of an anomaly worth stating plainly, since it looked like a second bug: dead=1648 is not a mis-scoped write. The kill happened around t1645–1648 ("'Mokmurian it is,' you say. 'Cut the head off the snake.'" — t1645) and was stamped correctly then. What failed at t1742 was a later envelope re-asserting that death in order to close the quest and pay it out — which is why the receipt is named mokmurian_true_death. The death was never in doubt in the data; only the credit was.

The protocol did not fail randomly; it failed under sustained pressure from a note that could not be satisfied.

5 · Defect classes, separated from the incident

The single incident is fixable in a line. These are the classes a reviewer should weigh, because they will produce different incidents next month.

C1 · The envelope allow-list is a whitelist over an open vocabulary Confirmed

Seven tags are permitted inside a death envelope out of a vocabulary of roughly fifty-seven. Every other tag a natural boss-death response might carry — COMBAT_END, and plausibly ENEMY_SLAIN, LOCATION_STATE, CORE_MEMORY, STORY_BEAT, LORE, DECISION, NPC_NOTE, FUTURE_EVENT_RESOLVED, TIME_ADVANCE, CONDITION_REMOVED — is a landmine that voids the death and its rewards. The design assumed the envelope would contain only the death and its direct consequences; the GM writes whole turns.

C2 · A validation failure is silent to the player and permanent to the campaign Confirmed

The refusal produced no toast, no visible marker, and no entry the player could act on. The only player-visible consequence was the fiction going wrong, forty turns later, in a way that reads as the GM being forgetful rather than the engine being stuck. Combined with "quarantined receipts never retire", a single malformed envelope is a permanent campaign condition. Contrast the project's own standard elsewhere: "failures should be loud, visible, and attributable."

C3 · An unsatisfiable nudge degrades the prose Confirmed

The nudge fired 14 times for a condition no GM output could clear, and the GM eventually answered it in the story. Every engine note should have the property that some response satisfies it; a note whose cause is an engine-side shape violation does not. This is a generalisation of the "permanent noise" rule the project already applies to buildSayComplianceNudge.

C4 · Refusal semantics let bookkeeping overwrite canon Confirmed

The most serious class, and the one I would put in front of Fable first. The W2 envelope exists to protect canon from unsupported writes. In this incident it did the opposite: the engine held a valid death record (dead=1648) and an unresolved conflict asserting that death had no valid record, served both to the model in the same prompt, and the model's only available reconciliation was to un-kill the boss in the fiction. A guard that can cause the corruption it exists to prevent needs its failure mode designed, not just its success path.

C5 · A conflict row is an unbounded, name-keyed blackout Confirmed

Established in §2b. One unresolved row disables quest completion and all reward application in any response containing that subject's name as a substring — including rewards belonging to unrelated quests. The blast radius is not the failed operation; it is every future turn that says the name. In a campaign whose central antagonist is the conflicted subject, that is most of them.

6 · Candidate remedies — not implemented

No code has been changed. These are for the review conversation.

  1. Widen the allow-list, or invert it. Smallest fix: add COMBAT_END (and audit the rest of the vocabulary for the same class). Better: treat the envelope as governing only the identity-bearing operations and let unrelated tags in the same response apply normally, instead of voiding the envelope. Risk: the current all-or-nothing semantics are what make replay idempotent — loosening needs care against the #168 receipt contract.
  2. Refuse the operation, not the response. If an envelope carries a tag outside the list, strip that tag and warn, rather than quarantining the death and its rewards. Risk: changes the meaning of a receipt; needs the W2 tests re-derived.
  3. Make quarantines expire or be repairable. A permanently poisoned id with no operator surface is the structural half of this. Options: retire quarantined receipts after N successful summaries, or surface them in a repair tool (the map_cleanup.html precedent).
  4. Make refusals loud. A toast and a persistent marker when an envelope is refused, so the player sees the engine dropped a reward at the moment it happens rather than inferring it from broken fiction 40 turns later.
  5. Bound unsatisfiable nudges. If a conflict's attempt count passes a threshold with no state change, stop asking and escalate to the player instead of the GM.
  6. Repair this campaign. Independently of the fix: credit 2200 XP and 1500 gold, complete "Mokmurian's Army", and clear the conflict row. The Sync modal can do the XP/gold by hand today. The level-boundary question is answered: Ammut sits at 81800 XP against an L11 gate of 85000, so the credit lands at 84000 — no level-up fires, and the t1742 award alone closes 69% of the remaining gap. (Measured by the control-arm replay: 81800→84000, 2160→3660 gp, quest archived completed.)

7 · What a reviewer should attack first

  1. The causal chain from refusal to the body-double narration (§3a). The most consequential claim in the report. The two contradictory inputs are now documented facts, but that the model reconciled those particular inputs into that particular story remains an inference. The alternative worth testing: that a proxy/body-double idea already existed as a genuine plot beat before t1742 and merely collided with the refusal. Turns 1640–1742 would settle it.
  2. Whether the §2b cascade is the intended reading of identity.js:858. I am confident about what the code does — the four-arm replay removes any doubt about behaviour — but less confident it is doing what entry-13 meant. The comment says "a death REFUSED in THIS response", while the predicate reaches every unresolved conflict, forever. If that widening was deliberate, the rationale should be recovered before it is narrowed.
  3. Whether ARC_COMPLETE should have survived a response whose quest completion was de-authorised (§4). It is outside the stripped set, so the arc closed while its quest stayed active — the skeleton and the quest log now disagree about the same arc.
  4. The remedy fork itself. Remedies 1 and 2 pull in opposite directions (widen the allow-list vs. change refusal scope), and both touch the W2 receipt/idempotence contract. That is the design conversation this report exists to feed, not to pre-empt.

Resolved since the first draft, by controlled replay: the unrelated-quest blackout is proven, not inferred (§2b-ii arm 3); the repair's level-boundary question is answered (remedy 6: 84000 < 85000, no level-up); and the t1773 "loss" was a false positive of the ring's 10-label cap (§1).
