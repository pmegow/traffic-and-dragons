# The atlas — TnD Maps in the game (integration plan)

**Status: BUILT 2026-11-05 (TODO #684, ◉ ready to test).** Approved 2026-11-04. Owner rulings, by question chips: the door is **the location name**; the
order is **pane before prompt**; the scope is **new campaigns, opt-in**; placement is **exact names + We are here**.

TLDR: the game vendors `tndmaps.js`, keeps a small per-campaign **atlas** (the recipes of the zones the party has
entered, and where the party stands), redraws every map from those recipes, tells the GM what the map says each turn,
and shows the map in a pane.

## The name

The game already calls its location graph "the map" (`memory.map`, `map_viewer.html`, `map_cleanup.html`). The drawn
maps are therefore the **atlas**: `atlas.js` (engine), `ui-atlas.js` (pane), `worldState.atlas` (store). The vendored
library keeps its own name, `tndmaps.js` / `TnDMaps`.

## What was measured (library 0.12.0, this machine, Node)

| Zone | Generate | Document | Recipe | Digest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| region | 130–265 ms | 303 KB (81 KB gzipped) | 249 B | 3,636 chars |
| city | 93–137 ms | 134 KB | 668 B | 2,518 chars |
| village | 21 ms | 37 KB | — | 577 chars |
| inn interior | 11 ms | 13 KB | 362 B | 1,923 chars |
| sewer | 22 ms | 38 KB | derived from the town | 1,549 chars |
| wild zone | 85 ms | 18 KB | 606 B | 1,031 chars |

`tndmaps.js` is 718 KB of ES5. Documents are far too large for the save (localStorage and every cloud sync), so the
campaign stores **recipes only**, as the brief says.

## The crux: the game's places are narrative, the map's are geographic

The game's location pointer is whatever the story named — "The Salted Wound Tavern", "A shipwreck on the Blighted
Coast" are *world* locations today. The map knows Great Brem, its tavern `tavern-1`, the wild zone `z202_178`. Nothing
ties one to the other, and a guessed tie is worse than none: a ruin pinned as a town of 600 houses would be served to
the GM as canon. So the rule is **the engine never guesses a binding**. A place is on the map only when:

1. the story's name **is** a map name (exact, after case and a leading "the" are folded) — the GM is shown the map's
   names every turn and told to use them, so ordinary travel binds by itself; or
2. the player says so in the pane (**We are here**, one tap on a place).

Anything else is honestly **off the map**: the pane lists it, the GM is told the party's last mapped position, nothing
is invented. Adopting the story's names onto the map (renaming a tavern, placing a preset start) is later work, below.

## Design

- **Store** (`worldState.atlas`, about 1 KB, rides saves, sync and checkpoints):
  `{v, seed, at, zones: {<zoneId>: {kind, parent, via, params, lib, fp, turn}}, names: {<location key>: {zone, id}}}`.
  A zone id is the library's own derived seed (`<campaign seed>/p0`, `…/p0/inn-1`, `…/p0/sewer`), unique by
  construction. `params` holds only what was authored; everything else derives from the parent through the portal `via`.
- **Zone builders are one table** keyed `parentKind>childGenerator` → the library's nesting function
  (`region>town: town.fromRegion`, `town>interior: interior.fromTown`, `town>dungeon: dungeon.fromTown`,
  `region>dungeon: dungeon.fromRegion`, `region>wild: wild.fromRegion`, `dungeon>dungeon`, `interior>dungeon`).
- **Entry**: after a turn commits (never inside the tag parser), `atlasFollow()` reads the location pointer, resolves it
  through `names` or an exact name in the current zone, and enters the zone behind that portal — generating it once.
  Idempotent, so it also runs at load.
- **Documents** are rebuilt lazily from the recipes and held in memory only. Each recipe carries the library version
  and a fingerprint of the document it drew.
- **Version drift** has two guards. At vendoring time a test holds fingerprints for fixture seeds: a re-vendor that
  redraws any of them fails until the upgrader accepts it on purpose. At play time a rebuilt zone whose fingerprint
  differs is said out loud (toast, log, a `redrawn` record on the zone) — never silent.
- **Party marker** is derived from `atlas.at` at paint time, never stored.
- **Prompt**: one `MAP` block — where the party is on the map, the current zone's digest and the current place's
  neighbours with bearings, distances and days — with an explicit cut. Volatile half only; a campaign without an atlas
  is byte-identical. This is drift-surface work: review before, prompt diff on real saves after.
  **As built, two changes from this plan.** The block sits right AFTER `buildGeoBlock()` in `buildSysPrompt`, not
  inside it: Table Talk reuses `buildGeoBlock`, and a dungeon's digest would hand the help desk the chambers the
  player's sheet keeps fogged. The cap is 3,600 characters, not 2,400: a city's digest alone is about 2,500 and its
  list of trades is what sub-locations bind to, so 2,400 would have cut exactly the names the GM needs.
- **Pane** (`ui-atlas.js`): `TnDMaps.view` on a canvas, ink style, a breadcrumb up and down the entered zones, a card on
  a tapped place, **We are here**. Only entered zones can be opened (no peeking into an unvisited dungeon); a dungeon
  shows its way in and nothing else until room tracking exists.

## Proposed order (one commit each, version bumped, tests with it)

1. **Vendor**: `tndmaps.js` byte-identical, `dev/tndmaps.sha256`, load order, service-worker shell, engine manifest.
2. **Atlas engine**: `atlas.js` — store, recipes, rebuild, fingerprints, follow by exact name, and the
   fixture-fingerprint gate (it uses the engine's own fingerprint). No prompt, no UI.
3. **Pane and door**: `ui-atlas.js`, the opt-in at Begin, We are here.
4. **The MAP block** in the geo block — last, on proven ground.

## Independent review (2026-11-05, before the push; owner's ruling)

One read-only reviewer read the four commits and reproduced its findings against the real engine. All were fixed in a
fifth commit, each with a test and a battery clause:

- **The rule was broken (high).** A name match was recorded as a tie and kept for ever, and a sub-location was
  looked for on every sheet entered beneath the town. So "Common Room" in a second inn put the party in the first
  inn's, a room of an invented house became a room of an inn, and a tavern's name used in another town jumped the
  party back — and the MAP block told the GM so. Now only the player's taps are ties; a name is looked up afresh, on
  the sheet the party stands on and the sheets above it, never beside or beneath.
- **A stale card** tied whatever the story named when the button was pressed, not what the button said. The tap now
  carries what it showed and is refused when the story has moved on; a turn that lands repaints an open pane.
- **A party off the map** was still drawn as standing in the place it left, and that place's card withheld We are
  here. The marker is now "Last mapped place" and the card offers the tap.
- **Two false exact positions**: a wild zone entered through its north edge arrived at the next zone's own north
  edge; the sheet above marked the first way the party ever used. Both now use the way that points back, and say
  "somewhere here" when several ways lead in.
- **Smaller**: a store whose zones loop is set aside at load; the once-only notices are per campaign; a merged place
  keeps its tie; the quick start clears a Begin switch an abandoned Review step left on.

Known and left as they are: the HUD's location label follows the spotlight character while the atlas follows the
hero's pointer (a split party); a world place whose name contains `|` shares a key with a sub-location.

## The live spot-check and the tie that was never said (2026-11-05, #692)

The first real GM turns in a mapped campaign ([audit](../audits/AUDIT_684_atlas_spotcheck_v1353.md)) confirmed the design — the GM wrote the exact map name on 6 of 6 moves and the atlas followed across four scales — and found what no gate could: **a tie was kept and never said.** The player placed the story's "The Crossroads of Ashenveil" on Hushwick; the MAP block said only "The party is in Hushwick."; the GM kept the crossroads as its own place and put Hushwick two hours east of it.

**The fix (owner rulings by chips, 2026-11-05).** The where-line says the tie for the place the party stands in: `The story's "<story place>" is at <map place> on this map: the player placed it there.` Ruled "is at" over "is the same place" (which would have the GM re-describe a crossroads as a village and needs a rule about which name its tags keep) and over "lies within" (wrong when the story place IS the settlement under another name). Ruled the current place only over a list of every tie (the prompt would grow with every tap; a far-off tied place is still resolved by the engine when the story goes there).

**The live re-test** (same realm, same tie): the GM answered that the crossroads is "the dead scrub wrapping around Hushwick itself", a mile from the village, and walked in in 25 minutes with the sheet's own layout.

**The independent review before the push** found no high defect and two medium ones, both fixed: a tie to a wild sheet's edge was said as "is at north on this map" (an edge's name is a bearing; it is now said as the edge of its sheet, `ATLAS_TIE_AT`), and the commonest tie — a building the GM invented in a town the map names — had no test. It also showed that the battery's attribution could not fail (the suites print a test's name on PASS too): every clause now requires the named test's own FAIL line. One finding is its own row (#695): a sub-location can be tied to a sheet outside its world place, and the where-line then says two placements that contradict. "The current place only" is read as the place the story's pointer names.

Two more findings from the same run are open rows: same-named rooms of different buildings share one `Town|Name` node in the game's own location graph (#693), and a settlement is described before its sheet exists (#694).

## A room is filed under its building (#693): the rulings, the parked first build, the redesign (2026-11-06)

**The defect, reproduced.** The MAP rule teaches a chamber as a `[SUBLOCATION:]`, the game keys a sub-location `World|Name`, and inside a mapped building a name is an identity only on its own sheet — every inn has a Kitchen. Through real turn commits in the fixture capital, two inns' "Beer Cellar 1" shared the node `Great Brem|Beer Cellar 1`, and the second cellar was told to the GM with the first one's description and its smashed still.

**Owner rulings (chips, 2026-11-06).** (1) A room is filed under its building, "The Old Ram — Kitchen" — over one entry per building. (2) A place the building's map does not have, named while the party is inside the building, is filed under the building too ("The Old Ram — Back Room"): it cannot merge with another building's room; the accepted risk is a place the GM invents elsewhere in town without leaving the inn first.

**The first build, and why it is parked.** It made the map step 0 of the game's one place resolver (`resolvePlaceName`): a room on a sheet beneath the world place's own was named with the zones that hold it, and the atlas walked that name. It passed its test group, the full suite, the replay baselines, prompt byte-identity on three real saves and a live re-test. An independent reviewer then reproduced, through real commits: the fusion still reachable in four flows; a dungeon that ejects the party when the GM names the zone by its own title; a passed-through room filed bare while the timeline calls it qualified; and regressions in the world-twin guard, the party-split fold and the exits. Nothing was committed to master. The build, the reviewer's report and its scripts are on the local branch `wip/693-rooms-under-building` (`dev/review-693/REPORT.md`).

**The mechanism of the failure.** The building a room belongs to was taken from where the MAP last stood. The map follows only the pointer a reply ends on, so any step it could not follow lost the building: an invented room, a re-stated `[LOCATION:]`, a building first entered and a room named in one reply. And a room's name is compared with sub-location leaves in more places than the resolver.

**What the redesign must do.**
1. **The building comes from the pointer.** The sub-location in force at a tag says where the party is: a thing with a way in on the world place's sheet (a building, a grate) or a qualified name puts the party in that zone — entered or not. The map only validates names.
2. **Inside a building, a name is filed under the building** unless it is a thing on the world place's own sheet (another building, a landmark), the zone's own title, or the sub-location in force re-stated. This holds whether or not the building's sheet bears the name (ruling 2).
3. **One name per map position.** Zone names from beneath the world place's sheet down, then the thing. A way in and the room beneath it are one node; a zone named by its own title keeps the party in it.
4. **The follow enters along a qualified name.** It is the writer and may open the zones the name passes through; the resolver stays a pure read.
5. **Every comparison of a name with a sub-location leaf goes through the resolver with the tag's position:** the world-twin guard (`locationWorldTwinConflict`), the party-split fold, the #415 exits, pass-through filing, and the item, state-note and soundscape operands.
6. **A named place operand in a tag run that changes building** is never silently the new building: refuse it loudly or read it where the run began.
7. **A building the player tied under a story name** keeps the story's name for its rooms (the #692 ruling: the engine claims no more than the tap said).
8. **Load:** derive `home` by re-resolving the world place, not from the chain's shape.
9. **The rule sentence the GM reads must be true in every flow.**
10. **Tests:** one test per behaviour, an assertion for each of the reviewer's sixteen surviving mutants, each battery clause naming its own test.

**Accepted limit.** A bare room name written from the street stays a town-wide node, as in every unmapped campaign.

### The second build (2026-11-06): the position is read from the pointer's names

**The model.** A sub-location is a PATH of names joined by " — ": the zones beneath the world place's sheet, nearest first, then the thing — `The Old Ram — Kitchen`, `Temple of Ordrast — The Crypt — Hall of Drashest`, `The Sewers of Great Brem — South Junction`. A bare name is a thing on the world place's own sheet (a building, a landmark, a grate — the way in and the landing beneath it are one node, since the landing bears the grate's name), or an unknown place at the town's level. Nothing new is stored: the atlas keeps no "home" and no "building in force"; both are READ, every time, from the pointer's names through the map (`atlasPosition`), so a step the map could not follow loses nothing.

**Reading a position (pure).** `atlasPosition(A, world, sub)`: the world's zone (its tie, else its name on the sheets from where the atlas stands up to the region — the only use of the atlas's own position, and the world tag re-states it every time); then each segment of the sub-location on the sheet reached so far — the player's tie for that prefix, else the one thing bearing the name (a way in puts the party in the zone it opens, entered or not; a spot ends the walk), else an entered child zone titled so. A sheet the follow has not recorded is drawn for the look and never recorded (`atlasPeekDoc`: an interior is 8 ms, a dungeon 14, a town 50; a bounded session cache). A zone's segment is its own title, except a tied way in that is the zone's ONLY way in, which keeps the story's name (ruling 7: the tap said "Widow Hale's House is at this door"; a sewer with three grates keeps its title, or two grates' chambers would be two nodes).

**Keying a name X at a position (pure, `atlasPlaceCanon`, step 0 of `resolvePlaceName`).** In order: (a) X on the world place's own sheet, and on no sheet the party is inside (`atlasChainBears`, review 2 — inside the Old Ram, a "Beer Cellar 1" the street also bears is the Old Ram's) → the map says nothing (bare, today's steps); (b) X with two or more segments whose first segment names a way in or a tie → the canonical path (the GM may write `the old ram - kitchen`); a segment nothing bears, and whatever follows it, are rooms of the zone reached, so the key is the zones reached and the LAST segment (review 3, finding 1: a key never carries a room in the middle); from the street, a pair of three or more whose first segment is no place anywhere is the invented building and its last room; then, with the party in no zone beneath the world's sheet → nothing (the accepted limit); (d) X re-states the sub-location in force → the same node; (c) X is the title of a zone the party is in → the path to that zone; (e) X is a thing on a sheet the party is in, deepest first: a way back is the thing above it (`Temple of Ordrast — Crypt Stair`, never `… — The Crypt — Crypt Stair`), anything else the path to its sheet plus the map's spelling; a name a sheet bears twice is filed under that zone as written (the map does not pick); (f) nothing bears X → under the deepest zone, as written (ruling 2) — and from the street inside a place the SAME reply invented (the timeline's `fresh`), under that place: the invented building and X, never the rooms the reply passed through between them (review 3, finding 1). A partial path read from where the party stands (`atlasKeyFromPosition`) follows the same rules, and a segment that is the title of a zone beneath a chain sheet extends the chain as the walk would open it. The final segment is folded against existing children (case, article), so no twin.

**The footer pair.** `[LOCATION:Town][SUBLOCATION:Kitchen]` written inside a building: #511 reads the same-world tag with no arrival before it as leaving to the street (the end state is unchanged here), but for KEYING a sub-location in the same reply the timeline carries the sub-location that was in force — a room named after a re-stated town is the room of the building the party was in. `[SUBLOCATION_LEAVE]` is an explicit departure and carries nothing.

**The follow (the writer).** A bare name is followed exactly as before (the tie, then the sheets from where the party stands up to the world's sheet — a legacy save with a bare pointer keeps its spot; a zone named by its own title stays). A path is walked from the world's sheet, segment by segment, opening the zones it passes through (a qualified name for an unentered building enters it); a segment nothing bears, or two things bear, leaves the party in the zone reached with the reason.

**The neighbours (requirement 5).** Every comparison of a GM name with a sub-location leaf passes the TAG'S position (`{sub}`) to the resolver: the timeline's arrival; the world-twin guard (which also refuses a world move onto the last segment of any known child, so W5 keeps its teeth from the street); `[PARTY_SPLIT:]`'s sub-location operand (stored resolved, so the co-location fold matches); `[EXIT:]`'s "already a place here" and the door resolution after a move; `[LOCATION_ITEM:]`'s and `[LOCATION_STATE:]`'s place operands; the soundscape target; the passed-through filing (by the resolved name — a room the party walked through is one node with the room it stands in). Requirement 6: a named place operand in a tag block whose start and end resolve it to different nodes is read where the block began, and the receipt says so.

**Not done.** Legacy mapped saves (three days of an opt-in feature) are not migrated: a bare room node stays bare until the party re-enters its building by name; the follow keeps its spot meanwhile. Node keys never carry "|" inside a segment; the library's names carry no spaced dash (checked over the fixture realm; a map name with one is read whole first, by rule (a)).

**The independent review of the second build (2026-11-06) and what it changed.** The reviewer ([audit](../audits/AUDIT_2026-11-06_693_second_build_review.md)) found every first-review flow closed and four new highs: (1) a one-reply descent — `[SUBLOCATION:grate][SUBLOCATION:chamber]` — keyed the chamber under the sewer's TITLE, which the follow could not open (it knew zone titles only for entered zones), so the party stayed above ground and later chambers split from their nodes; now `atlasChildByTitle` looks behind a sheet's ways down into dungeons (peeked) and the follow opens the zone through its way in; (2) a door written as the sub-location ("the back door", which the digest hands the GM) was a bare town-wide node and the next room fused — a way back onto the world place's own sheet is now the building's own node; (3) a partial path ("Kitchen — Pantry"; "The Crypt — Hall of Drashest" from the crypt) left the pointer on a key no node had, because the timeline keyed it as written and the filer re-resolved the leaf differently — now `atlasKeyFromPosition` reads such a path from where the party stands (a known room in the middle is passed through, an unknown tail is filed under the zone reached) and every key the resolver writes re-resolves to itself without a position (tested over every node of a run); (4) campaigns without a map were not byte-identical — the block rule's two readings differ when a block changes WORLD — so the rule now runs only where the map answered. Mediums: a pair opening with the world place's own name ("The Weeping Hollow — The Silver Cavern" in the cave) names the rest; a town re-stated twice keeps the carry; the carry reaches the item and state operands written after the footer (`state.carried`, read by the resolver as the position); the receipt names both places; a tie renames a building only by a bare story name. Accepted and recorded: a bare `[EXIT:]` or `[PARTY_SPLIT:]` naming a room from the street stays at the town's level (the accepted limit's cousins); the W5 scan from the street folds no article (pre-existing). Rule (d) of the list above has no code of its own — it falls out of (b), (e) and (f).

**Critical pre-review (before the code).** Touches: `resolvePlaceName` (every place writer), `placeTimeline` (the sub in force), `locationWorldTwinConflict`, `fileSubLocation`'s pass-through call, `fileLocationItem`, `fileExit`/`resolveExitsAfterMove`, the split handler, `atlasFollow`, `atlasTiesHere`, the MAP rule sentence. Silent failures it could cause: (1) a fusion the other way — the same room under two keys (the map's name and a tie's, a hyphen and a dash, a way in and a landing) — closed by canonical segments, the only-way-in rule and rule (e)'s way-back clause, each with a test; (2) a world's twin guard that no longer fires — closed by routing it through the resolver with the position AND the last-segment scan; (3) a campaign without an atlas changing by a byte — the step answers null without an atlas (asserted, and the replay baselines stay byte-identical); (4) a peek that writes — the peek cache is separate from the recipes and the tests assert `A.zones` unchanged after a read; (5) a throw inside the parser — the step catches, reports once, answers null. Verification: the engine suite with one test per behaviour and an assertion for each of the reviewer's surviving mutants that still has a meaning (the A.home mutants have none: there is no home), the battery with each clause naming its own test, the full run-tests, the four replay baselines, a live scripted spot-check through the real GM, and an independent review before the push (owner ruling 2026-11-05).

## You are here: a place the GM invents is ADDED to the map (owner rulings 2026-11-06)

**The turn.** "We are here" had the player point at the map and announce the party's place. The owner turned it around: the GM, who is told the map every turn, says where the party is — and a place the GM names that the map does not know is **added to the map**, not left off it. The player's tap stays as the correction, and the player's tie wins over a later declaration by the GM.

**Rulings (chips, 2026-11-06).** (1) An invented place is added to the map; the map library must learn to add a settlement to the region (it could only rename the generated ones, reshuffling the others). (2) A player's tap wins over the GM's later declaration: the declaration is refused and the GM told where the player put it. (3) With no declaration the engine adds the place anyway, beside the party. (4) Legacy mapped saves are not migrated. (5) A town-level place takes its kind from its name (inn, tavern, forge, shrine, …: a real establishment with an interior on a free house near the party), else it is a named feature at a point near the party. (6) The GM may say where: `[MAP_PLACE:X|near A]`, `|between A and B`, `|at A` (X IS the map's A: a tie). (7) Rooms and chambers the GM invents inside a building or a dungeon are drawn on the sheet too, stored as a delta.

**The delta.** Every addition is an EDIT in the zone's recipe (`A.zones[z].params.edits`, the library's own `edits` parameter at every scale: the region's new `add`/`rename`, a town's `add` of an establishment or a custom feature, an interior's `add` of a room, a dungeon's `add` of a chamber). A recipe is still bytes; the library redraws the zone with the edit laid over the seed's drawing, deterministically, and nothing else on the sheet moves (the library's "nothing may shuffle" rule: an added settlement gets no road and shifts no name). An interior is the exception the library allows itself — an authored room claims its spot first and may displace a generated room, in the fixture temple the crypt stair — so the GAME checks: a drawing that no longer bears a thing the sheet had, by id and name, or a way (a portal) it had, is rolled back and refused in words (`atlasLost`; review 2, finding 2); a room is tried on the party's floor at 8 then 6 m², then on any floor, then on each floor the building has, before it is refused. After an addition the zone's fingerprint is re-stamped on purpose — the game's own edit is not drift. `A.adds[storyKey] = {zone, id}` indexes them; one addition per story place; a tap that places that story name elsewhere removes the addition when nothing hangs from it.

**Where an addition lands.** Declared: `near A` → a free point beside the one thing bearing A on the sheet the party is on; `between A and B` → a point of open ground around the midpoint (the eight compass bearings at 50–170 px, nearer first; the A→B direction is one of them); `at A` → a tie for X marked as the GM's (refused when the player already placed X). Undeclared: beside the party's position on that sheet — its LAST mapped place while the pointer is off the map, which it is during the party's own adoption (review 2, finding 3: the first build sent every undeclared world addition to the capital) — a settlement in open country beside the town the party was in (the capital's doorstep only at the opening, nobody on the map yet), a trade in the ROOMIEST free house within 80 px of the party's spot, then 160 (the library's nearest house was a hovel whose inn could not hold its own kitchen), a room on the party's floor of the building when it fits there, a chamber beside the one the party stood in. A declaration the map cannot read (a name that is no thing, or two things) is refused: the place lands at the default and the GM is told once.

**The fingerprint scheme.** The library's region gains a default `edits: []`, which changes the `params` echo of every region document. A fingerprint hashed the whole document, so every saved realm would have read as redrawn by a newer library. Scheme 2 hashes the drawing without `params` (`rec.fpv = 2`); a record without `fpv` is compared under scheme 1 and re-stamped silently when the drawing is the same (the same shape as the `asLib` rule). The fixture gate's table is re-hashed under scheme 2 — the drawings did not change — and `ATLAS_RECORDS_0_12` stays as scheme-1 records, the proof that old records settle silently.

**Library 0.14.0.** Region `edits` (`add` {id, name, kind, at} — the point must be land, the id must not look like a generated `p<n>`, the name is the author's; `rename` {id, name} by id, without a reshuffle; `note`), applied after roads, caves and glyphs so nothing else moves; the added place has no road (its neighbours are "as the crow flies") and a site read from the terrain at its point, so `town.fromRegion` draws its town. The drawn fixture's region entries change by the params echo only (accepted with that note).
