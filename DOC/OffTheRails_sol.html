Off the Rails: Party Presence Failure at the Spire

Campaign: Rise of the Runelords (Ammut) · latest export: Rise_of_the_Runelords__Ammut__Ammut_t1467.tnd · analyst: Sol · 2026-08-07 · independent investigation
Bottom line: the campaign did not suddenly lose every character’s location. It repeated the same split-party lifecycle failure in the opposite direction. The old failure left companions falsely marked absent; the current failure has no surviving split records and therefore marks everyone falsely present. Fiction and state first diverged at t1443, when Daeris stayed at the inn but no durable absence survived. Morwen’s equivalent divergence was t1457, when she remained outside the sealed Spire door. The first visible narrative derailment was t1463, when Daeris appeared inside without travel. By t1466, summarization had converted that mistake into durable memory, so later turns now reinforce it.
Three direct answers: Morwen did not move from inside to outside in the transcript—she was consistently left outside after sealing the door. Daeris unquestionably teleported from the inn into the Spire at t1463. Frizwick unquestionably belongs inside with Ammut, and her exported sheet contains 52/52 HP; her missing health display is not explained by campaign location state.

5 · Root-cause chain

A. The affected versions could erase a correct stay-behind record

The t1431 investigation produced split aging and co-location cleanup in v1.542–v1.544. The co-location rule automatically rejoined a companion whenever their split location exactly matched the party’s current node. That is correct for an old record when the party arrives—but it also erased a new stay-behind record written before the party departed on the next response.

Daeris’s t1443 response was generated on v1.544. Morwen’s t1457 response was generated on v1.546. The one-response grace that distinguishes “fresh stay-behind” from “stale co-location” did not ship until v1.550 (TODO #135, commit 5d8bbfb). Therefore both live separations occurred inside the exact affected window.

Honest evidence boundary: the export keeps cleaned transcript prose, not the old raw state tags or the parser’s transient mutation list. Consequently, two mechanisms produce the same t1467 save: (1) the GM emitted the correct split tag and the v1.544/v1.546 co-location sweep deleted it immediately, or (2) the GM never emitted the tag. The first is the strongest explanation because it is version-matched and mechanically reproduced, but this file cannot prove which branch happened on those two live responses.

B. Once the record is absent, every presence reader defaults the wrong way

The data model uses partyMember:true for membership plus an optional negative override, charSheet.splitLoc, for absence. The default is co-presence:

  • helpers.js:340–356: absent split means the companion inherits the main party location.
  • api.js:61–72: the strict SPLIT THREADS block is emitted only for existing split records.
  • api.js:694–727: every living party member receives a full sheet under the heading “companions fighting alongside the player,” even when a valid split exists.
  • game.js:210–223: buildSceneManifest() unconditionally authorizes every party member as present before checking any location evidence.
  • api.js:269–293: the split audit can age and challenge stale records, but it cannot inspect a record that does not exist.

This is why one missing mutation spreads so widely: the prompt roster, combat kit, suggestion manifest, party size, and UI all begin from membership and require the absence override to contradict it.

C. The player’s plural wording triggered the latent state error

Through t1461 the GM follows recent fiction and writes only Frizwick inside. At t1462 the player says, “reach the room the wives are in,” even though only Frizwick is there. At t1463 the player explicitly calls for Daeris. With no SPLIT THREADS block, the prompt’s structured surfaces say Daeris is a party member fighting alongside Ammut, and the newest user wording presupposes her presence. The model resolves the conflict by inventing an arrival: “Message must have carried further than you’d hoped.”

The wording is a trigger, not the root cause. A healthy state would have supplied a strict “Daeris is elsewhere and cannot assist” counterweight. The player should not have to perfectly remember an invisible structured roster to prevent teleportation.

D. Summarization fossilized the error

The t1459 chapter summary is still correct: Morwen is outside and only Ammut and Frizwick descend. The next chapter summary, stamped t1466, records Daeris’s Sacred Flame as if it were valid history. Daeris’s NPC knowledge and lastSeenAt are updated accordingly. At that point the mistake is no longer a single bad response; it is represented in recent transcript, chapter memory, NPC knowledge, and location memory. That is why continuing forward will make self-correction progressively less likely.

9 · Recommended engine hardening

A. Detect missing transition obligations — TODO #137

After a response narrates a named party member staying behind, leaving, waiting outside, or otherwise changing threads, require a matching split mutation. Detection should be loud and neutral: queue a highest-authority engine note asking the GM to emit a split/rejoin tag or clarify that no separation occurred. The engine must not auto-place a character from prose.

B. Stop equating membership with physical presence

Party sheets, combat instructions, and buildSceneManifest() should derive scene presence through the same effective-location boundary as geography and UI. A split companion may remain a party member and retain a sheet without being described as “fighting alongside” or authorized as a local interaction target.

C. Retain mutation provenance long enough to diagnose field saves

Persist a compact recent record of emitted state tags and applied/ignored mutations, including why a tag was ignored or auto-rejoined. Had the t1443/t1457 responses retained that receipt, the purge-versus-non-emission fork would be decidable instead of inferred.

D. Add the two exact field sequences to the test suite

  • Daeris stays in an inn this response; the party departs on the next response.
  • Morwen closes a threshold from outside while Ammut and Frizwick move into a new sub-location.
  • A correct split survives, suppresses local combat authorization, and replaces vitals with split location.
  • A deliberately missing split tag produces a visible correction obligation instead of silent phantom presence.
  • Summarization does not overwrite an explicit remote location merely because the player names the remote companion.

10 · Final assessment

The campaign went off the rails in layers:

  1. Historical state rot: Frizwick’s t1066 separation survived long after her t1068 reunion, later joined by stale Daeris/Morwen records.
  2. Manual reset: the player repaired all three at t1431/t1432.
  3. Immediate inverse relapse: Daeris’s t1443 and Morwen’s t1457 real separations left no durable records during versions vulnerable to the fresh-split purge.
  4. Visible break: Daeris teleported at t1463 after plural player wording met false authoritative co-presence.
  5. Canon contamination: t1464–t1467 combat and summary writes made the teleport self-reinforcing.

So the answer to “when did it become a mess?” is: the location-state system first showed long-lived rot by t1265, was manually healed at t1431, diverged again at t1443, and became visibly wrong at t1463. The campaign’s story continuity is recoverable; the damaged surface is specifically party presence and the memory written downstream from it.
