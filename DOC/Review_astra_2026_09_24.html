Traffic and Dragons / product review / 24 September 2026

Improvements that earn their place

Ten findings with a concrete player benefit: protect saved work, make actions behave as promised, and give the starter content a clearer payoff.

Astra · review only · eight functional/usability findings + two content recommendations
Only this report was created by this review. No application code, tests, configuration, version markers or TODO records were edited.

Where I would start

First protect the two existing stores of user work: exported files and the designer draft. Then fix campaign ownership of asynchronous renders and the Home-to-game handoff. The keyboard modal issue also deserves early attention because the reproduced failure reaches the Send handler, not merely an inconvenient tab order.

R1 / P1 · protect saved work

A campaign-folder rename can overwrite another campaign’s exports

Player benefit. Keeps a harmless rename from destroying an existing save, image, or character export.

Reproduced with in-memory file-system handles

Evidence and cause

ui-files.js:320–352: renameCampaignFolder opens the destination with {create:true}, even when that directory already exists. _copyDir opens same-named files with createWritable and writes over them. It then removes the source directory recursively. Neither step asks whether the destination belongs to another campaign. ui-files.js:272 also maps distinct names such as “Night?” and “Night!” to the same folder slug.

What I checked

Created two disposable in-memory folders, Alpha and Beta, each containing saves/shared.tnd. Renaming Alpha to Beta changed Beta’s file from “BETA SAVE” to “ALPHA SAVE”, removed Alpha, and reported “Renamed to Beta/”. The shared filename is a supported situation: the export dialog lets the player choose a filename. No real folder or save was modified.

Suggested improvement

Refuse an occupied destination unless it is proven to be the same directory; offer a unique destination before copying anything. Keep the source intact until the entire move succeeds. Preserve a stable mapping from campaign identity to its folder so duplicate or sanitized names cannot silently share storage. This can be a focused file-handling fix; it does not require changing campaign canon.

Proof of improvement

Rename into an occupied directory containing both matching and distinct filenames. Assert every original destination byte is preserved and no source is deleted on refusal or partial-copy failure. Also try punctuation collisions and two campaigns with the same display name.

Scope: Medium · files and campaign-folder UI
The existing #336 folder-root work explains how folders are chosen; it does not make a destination collision safe.

R2 / P1 · protect authored work

“Open in designer” discards the designer’s saved draft

Player benefit. Protects the time someone has invested in writing their own adventure.

Reproduced in isolated Chrome

Evidence and cause

home.html:118–121 writes the chosen catalog blueprint directly into bpd_draft_v1 and marks it dirty:false. That is the very same slot used by the editor’s autosave at blueprint-designer.html:137–141. The editor’s beforeunload warning at line 714 cannot protect a draft when the overwrite happens on Home.

What I checked

Seeded the isolated browser with a dirty draft named “UNSAVED ORIGINAL REVIEW FIXTURE”. Clicked the first catalog card’s “Open in designer”. The stored draft became “The Iron Meridian”, dirty:false. There was no choice to retain the original.

Suggested improvement

Use a separate incoming-blueprint slot. Let the designer inspect it and offer to keep, export, or replace its existing draft. Retain the prior draft until that choice succeeds. Multiple permanent draft slots are optional; the immediate improvement is protecting the one already promised.

Proof of improvement

With a dirty draft stored and the editor closed, open a catalog blueprint. Cancel must preserve the prior draft exactly; an explicit replacement must load the selected blueprint. Repeat with the editor open in another tab so its delayed autosave cannot silently win the race.

Scope: Small–medium · Home and designer handoff
Preserves the designer’s documented “reload never clears the editor” expectation; no editor feature redesign is needed.

R3 / P2 · keep results with their campaign

A delayed scene render can land in the campaign loaded afterward

Player benefit. Prevents another campaign’s scene, portrait references, or exported image from being attached to the current story.

Reproduced through doRender and campLoad with a delayed model stub

Evidence and cause

game.js:3524–3545: doRender captures initial scene data and awaits callGM, then calls addMsg against the current story pane. It has an _rendering latch but does not set busy. ui-campaigns.js:260–295 permits a campaign load when busy is false. After the await, the render also reads the live worldState.character for image seeds (game.js:3631). Save and Portrait callbacks use live state again (game.js:3573, 3605).

What I checked

Started the real doRender function with the prompt response held. It reported busy:false and _rendering:true. Used campLoad to switch to a different synthetic campaign, then released the old prompt. The old campaign’s render-output block appeared in the new campaign’s narrative. No model or image service was called.

Suggested improvement

Capture a render-job identity containing the originating campaign, turn, character and destination. Re-check that identity after asynchronous boundaries and before applying a portrait or recording an export. A stale job may be kept as an explicitly labelled result or cancelled, but must not silently use the newly active campaign. Also stamp the source turn at render start; the current-scene Save handler currently obtains it when Save is clicked.

Proof of improvement

Hold the prompt response, switch A → B, release it, and verify B receives no A output or portrait changes. Repeat while the image response is pending and while a portrait download is pending. Render turn 10, play turn 11, then save: the pointer must still identify turn 10.

Scope: Medium · render job lifecycle
#206 provides historical rendering and #400 proposes a rendering utility. This is a concrete correctness gap that can be fixed without waiting for that larger project. Wrong-portrait and later-turn export effects are source-traced risks; the campaign-switch output leak itself was executed.

R4 / P2 · make the main action reliable

Home’s “Play” choice is ignored when a saved campaign exists

Player benefit. A returning player can start the story they just selected without figuring out an invisible second step.

Reproduced in isolated Chrome

Evidence and cause

home.html:111–115 stores the selected blueprint and navigates to the game. ui-boot.js:403–455 consumes that handoff only in the no-saved-world branch; the saved-world branch resumes the previous campaign. The Quick Start payload uses the same no-saved-world gate.

What I checked

Saved an “Existing review campaign” in the isolated browser, visited Home and clicked Play on The Iron Meridian. The game resumed “Existing review campaign”; The Iron Meridian remained in HOME_PENDING_BP_K. The game screen was visible and no new-campaign choice had been offered.

Suggested improvement

Handle an incoming story choice before ordinary resume. If a campaign is already active, present a clear “Start this story / Continue current campaign” choice and safely snapshot the current campaign before switching. Consume the payload only once that choice succeeds. Keep Quick Start and normal Play under the same handoff policy.

Proof of improvement

Exercise a fresh browser, an existing campaign, a pending Quick Start, and a storage-full snapshot failure. The selected story must start or be explicitly declined; failure must preserve the current campaign and must not strand a hidden pending choice.

Scope: Small–medium · boot and Home handoff
Builds on #290/#307. This is specifically the returning-player path, not a request for another onboarding system.

R5 / P2 · prevent unintended input

Shared modals leave keyboard focus on controls behind the overlay

Player benefit. Keyboard users can operate the dialog, and Enter cannot accidentally submit a story action behind it.

Reproduced with real keyboard events in Chrome

Evidence and cause

ui-shell.js:128–143 gives modalShell an ARIA dialog role but does not move focus, contain Tab, disable the background, or restore focus. The item card uses that shell at ui-sheets.js:254–269. The first-turn overlay already demonstrates working focus/Tab/Escape handling at ui-modals.js:1032–1050.

What I checked

Focused action-input, then opened the item card. Focus remained on action-input. Pressing Enter invoked a stubbed sendAction once; Tab moved to mic-btn behind the card; Escape left the card open. The send handler was replaced only in the isolated browser, so no gameplay request was sent.

Suggested improvement

Give the shared shell initial focus, topmost-dialog Tab containment, background inertness and focus restoration. Respect modal-specific close rules: ordinary dismissible dialogs may close on Escape; explicit decision dialogs should keep their required choice. Test nested dialogs so closing an item card returns to its sheet, not to the story input.

Proof of improvement

Open a dialog from the action box and from a sheet. Enter must never reach underlying gameplay; Tab and Shift+Tab stay in the top dialog; closing restores the initiating control. Verify the existing first-turn overlay and forced-choice dialogs still behave correctly.

Scope: Medium · shared modal accessibility
#23 improved the first-turn overlay and parts of the sheet. Its good focus behavior has not reached the shared modal shell.

R6 / P2 · make deletion trustworthy

Campaign deletion never waits for or displays the cloud result

Player benefit. Players can tell whether a campaign was deleted and retry a failed operation without a confusing disappearing/reappearing row.

Executed call-order probe; failure handling traced in source

Evidence and cause

ui-campaigns.js:461–468 removes the local copy, calls deleteCampaignFromServer(id,null), and immediately reopens the picker. storage-adapter.js:1090–1097 reports a delete error only to console when no callback is supplied. Reopening the picker starts a list sync, which can still see the campaign before its DELETE has completed.

What I checked

The real campDelete function, with storage/UI boundaries stubbed, executed “local delete → remote delete started → picker reopened before delete result”. The callback passed to the transport was null. No remote delete request was sent in this review.

Suggested improvement

Make one UI operation own the delete acknowledgement. Show deletion in progress, await the remote result, and only then finalize the local removal/list refresh. On failure, retain a recoverable local copy and offer Retry with the reason. Keep the existing irreversible-delete confirmation, including a clear indication of what will be deleted.

Proof of improvement

Delay DELETE while allowing the list GET to finish, then test success, HTTP failure and offline timeout. The row must not falsely appear deleted or silently resurrect; a failed cloud delete must be visible and retryable.

Scope: Small–medium · campaign UI/transport callback
UA20 addressed stale campaign-list merging across devices. This finding concerns acknowledgement and ordering of the initiating device’s own delete; it does not call for a new tombstone architecture.

R7 / P2 · give accurate help

Table Talk sends item-definition questions to the wrong workflow

Player benefit. Players discover the existing definition button and can correct item canon without taking an extra story turn.

Verified in the assembled Table Talk prompt

Evidence and cause

table-talk.js:284–288 instructs the assistant that item mechanics become canon only on a STORY turn and explicitly says to raise the issue on the next story turn. The existing defineItemFromStory review flow is independent of that, and ui-sheets.js:254–269 now exposes “Consult story & define” directly on the card.

What I checked

Built the Table Talk prompt for “How do I define Matched runic daggers?” It contained the instruction to use the next story turn and contained neither the new button label nor the Define-button route. This checks the supplied instructions; no live model answer was sampled.

Suggested improvement

Route this question to the item card’s Consult story & define button (and the sheet’s existing Define control), explaining that the proposed definition still needs acceptance. Keep the prohibition on Table Talk itself writing canon. Align the prompt’s help instructions with the UI capability rather than adding another repair path.

Proof of improvement

Assemble the prompt for an undefined carried item and verify that it gives the direct review route, preserves player confirmation and does not claim Table Talk can perform the change. Check a live answer afterward.

Scope: Small · protected help prompt; reviewed implementation required
Follow-through on #230/#435, distinct from changing those features. This review leaves table-talk.js untouched.

R8 / P3 · inexpensive discovery improvement

The curated shelf hides its own better-written story pitches

Player benefit. Players can choose a story by what they will do, without decoding a truncated paragraph of setting history.

Rendered in Chrome; all five catalog cards inspected

Evidence and cause

home.html:124–128 chooses bp.premise before meta.blurb and cuts it at 240 characters. samples/catalog.json:1–32 already contains concise player-facing pitches. In the current render all five shelf descriptions ended with an ellipsis; The Last Lantern-Seed card spent its available space on ancient history and failed to reach the seed-carrying task.

What I checked

The rendered Lantern-Seed card ended “Now the lanterns are failing one by one, …”. Its catalog blurb already says that the player carries the last living seed to relight the road. The Riverlight card was similarly cut before explaining the player’s investigation objective.

Suggested improvement

Use the curated blurb for curated cards, with the premise only as a fallback. Keep the fuller authoring premise in the designer. For library cards, add an optional player-facing summary only if needed; the five existing curated entries already supply the immediate solution.

Proof of improvement

At desktop and phone widths, every curated card should state an activity and stakes in a complete short pitch. Verify the designer still retains the full premise and that an entry without a blurb has a useful fallback.

Scope: Small · shelf copy selection
A targeted improvement to the shipped #44 starter shelf; no extra catalog, recommendation engine or new content is required.

C1 / Content · strengthen the first adventure

Give the Riverlight taster one clear mystery and a smaller path to its payoff

Player benefit. The default quick-start adventure becomes easier to follow, solve and finish in a satisfying first session.

Authored-content review; no live play-duration claim

Evidence and cause

home.html:148–149 prefers the taster for Quick Start. samples/the_riverlight_masquerade_one-evening_taster.blueprint:6–70 currently contains three acts and eight arcs. Act Two reveals that the forgery is meant to be discovered to frame a guest, rather than substituted for the treaty. Act Three’s goal returns to the false treaty being publicly sealed as truth, while its turning point says to expose “the real forger”. Devrin is the commissioner/framer (line 107); the Masked Forger is a distinct scribe (line 119). The closing rule still refers to “five arcs” (line 156).

What I checked

Static comparison of premise, both turning points, finale objective, NPC secrets and closing rules. These supply competing explanations of what midnight accomplishes and of whose guilt must be proved. Eight arcs alone do not prove that real play takes too long, and no such measurement was made.

Suggested improvement

Write one private causal account: Devrin commissioned the forgery to frame Petrell; decide exactly what the midnight ceremony will make irreversible. Make the final objective expose that scheme, distinguishing the paid scribe from the mastermind. For the taster, use a compact spine—arrival, evidence gathering, confrontation—with the other rooms as alternative clue routes rather than eight required resolutions. Let several independent clues identify the same scheme, so missing the go-between does not strand the ending.

Proof of improvement

Run a first-time-player playtest with a stated session target. Players should be able to explain who arranged the frame, what they found, why midnight matters and what their decision changed. Test at least one route that never corners the go-between. Measure completion time before advertising a tighter duration.

Scope: Medium · content edit plus a short playtest
This keeps the masquerade, suspects and twist. The gain is a coherent, reachable payoff, not different fantasy names or prose for its own sake.

C2 / Content · make story selection informed

Preview house rules that materially change how a chosen hero can play

Player benefit. Players can choose a compatible hero and story with informed expectations about major restrictions and costs.

Source/content review of catalog, hero selection and blueprint rules

Evidence and cause

samples/the_weight_of_the_coffered_god.blueprint:156 requires a concrete bodily, memory or years-of-life cost whenever magic is used. samples/the_iron_meridian.blueprint:173 makes a faction operative reading a forged tag automatic detection with no roll. The Home cards display tone/act/NPC/place counts and a premise excerpt (home.html:124–129); Quick Start offers a Warrior, Rogue and Cleric (samples/characters/catalog.json:1–23) without explaining those restrictions at selection.

What I checked

Compared the player-facing catalog/hero choices with the actual rules that will accompany those adventures. No model session was run, so this is an expectation-setting recommendation, not a claim that a particular player has already suffered an unwanted state change.

Suggested improvement

Add one short, spoiler-safe “In this story” note for exceptional rules. Examples: “Magic always carries a personal toll” and “Forged indenture tags fail direct inspection”. Distinguish setting laws from surprises meant to be discovered. Keep the grim rules if they are the intended experience; make the commitment visible before a player selects a spellcaster or plans a forgery-led escape.

Proof of improvement

A reader choosing Maud for Coffered God or Ilse for Iron Meridian should be able to identify the relevant restriction before starting, without learning the antagonist’s secrets. Check the wording against the actual rule and the first encounter in a playtest.

Scope: Small–medium · authored player-facing notes and preview UI
This is not a request to soften the setting, add generic warning screens, or expose GM-only blueprint secrets.
