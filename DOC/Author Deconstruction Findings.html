1. Final verdict — the unified model

Voice ships as retrieval, not description. All ten groups — approaching from stylometry, craft theory, LLM research, genre theory, author criticism, rhetoric, imitation practice, and perception science — independently converged on one mechanism: a name-free directive works to the degree that each of its clauses is a POINTER at something the model already represents densely, and fails to the degree that its clauses are descriptions the model must compose. An author name is not special — it is merely the highest-density label available for a cluster the model already holds. Genre is the most common substitute pointer, but it is one of at least six handle types, and density, not genre-ness, is the operative variable.

The quality of a directive is, in effect: Σ(density of what each clause retrieves) − (interference between composed constraints). This single formulation explains every field result to date:

  • The dial arm lost because twelve co-equal scalars are coordinates with no corpus referent (no craft tradition, no stylometric literature, and no reader vocabulary quantifies style as numeric dials — three groups filed this as an explicit negative). Composition of independent style constraints is a documented failure mode in the steering literature: constraints interfere and the model resolves the conflict by averaging. Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowning Humor 8 is that failure class observed at the prompt level.
  • The distilled arm closed the gap because the hand-authored paragraphs smuggle in pointers: genre nouns ("cosmic dread", "gothic"), narrator roles ("tired-soldier narration", "a war report written by someone with excellent nerves"), and procedural rules ("never name the emotion", "describe by implication") — every one of which is retrievable or executable rather than averageable.
  • Lovecraft beat Poe not because rhythm is intrinsically unspecifiable but because Lovecraft's markers were stated as retrievable lexical categories while Poe's were stated as adjectives ("mounting cadence", "musical") — labels for an unheard pattern. Rhythm IS promptable when written as countable orthographic operations (sentence-length rules, punctuation, named repetition schemes, deviation/contrast rules); it is unreachable only below the token layer (stress, syllables, sibilance), which imposes a real but partial ceiling on Poe.
  • The falsified footer hypothesis resolves cleanly (the earlier idea that a narrator-personification closing image is what replaces the name): the footers that seemed to work ("a cathedral with a whoopee cushion in the crypt", "a bedtime story that knows something it isn't telling you") were never personifications — they are two-schema collision pitches, i.e. compound pointers. The footer hypothesis named the wrong feature of the working clauses.

The design consequence, converged on independently by six groups: the shipping artifact is not a flat paragraph and never a dial list — it is a typed, slotted directive (handle first, then ostensive lexicon, channel-partition rules, countable prosody ops, and procedural stance rules), with the 12 dials surviving only as the authoring UI that regenerates it. See §6.

2. Cross-group convergences

Where independent groups arrive at the same conclusion from different evidence, confidence compounds. Six convergences, in strength order:

C1. Pointer-vs-description is the mechanism 10 / 10 groups

Every group's central hypothesis is a variant of the same claim. The strongest independent formulations: stylometry ("a name retrieves a stored joint distribution; a dial list asks the model to satisfy twelve descriptions and it averages them"), LLM research ("inference-time style control amplifies existing capabilities rather than injects novel ones"), authors-B ("a directive succeeds to the degree it can EXHIBIT rather than merely LABEL"), imitation craft ("corpus-addressable handle + lexicon/procedural constraints are the two channels description can carry").

C3. Rhythm ships as countable operations, never adjectives 9 groups

The lexical-vs-rhythmic sub-finding is refined into enumerable-vs-qualitative. Promptable rhythm = things in the token stream: sentence-length targets and spreads, fragment ratios, punctuation ops (em-dash caesura, semicolon bans), named schemes (anaphora, epistrophe, tricolon, polysyndeton), and — per the perception group — deviation rules ("every third or fourth sentence collapses to under five words"), because readers detect cadence only as departure from a local baseline, never as a consistent average. Unpromptable rhythm = sub-token phonetics (stress, syllable count, sibilance): no notation exists anywhere (music, scansion, ToBI, and SSML all fail to transfer to prose — a three-way independent negative), and the model has no syllable layer. Abercrombie's shipped "most under twelve words" is the project's own existing proof of the countable form; roughly three of five of Poe's rhythm devices are orthographically recoverable, predicting improvement but a ceiling below Lovecraft.

C4. The lab needs blinding and a measurement harness before more verdicts 4 groups

The current protocol is one unblinded judge who can see which arm is the name control — and author-name priming measurably inflates attribution ratings (the wine-label effect, Plassmann 2008 as the domain analog). The name control's lead may be partly expectancy. Fixes converged on: a blind mode (labels stripped, arm order randomized per run, reveal after verdict), duo-trio judging against the flavor reference ("which of these two matches the reference" — the lab's layout is already ISO 13301 shaped), best-worst scaling across the four arms, judging at shipped turn length (150–250 words), and a pure-JS stylometric proxy (Burrows/Cosine Delta over top-frequency words, punctuation rates per 100 tokens, sentence-length mean and SD, fragment rate) scored against the name-control arm's output as the target centroid — no author corpus needed, no API cost.

C5. Distinctness is a set-level failure per-voice testing cannot see 4 groups

Handles collide: grimdark collects Abercrombie, Cook, Abnett, and Moorcock onto one attractor (and no critical source subdivides grimdark into substyles — searched for, not found); gothic collects Poe, Rice, and Muir; sword & sorcery collects Howard and Moorcock. And one collision is already live in shipped data: VOICE_DISTILLED.leguin's "as if the story had always existed and is only now being told" is near-verbatim the phrase critics use to characterize Gaiman. A distinctness gate (blind separability of same-handle outputs) must sit beside the fidelity gate, and each same-handle voice needs a differentiator clause held out from the handle.

C6. This is unpublished territory — the lab is the only instrument 10 / 10 groups

Every group filed the explicit negative: no published work compares author-name prompting against name-free description or genre-name prompting; no work on removing author identity while preserving voice; no prose-rhythm evaluation protocol; no method for synthesizing a handle for an idiosyncratic author. The literature supplies mechanism and constrains design, but cannot adjudicate the genre-handle question. Every step forward is a lab experiment we run ourselves.

  • Constraint budget: ~4–6 hard constraints + at most 2 devices (interference is the documented failure mode past that); prohibitions capped at ~⅓ of clauses, each converted to a positive substitute where possible ("no similes" → "comparisons only to things a soldier has personally handled").
  • The neutral display name IS the handle (Cosmic Horror, Gothic, Grimdark, System Crawl, Deadpan Minimal…) — one string does both jobs — but only after the distinctness gate passes, with a held-out differentiator per same-handle voice.

  • Ship-safety side-finding: the author-name control is not merely undesirable but unshippable — third-party audits report providers now refusing/redirecting named living-author style requests (8 of the 14 are living), and the game is provider-agnostic. A name in vc is a silent mid-campaign voice-loss risk. De-branding is cross-provider robustness, not just legal hygiene.

7. Contradictions & tensions the groups could not resolve

  • Rhythm downstream of lexis? One brief asserts vocabulary-anchored phrasing ("energetic, punchy prose") beats explicit numeric length rules — directly contradicting the countable-ops consensus. Experiment #1's three-arm design exists to adjudicate this; do not ship rhythm ops set-wide until it runs.
  • Detection features ≠ perception features. Stylometry attributes text by unconscious distributional statistics (function words, character n-grams); readers perceive voice through vocabulary, imagery, and jokes. The proxy harness (#7) could be satisfied by prose that still reads wrong. It must be validated against the existing by-ear verdicts before it arbitrates anything — the ear stays ground truth per project rule.
  • Handle strength cannot be predicted a priori. Nothing ranks which labels a model represents densely — "literary minimalism" and "cosmic horror" are both named traditions, but their retrieval density is unknown until probed (#11). Handle assignment for the hard voices stays empirical per voice.
  • Genre attractor vs individuation. A handle raises a voice's floor but pulls toward the genre centroid — "grimdark" may erase Abercrombie into generic grimdark. How narrow a handle can get before it stops retrieving is untested, and is the most likely practical failure of the density hypothesis.
  • Composition of handles is unstudied. Whether handle + dial-deltas + devices reinforce or interfere (two competing descriptions?) has no literature and no lab data yet. Given the dial arm failed BY averaging, handle interference is the obvious next failure mode to watch.
  • Everything is single-passage; the game is hundreds of turns. No evidence anywhere on directive decay, self-reinforcement from in-voice sessionLog history (which predicts the lab UNDERSTATES in-campaign fidelity — the opening turn is the highest-leverage moment), repetition fatigue of countable ops by turn 40, or survival inside the real prompt environment (28+ rules, state tags, second-person present tense — a register none of these authors wrote in). The /playtest corpus is the instrument for this, not the lab.

8. Evidence-quality caveats

Read the group records with these filters on:

  • Web budget exhaustion: the five author/craft/perception-side groups (authors-A, authors-B, rhythm, imitation-craft, perception) report most or all gatherers exhausted their web-search budget and reported from training recall (Feb-2025 cutoff). Their named sources are leads to verify, not citations. The four technical groups (stylometry, llm-style-control, name-pointer, genre-schema) retrieved live sources including 2025–26 arXiv IDs — which this orchestrator has not independently verified either.
  • Caught hallucinations (2): one gatherer invented a nonexistent Peter Clines novel by "Jack Clines"; another wrote its entire Martha Wells section about H.G. Wells. Both were caught and discarded by the Opus synthesizers — but they calibrate how much trust the uncaught remainder deserves.
  • Same-model echo: 50 gatherers sharing one training distribution are not 50 independent witnesses. Cross-group convergence is partly one popular framing echoed ten times; the convergences in §2 are weighted toward claims with mechanistically different supporting evidence, not mere repetition.
  • All compliance percentages are estimates (85–95% lexical, ~70% syllable, etc.) — community consensus recalled from training, not measurements. Never cite them as evidence in DOC_author_voice.md.
  • All ten groups self-rated confidence: medium. None claimed high — consistent with the unanimous finding that the core question is unpublished territory.
  • The existing ear-test verdicts are n=1, unblinded. "Fantastic" vs "fine" — the pair the whole rhythm analysis rests on — is one judgment by one reader who knew which arm was which. Experiment #6 exists for this reason.

9. The ten group syntheses — full record

Each Opus synthesizer's complete output: hypothesis, evidence, implications, and gaps, verbatim. This is the durable record of the research; the raw 50 Haiku field briefs live only in the session workflow journal. Where a record says "Hypothesis 1" it means the falsified footer hypothesis (a narrator-personification closer replaces the name); "Hypothesis 2" means the genre-handle hypothesis (a description works when it resolves to a genre the model represents richly); "finding 1" means the field observation that the raw dial list lost to the author-name control by averaging.

Group 1 — Computational stylometry — what measurably distinguishes authors  medium confidence
Hypothesis
Authorial identity is carried mainly by high-frequency, low-salience DISTRIBUTIONAL features — function-word rates, character n-grams, punctuation rates, sentence-length mean AND variance — which are precisely the features a prose directive cannot specify and a generator will not follow as an instruction. A name works because it retrieves a stored joint distribution over those features; a 12-dial list fails because it asks the model to satisfy twelve salient descriptions simultaneously and it averages them instead. Therefore a name-free directive succeeds exactly to the degree that it does two things: (a) names a retrievable POPULATION (usually a genre) whose distributional centroid sits near the target author — which is why Lovecraft/Poe landed and Muir did not; and (b) converts the residue that no population retrieves into explicitly COUNTABLE mechanical constraints (words per sentence and its spread, comma/dash/semicolon rates, fragment count, literal repetition counts) rather than adjectives. Corollary and the sharpest prediction: the Lovecraft-fantastic / Poe-fine gap is not about lexical-vs-rhythmic being intrinsically unspecifiable, it is that Lovecraft's markers were already stated as retrievable lexical categories while Poe's were stated as adjectives ("mounting cadence", "musical") — restate Poe's cadence as numbers and the gap closes.
Key evidence
  • Function-word frequency is called 'the single most reliable indicator of authorship' independently in all five briefs, and is described as used UNCONSCIOUSLY and topic-independently (Stamatatos survey; Programming Historian 'Introduction to stylometry with Python'; MDPI Authorship Attribution survey). These are exactly the features no style directive ever mentions and no author could self-report.
  • Mosteller & Wallace (1964, Federalist Papers, via toolingup.stanford.edu): 30 'marker' function words separated Hamilton from Madison; Hamilton used 'upon' roughly 10x more often than Madison. The discriminating signal was a preposition rate, not imagery, diction, or tone.
  • Burrows' Delta (2002) operates on the 200-300 most frequent words (overwhelmingly function words): 92.6% correct attribution on single-authored Early Modern English plays, 95.7% on highest-confidence subset; Cosine Delta outperforms it (90%+ adjusted Rand index); >90% typical accuracy across implementations (Briefs 1 and 5). Critically for us, Brief 1 reports Delta holding 80%+ on texts of only 100-550 words — the size of our lab rewrites.
  • JGAAP (Java Graphical Authorship Attribution Program) using this feature family identified J.K. Rowling behind 'The Cuckoo's Calling' (Brief 5). A book-length voice deliberately disguised by its own author was still caught by distributional features — evidence that these features are both decisive and outside conscious control.
  • Character n-grams (n=3-8) reach >=90% accuracy and are explicitly 'robust to topic shift' (Brief 5, N-gram feature selection / character n-gram robustness studies). But the same brief reports word vs character n-gram performance is statistically equivalent (mean 77.90 vs 80.89) — so 'character n-grams dominate' is overstated; the honest reading is that several unconscious surface-statistic families all work.
  • Syntactic features alone hit >77% accuracy across 26 authors (Influence of Lexical, Syntactic and Structural Features, ScienceDirect), and syntactic features are described as 'deep linguistic features comparatively more difficult to consciously manipulate'. POS n-grams underperform lexical/character features because the tag inventory is only ~50-100 items versus thousands of lexical items (Briefs 1 and 2).
  • Sentence length: weak as a standalone feature but contributes meaningfully in multi-feature models, and the VARIANCE/distribution shape carries signal distinct from the mean (Brief 2). Brief 2's explicit NEGATIVE finding: no published threshold ranges define what 'short', 'medium' or 'long' means for prose authors — our 1-10 Cadence dial has no external calibration whatsoever.
  • Punctuation is 'a largely overlooked stylistic feature' yet author-distinctive; tracked as comma rate per 100 tokens, em-/en-dash rate, semicolon preference, exclamation rate; and syntactically CLASSIFIED punctuation beats raw mark counts (Cambridge, 'Pull out all the stops: Textual analysis via punctuation sequences'). No brief could rank which mark is most diagnostic.
  • Style-vs-genre overlap, quantified (Brief 5, arXiv 2411.04950): stop words make up 26.5% of author-distinctive tokens versus 23.7% of genre-distinctive tokens, pronouns the most common type at 11.5-12.96%. Author signal and genre signal live in nearly the SAME feature space — direct mechanistic support for Hypothesis 2 (a genre name is a near-equivalent pointer), and simultaneously its ceiling (genre retrieves a population centroid, not an individual).
  • Cross-genre attribution accuracy drops to 77.27% versus single-genre, and models 'conflate author identity with topic and genre rather than learning genuine stylistic patterns' (Separating Style from Substance, arXiv 2408.05192; CROSSNEWS). Hard-positive plus hard-negative sample selection produced a 62.7% relative improvement in cross-genre attribution — i.e. the field's own fix for genre/author entanglement is contrastive pressure, the generation-side analogue of which is 'two voices that share a genre must be differentiated on something other than the genre handle'.
  • Rhythm/cadence is a consistent NEGATIVE finding in four of five briefs: no dedicated prose-rhythm feature research surfaced; rhythm work is siloed in verse (syllabic-quantity features for Latin, assonance/consonance useful only inside ensembles, accentual regularity acting as an 'inhibiting factor'); Brief 5 states no competition result isolates rhythm as a baseline against lexical methods. The literature has no operational vocabulary for prose rhythm — which is the same handicap our Poe directive has.
  • Vocabulary-richness measures are a dead end as levers: TTR is length-sensitive, VOCD values decline as text length grows, richness metrics show 'extreme variability in large text groups' and are 'of marginal value in large-scale stylistic studies', and are explicitly weaker for GENRE differentiation than for authorship (Briefs 4 and 5). Only MTLD/MATTR/HD-D are length-stable.
  • Brief 3's decisive negative: 'No published work specifically measures how genre-neutral prose DESCRIPTIONS (vs dial lists vs named author references) perform as re-identification vectors.' The exact experiment this project is running has no prior art — no literature will adjudicate Hypothesis 2 for us.
Implications for shipping
  • Stop judging arms by ear alone — add a pure-JS measurement harness to author_voice_lab.html that scores every output on the feature families the field says actually discriminate: a Burrows'/Cosine Delta over the top 200-300 most frequent words, punctuation rates per 100 tokens (comma, dash, semicolon, exclamation), sentence-length mean AND standard deviation, and fragment rate. Use the AUTHOR-NAME CONTROL arm's output as the target centroid (no author corpus needed, no API cost). Delta holds 80%+ at 100-550 words, so pool 3-5 rewrites per arm before trusting a distance. This converts 'fantastic vs fine' into a number and would have caught the dial-averaging failure (Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowning Humor 8) mechanically.
  • Rewrite the rhythm-bearing clauses of every distilled directive as COUNTABLE constraints instead of adjectives, and run Poe as the discriminating case. 'Build unease through repetition and mounting cadence' and 'musical and deliberate' specify nothing measurable; the replacement is explicit: mean sentence ~28 words with a 6-45 spread, at least one phrase repeated verbatim three times with each recurrence shorter than the last, semicolons present, comma rate ~2.5 per sentence. Prediction: Poe moves toward Lovecraft's result and its Delta distance to the name-control shrinks. If it does NOT, the lexical-vs-rhythmic sub-finding is real and rhythm is genuinely un-promptable — which would be a novel finding, since the literature has no answer either.

This predicts a testable ordering: voices who effectively ARE their genre's centroid (Lovecraft/cosmic horror, Poe/gothic, Howard/sword-and-sorcery, Rice/vampire gothic, Abnett/military SF, Moorcock/doomed-albino high fantasy) ship on a short directive; outliers INSIDE a shared genre (Le Guin, Gaiman, Clines) will land generically — recognizable as the genre, not as the voice; genre-less voices (Muir, Wells, Dinniman) need the most authoring. On the planned Wells-vs-Rice discriminator, log a third prediction that Hypothesis 1 and 2 both miss: Rice lands but generically, because the handle retrieves the genre centroid rather than the individual.

Muir = 'gothic funerary ritual' x 'modern group-chat banter', alternating within the paragraph; Wells = 'workplace-competence procedural' x 'anxious first-person interior monologue'; Dinniman = 'livestream/speedrun commentary' x 'body-horror dungeon crawl'. Each pointer retrieves a coherent joint distribution the way a name does; the collision IS the voice. This directly attacks the diagnosed failure mode (averaging) with the mechanism the evidence says works (retrieval).
  • Keep the 12 dials strictly as the authoring UI that generates a distilled paragraph, and never ship a raw dial list into buildSysPrompt. The evidence explains why they lose: dials name the salient, consciously-controllable minority of style (imagery, diction, tone) while the discriminating mass is unconscious distributional statistics; and 12 simultaneous extreme scalars are a constraint-satisfaction problem the model resolves by averaging. Also drop vocabulary-richness ideas (TTR, hapax ratio, Yule's K) from both the dial space and any metric — they are length-sensitive, genre-confounded, and of marginal value at scale.
  • Adopt 'unmasking' as a cheap per-clause diagnostic before the shipping rewrite: ablate one clause at a time from each distilled directive, regenerate, and measure the Delta shift against the name-control centroid. What the evidence could not answer
  • The bridge assumption is untested and the briefs cannot test it: stylometry measures what a CLASSIFIER uses to attribute finished text (function-word rates, character n-grams), while our ear tests measure what a READER perceives as voice (imagery, jokes, diction weirdness). These may be different feature sets. If they are, matching a Delta distance could be satisfied by prose that still reads wrong, and vice versa — the harness must be validated against a handful of by-ear verdicts before it is trusted as an arbiter.
  • Every source is about DETECTION, none about INDUCTION. No brief contains a single result on whether describing a feature to a generator produces that feature. The entire generation side of the project's question is unstudied (Brief 3 states this explicitly for prose descriptions as re-identification vectors).
  • No ablation study anywhere ranks single feature types (lexical vs syntactic vs semantic vs rhythmic vs prosodic) with topic and genre held constant — Brief 5 flags this as an explicit gap. So the briefs cannot tell us whether the Lovecraft/Poe difference is a lexical-vs-rhythmic effect at all, or an artifact of directive wording.
  • No published thresholds define short/medium/long sentence-length signatures for prose authors (Brief 2 negative). Our 1-10 dial bands are therefore uncalibrated inventions; converting them to numbers requires measuring our own reference passages, not consulting the literature.
  • Prose rhythm has essentially no operational feature vocabulary — rhythm research is siloed in verse (syllabic quantity, accentual regularity), and no competition isolates it. Whatever cadence metric the harness uses will have to be invented in-house (sentence-length autocorrelation, clause-length series, repetition counts) with no external validation.
  • Nothing quantifies how far a genre centroid sits from an individual author inside that genre, so the centroid-proximity ranking above is a prediction with no prior measurement behind it. Related: no work compares author-signal portability across dramatically different genres at scale (the largest cross-genre benchmark, CROSSNEWS, covers only news and social media).
  • No comparative ranking exists of which punctuation mark (comma vs semicolon vs dash) is individually most diagnostic, so the harness must weight them empirically rather than by prior.
  • Minimum reliable sample size for our specific case is unresolved: Delta's 80%+ at 100-550 words comes from an AI-detection study on essays, not from short stylized fiction rewrites, so the pooling requirement per arm is a guess that the harness should itself measure (run the same arm twice and check the self-distance floor).
Group 2 — Craft theory of voice — how writers and critics decompose style  medium confidence
Hypothesis
A name-free directive re-creates a voice in proportion to how many of its features are stated as NAMED HANDLES that already carry a rich, procedurally-specified referent in the craft/critical discourse the model absorbed — and genre is only one family of such handles. Craft-canonical constructs are equally good pointers: Gardner's psychic-distance level (1–5, with his own exemplar sentences reproduced across pedagogy), free indirect discourse, Joos's five registers, Saxon-vs-Latinate diction, anaphora/epistrophe/symploce, periodic-vs-cumulative sentence, literary minimalism/deadpan. Conversely, an invented 1–10 scalar ("Ornament 9") has NO referent anywhere in that discourse — Brief 2 records the explicit negative finding that no craft source quantifies style as numeric dials — so the model can only average it. That, not "name vs description," is the mechanism behind the dial arm's loss: it is handle-with-referent vs coordinate-without-referent. COROLLARY that predicts the Lovecraft-fantastic / Poe-fine split: craft discourse operationalizes DICTION densely (nine diction types, three formality/abstraction/connotation axes, etymology tiers, concreteness) and RHYTHM almost not at all (three independent briefs each report no scansion, no stress metrics, no syllable formulae — rhythm is taught by reading aloud and imitation). Therefore a rhythmic voice fails not because rhythm is inherently unspecifiable in prose, but because rhythm adjectives resolve to nothing; it should be specified through the few rhythm handles that ARE conventionalized — countable sentence-length rules, named repetition schemes, and paragraph-unit rules.
Key evidence
  • Brief 2, explicit negative finding: 'NO FINDING of quantified dial in craft literature — distance exists as a spectrum/scale, not as numerical parameters or coefficients (the dial framing appears unique to the author-voice research project).' The project's 12 scalars are an invented coordinate system with no corpus referent — direct mechanism for the averaging failure the lab observed (Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowning Humor 8).
  • Three independent briefs converge on the same rhythm gap: Brief 1 ('no systematic decomposition for phonological/rhythmic specificity beyond cadence and alliteration — no formalized measure of stress patterns or meter; lexical analysis is systematized, rhythmic is descriptive'); Brief 3 ('no research found naming or quantifying syllable-count formulae or stress-pattern metrics… Strunk & White draws a musical analogy but does not decode the mechanism; rhythm taught via reading aloud and imitation, NOT scansion'); Brief 5 ('no research found on systematic methods for teaching rhythm-based tone specificity separately from vocabulary/diction — Poe's rhythmic vs Lovecraft's lexical markers not yet systematized in pedagogy'). Convergent negatives from separate searches are the strongest single result in this group.
  • Diction, by contrast, IS densely operationalized and therefore richly represented: Grammarly/MasterClass nine diction types (formal, informal, pedantic, pedestrian, slang, colloquial, abstract, concrete, poetic); Writing Commons' three axes (formality / abstraction / denotation-vs-connotation); Saxon-vs-Latinate etymology tiers with canonical word pairs (make/create, light/illumination, burn/incinerate) and their documented effects ('Saxon hits readers in the gut, Latinate invites intellectual engagement'); monosyllabic = punch, polysyllabic = formal/abstract. This is exactly the layer Lovecraft's 'fantastic' distilled directive was made of.
  • Gardner's psychic distance is a NAMED five-level scale carried verbatim across Stanford TeachingWriting, UNR Writing Center, Jericho Writers, Gotham, and LibreTexts — including its exemplar sentences ('It was winter of the year 1853…' → 'Snow. Under your collar, down inside your shoes…'). A handle with canonical example text attached is the closest thing to an author name that carries no author name.
  • Free indirect discourse (Wood, How Fiction Works; Fiveable; Writers.com) is a named technique with a precise operational definition — character thought absorbed into third-person narration without quotation marks or 'he thought' tags, producing a simultaneous double view of character feeling and narratorial irony. It is a single-clause handle that encodes Interiority + Irony + psychic distance at once, replacing three of the project's dials.
  • Joos's five registers (intimate / casual / consultative / formal / frozen) plus Halliday's field-tenor-mode give Formality a named ladder instead of a 1–10 axis; Brief 5 adds that register SHIFTS within a passage are the documented signal of emotion, status or transformation. This is a ready-made handle for Muir's sublime-profane collision — 'frozen and intimate register slammed together in one sentence' is a linguistic construct, not a trademark.
  • Brief 5's unanimous scholarly consensus: 'No research found isolating tone as a single, decontextualized feature… tone is radically relational — it emerges from context, contrast, cumulative linguistic choices and reader construction; neither a single lexical property nor a fixed authorial attribute, but an effect of the whole system.' Independent per-dimension scalars are the wrong SHAPE for tone; a single coherent paragraph is the right shape. This is craft theory predicting the distilled arm's win over the dial arm.
  • Booth's implied author (Rhetoric of Fiction, 1961/1983) — 'the personality projected by narrative voice, distinct from the actual author,' inferred by readers from textual choices — is precisely what a narrator-personification footer specifies. This explains the falsified Hypothesis 1 without contradicting it: implied-author specification is ONE handle family among several (genre, craft construct, tradition), effective but not the mechanism.
  • Literary minimalism / deadpan is a named tradition with canonical exemplars and an operational definition: short sentences, quiet charged dialogue, extreme events reported in flat factual language, reader supplies the withheld emotion (MasterClass; Tumbleweed Words; Carver, Hempel, Ford's 'dirty realism', Ellis, McCarthy). Nersessian's 'Notes on Tone' (NLR 142, 2023) sharpens it as 'a degree of contextual discord — being impassive in circumstances that might call for expressiveness.' This is a synthetic genre handle sitting ready for Wells and Cook, both of which the project classes as handle-less.
  • The rhythm handles that DO exist are all countable or named, and the briefs supply the numbers: average 15–20 words per sentence; deliberate variation (5 + 27 + 12 reads dynamic, three 18s read uniform); the 'short, short, long' pattern; periodic vs loose/cumulative sentences with Joseph Williams's point that how a sentence ENDS is load-bearing; King's 'the paragraph, not the sentence, is the basic unit' and single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis; anaphora (start-repetition, 'drumbeat effect'), epistrophe (end-repetition, 'finality, like a period stamped harder each time'), symploce, parallelism. Brief 3's Le Guin case study is measured: 20–30-word sentences shifting to a 69-word sentence with more semicolons for the ice-traversal monotony.
  • Brief 1's five-element consensus taxonomy (diction, syntax, tone, rhythm/cadence, imagery — plus register, POV, dialogue proportion) shows the project's 12 dials are a re-derivation of a smaller canonical set with different, non-canonical names. Cadence/Ornament/Formality/Interiority/Sensory/Figuration/Irony map onto named constructs; Momentum, Darkness, Grandeur, Grit, Humor do not map cleanly onto anything the discourse names — a prediction of which dials are dead weight.
  • Brief 1 also records that 'authorial signature devices (gesture-level style quirks) are identified only post-hoc in analyses, not prescriptively taught' — i.e. Layer 2 device clauses have no pedagogical corpus behind them and must work purely as literal instructions. That is consistent with devices helping but never carrying a voice alone, and argues against expecting a device list to substitute for a handle.

Group 3 — LLM style transfer & controllable generation research  medium confidence
Hypothesis
A name-free directive re-creates a named author's voice in proportion to how well it SELECTS a region the model already represents densely, and it degrades in proportion to how much it asks the model to COMPOSE that region out of independent attribute constraints. Concretely and testably: (a) a short directive anchored to an existing nameable category — genre, register, period, or document-type — beats a longer 12-dial attribute list for the same voice; (b) directive quality stops improving and starts degrading past roughly 4-6 simultaneous hard constraints, because independent style constraints interfere rather than sum; and (c) for voices with no pre-existing category handle (Muir, Wells, Dinniman, Gaiman, Clines), 2-5 short synthetic exemplars generated at build time under the author-name arm close more of the gap than any additional amount of description — because exemplars restore the pointer function that the name performed, without shipping the name.
Key evidence
  • MECHANISM FOR 'A NAME IS A POINTER': steering-vector work states directly that inference-time style control 'amplifies existing capabilities rather than injects novel ones' (Distributed Interpretability and Control for LLMs, arXiv 2604.06483). This is the project's field diagnosis restated in representational terms: a name selects an already-dense direction; a dial list asks the model to construct one.
  • MECHANISM FOR 'DIALS DROWN EACH OTHER': multi-attribute interference is a documented, named failure mode. Multi-Trait Subspace Steering (arXiv 2603.18085) finds that naively adding multiple steering vectors distorts the activation manifold for subsequent vectors and degrades control; the fixes are injection at distinct layers or orthogonalization (K-Steering / Key-Orthogonal Projections, arXiv 2605.06342). C3TG (arXiv 2511.09292) exists specifically because 'existing methods lack coordination for simultaneous multi-attribute constraints.' The lab's observation that Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowned Humor 8 is this class of failure at the prompt level.
  • DIRECT HIT AGAINST THE 12-SCALAR COMPOSITIONAL PREMISE: 'Challenging Assumptions in Learning Generic Text Style Embeddings' (arXiv 2501.16073) reports that low-level style features do NOT consistently compose into high-level style dimensions, and that contrastive separation proved 'too aggressive.' The assumption the two-layer model rests on — that 12 low-level dials sum to a voice — is the assumption that paper found empirically fails.
  • SUPPORT FOR HYPOTHESIS 2 (GENRE HANDLE), INDIRECT BUT THE STRONGEST AVAILABLE: 'Interpretable Stylistic Variation in Human and LLM Writing Across Genres, Models, and Decoding Strategies' (arXiv 2604.14111) measured 1,010 style features across 26 categories over 8 genres and 11 LLMs and found genre exerts a STRONGER influence on stylistic features than the choice of model, which in turn beats decoding strategy. Genre is therefore a large, robust, already-realized axis of stylistic variation — exactly the property H2 requires of a pointer.
  • CONSTRAINED TAXONOMIES BEAT FREE-FORM ADJECTIVES: 'Steering LLMs with Register Analysis for Arbitrary Style Transfer' (arXiv 2505.00679) shows that descriptors drawn from Biber's Multidimensional Analysis register framework produce 'large gains in meaning preservation' and 'reduced semantic drift' versus open-ended style adjectives such as 'sarcastic' or 'humorous.' Its pipeline is exemplar -> extract register dimensions -> apply: descriptions DERIVED from text, not authored a priori. This is evidence for keeping a fixed dimensional vocabulary, but rating it from real prose rather than from the shipped vc string.
  • DESCRIPTION-ONLY IS THE WEAKEST PROMPT FORM MEASURED: 'Authorship Impersonation via LLM Prompting' (arXiv 2603.29454) reports zero-shot statistical-style-summary prompting under 7% accuracy across all models tested, while completion-based prompting reached at or above 99.9% on 4 of 5 models. 'Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet' (arXiv 2509.14543) finds 2-10 exemplars consistently beat zero-shot and description-only. Caveat applied below: both measure detector-evasion, not human ear.
  • EXEMPLAR DESIGN RULES, ALL PROJECT-RELEVANT: gains plateau around 5 exemplars (2509.14543); topic-CLUSTERED exemplars actively REDUCED performance and harmed style diversity (same); example choice swings results by up to 16.3% best-vs-worst (In-context Example Selection with Influences, arXiv 2302.11042); examples nearest the query have disproportionate influence (Learning to Retrieve In-Context Examples, arXiv 2307.07164) — an independent confirmation of the project's audit #2 decision to keep STYLE at the very end of the prompt.
  • SYNTHETIC PARALLEL DATA WORKS: StyleDistance (arXiv 2410.12757v2) trains purely on LLM-generated near-paraphrase pairs across 40 style features and still generalizes to real text, with 92% human agreement on distinguishing positive/negative style pairs, outperforming baselines on STEL and Style-or-Content. CISR's construction rule — same-author, DIFFERENT-topic positives — exists to prevent content leakage. Both underwrite generating de-branded exemplars at build time on scenes unrelated to the game's content.
  • EFFECT SIZE IS HIGHLY UNEVEN ACROSS ATTRIBUTES: human evaluation of style vectors (arXiv 2601.21505) found partial eta-squared of 0.616 for disgust and 0.540 for fear but 0.042 for surprise, with moderate steering strength (lambda about 0.15) the reliable setting. Some attributes are near-inert and over-steering breaks comprehensibility. Predicts a sparse subset of the 12 dials carries nearly all the voice, and that maximal settings are counterproductive.
  • THE LEXICAL-VS-RHYTHMIC SUB-FINDING IS CORROBORATED BY FOUR INDEPENDENT NEGATIVE RESULTS: no rhythm-specific embeddings or cadence vectors exist (brief 2); no steering work targets rhythm separately from vocabulary (brief 4); no research on prompting rhythm/prosody as a text-level feature, prosody being treated as acoustic only (brief 3); and computational stylistics itself calls cadence measurement 'underdeveloped compared to lexical/syntactic analysis' (Computational Stylistics in Poetry, Prose, and Drama, brief 5). Rhythm has no representational handle anywhere in the literature — consistent with Lovecraft (lexical) landing 'fantastic' and Poe (rhythmic) landing only 'fine'.
  • BUT RHYTHM IS EXPRESSIBLE AS COUNTABLE SYNTAX: 'Do LLMs write like humans?' (arXiv 2410.16107) quantifies style as construction rates — present participles at 2-5x the human rate, nominalizations 1.5-2x, agentless passives at 50% — and stylometry's operational rhythm features are sentence-length distribution, punctuation patterns, POS frequencies and function-word distributions (Burrows' Delta; Syntactic Stylometry, Hollingsworth; Syntactic RNN for Authorship Attribution, arXiv 1902.09723, which finds syntactic features content-independent and topic-robust while lexical features are topic-sensitive). Cadence is not unspecifiable — it is unspecifiable AS AN ADJECTIVE. Abercrombie's existing 'most under twelve words' is the project's own proof of the countable form.
  • EVALUATION IS THE CURRENT WEAKEST LINK AND HAS A READY PROTOCOL: the field standard is three independent axes — style strength, content preservation, fluency (multiple surveys; detoxification benchmarks combine them as J = style x content x fluency). BLEU correlates with human judgment at only r=0.64 and is inappropriate for one-to-many tasks; embedding similarity (Sentence-BERT, BERTScore/BLEURT) and perplexity are the recommended substitutes; LLM-as-judge (G-Eval, GPTScore) correlates better but carries positional, verbosity and self-enhancement bias; the human protocol is 2 annotators, 1-5 Likert per axis, Krippendorff alpha reported (0.81-0.83 = strong). Brief 5 states plainly that NO published protocol exists for evaluating author-name-removal fidelity.
  • INTERPRETABLE HANDLES CAN BE MANUFACTURED FROM A CORPUS: LISA (Learning Interpretable Style Embeddings via Prompting LLMs) reaches parity with neural contrastive baselines while producing human-readable style dimensions, and Latent Space Interpretation (arXiv 2409.07072) clusters author embeddings, takes centroids, and has an LLM generate a style description for each — achieving Pearson r = 0.79 agreement versus 0.2-0.4 for baselines, and improving human attribution accuracy about 20% when the explanation is shown. This is a concrete recipe for synthesizing a 'genre handle' for a voice that has none.
  • DISCARDED AS METHOD (kept only as mechanism): all activation-level work — sNeuron-TST (EMNLP 2024), FGAA (arXiv 2501.09929), CAA (arXiv 2312.06681), SAE-TS (arXiv 2509.23799) — requires residual-stream access the project cannot have behind closed provider APIs. Likewise fine-tuning routes (GRPO story-style tuning arXiv 2512.05747; TinyStyler 800M, arXiv 2406.15586) are out of scope for a no-build browser game on the player's own key. GRPO's four-level taxonomy (lexical / syntactic / narrative / semantic, all captured simultaneously) survives as a coverage checklist for what a shipped directive must specify.
  • DISCOUNTED AS OVER-READ: the sub-7% and 99.9% figures (arXiv 2603.29454) and the 19.37%-blogs vs 97.46%-news split (arXiv 2509.14543) come from authorship-VERIFICATION-evasion settings — a far stricter criterion than a reader's ear, and measuring a different construct. They are directional (description weakest, exemplars strong, idiosyncratic voices hardest) but must not be treated as calibrated predictions of ear-test outcomes.

Score every arm on the field-standard three axes (style strength / content preservation / fluency) rather than one gestalt impression; run the name arm and name-free arm BLIND with randomized order (positional bias is a documented LLM-judge failure); use 2 raters on a 1-5 Likert and report Krippendorff alpha; and add a cheap automatic proxy — cosine distance in a style-embedding space between the name-free output and the name-arm output, with real author text as a third anchor. The shipping bar becomes falsifiable: name-free-to-name distance must be no larger than the within-arm variance of the name arm itself. Note explicitly that no published protocol for author-name-removal fidelity exists (brief 5) — this one has to be built, and it is worth building since 14 voices x several arms is beyond reliable eyeballing.
  • TURN DIRECTIVE AUTHORING INTO SEARCH RATHER THAN CRAFT. Prompt-and-Rerank (arXiv 2205.11503) generates candidates then reranks on style strength + similarity + fluency, with small models matching large ones at 1% compute. Applied here: generate K candidate distilled directives per voice (varying the anchor category, the constraint subset, and the rhythm countables), score each with the metric above on the shared passage, and keep the winner. This is a one-time offline cost per voice, it removes the hand-authoring bottleneck for 14 voices, and it makes the genre-handle hypothesis directly measurable — anchored candidates versus unanchored candidates, scored, not judged.

Group 4 — The name-as-pointer phenomenon — how LLMs use author names  medium confidence
Hypothesis
An author name is not a special retrieval mechanism — it is simply the highest-density label available for a cluster in the training distribution. A name-free directive therefore works to exactly the degree that it names a target the model already represents as a DENSE, INDEPENDENTLY-INSTANTIATED cluster (many non-authorial exemplars: pastiche, genre convention, critical description of the style). Genre is the most common carrier of such density, which is why Hypothesis 2 keeps producing hits, but density — not genre-ness — is the operative variable. Where no dense label exists (Muir, Wells, Dinniman, Gaiman, Clines), the only equal-strength substitute is an in-prompt EXEMPLAR passage, which supplies the density directly instead of retrieving it. Corollary (the lexical-vs-rhythmic sub-finding): retrieval density governs DICTION only; sentence rhythm is governed by the decoder's own length/entropy prior, which neither a name nor a description moves — so rhythm-signature voices (Poe) are capped for BOTH arms and need mechanical, numeric constraints rather than a better pointer.
Key evidence
  • Author names are memorized far better than author prose. 'A Study on Verbatim Memorization of Literature' (CEUR Vol-3834 paper96) finds first-author names are nearly ALWAYS memorized while literary verbatim memorization is LOW overall — and that it rises with Fantasy/SciFi genre, web popularity, and UNCOPYRIGHTED status. Read together: what a name retrieves is mostly the dense secondary discourse about a writer (reviews, pastiche, imitators), not their sentences. This is the mechanical basis for 'name = pointer into a dense cluster'.
  • The nearest direct mechanistic evidence: 'Probing LLMs' Understanding of Literary Style' (arXiv 2502.03647) shows Llama-3 8b MEMORIZES author identities from pretraining (especially well-known authors), while Flan-T5 XL reaches comparable style discrimination through learned representations with minimal memorization. Two different substrates for the same behaviour — i.e. the name is a convenient key, not an irreplaceable one.
  • The same paper (2502.03647) CUTS AGAINST a naive genre-handle reading: authorship proves EASIER to characterize than genre; minor syntactic elements (punctuation, capitalization) contribute solely to authorship identity; pronouns matter consistently; word order is crucial (shuffling drops performance below bag-of-words). Resolution adopted here: definability-for-attribution is not the same as density-for-generation. A genre handle is a strong generation attractor precisely because it is a broad, fuzzy, many-instance category — but it will deliver genre-typical average prose, NOT the individuating minor-syntax fingerprint. That predicts the optimal shape is dense handle + a few individuating mechanical clauses.
  • The strongest hard numbers in the group are a regression-to-model-prior signature, not a 'surface vs deep' one. Oxford Academic (DSH 40(2) 587, GPT-4o stylometry): imitating Hemingway, lexical diversity 47.79 vs the original's 34.62 (too diverse); imitating Shelley, 17.70–19.75 words/sentence vs the original's 41.51 (far too short). The model overshoots on vocabulary and undershoots on sentence length in the same experiment — it lands near its own defaults regardless of instruction.
  • Same Oxford study: the stylometric markers LLMs systematically FAIL to replicate are 'recurring lexical choices, preferred constructions, and predictable redundancies'. Poe's shipped signature is literally repetition and mounting cadence — i.e. redundancy. This gives a mechanism for the project's Lovecraft-fantastic / Poe-only-fine split that has nothing to do with de-branding, and predicts the Poe deficit reproduces in the author-NAME control arm.
  • Conditioning strength ranking, from arXiv 2509.24930 ('How Well Do LLMs Imitate Human Writing Style?'): few-shot yields up to 23.5x higher style-matching accuracy than zero-shot; text CONTINUATION reaches ~99.9% stylistic agreement (treat the exact figure as metric-dependent and down-weight it, but the ordering is consistent across briefs); and prompting strategy influences fidelity MORE than model size. Exemplars are a pointer that requires no name.
  • Exemplar economics are favourable: arXiv 2510.13302 (LLM one-shot style transfer) reports one-shot gives 'substantial improvement over zero-shot with minimal additional cost', plateauing around 10 examples. One 120–200 word passage buys most of the available gain — affordable inside a cached system prompt.
  • The counter-evidence on exemplars is domain-scoped, not mechanism-scoped: 'Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet' (arXiv 2509.14543) finds extra examples help little for EVERYDAY authors, 19.37–65.88% accuracy on informal domains vs 95%+ on structured ones. That is a failure to capture weak/implicit idiolect, not a failure of exemplar conditioning for strongly-marked literary voices — the project's case is the latter.
  • Condensing a voice into a short descriptor is a known-hard problem, which independently predicts the observed 12-dial averaging failure: STYLL / augmented-zero-shot work (surveyed in Brief 4) reports descriptive adjectives 'reduce the need for extensive demonstrations' but that compressing an author's distinct style into few words 'remains challenging'; 'Interpreting Style Representations via Style-Eliciting Prompts' (arXiv 2606.05716) needed 1,010 distinct style features across 26 categories to span the space. Twelve scalars cannot.
  • Style can be steered non-lexically: 'Style Vectors for Steering Generative LLMs' (ACL 2024.findings-eacl.52) embeds style directions into hidden-layer activations. Confirms style is a real, addressable representation independent of the name string — but it is an inference-internal lever unavailable through a browser API, so it bounds what prompt-only work can achieve rather than offering a technique.
  • Vendor policy has turned de-branding into a RELIABILITY requirement, not just legal hygiene. Multiple sources (ground.news, FastCompany, IBTimes UK, No Latency's July 2026 audit, Slashdot 2026-07-27) report ChatGPT now refuses named LIVING authors while deceased authors remain compliant; Perplexity refuses both; Gemini complies; Claude/Copilot comply with disclaimers. Notably, the filter targets the NAME, and OpenAI's own redirect is a genre-ish paraphrase ('capture the hallmarks of atmospheric, character-driven horror') — third-party corroboration of the handle strategy's shape.
  • A confound in the project's own ear-test data that the literature exposes: the two voices that landed best name-free (Lovecraft, Poe) are also the two that are long-deceased, public-domain, and surrounded by a century of pastiche — exactly the conditions the CEUR memorization study associates with elevated retrievability. Genre handle and cluster density are currently perfectly correlated in the sample of 3.
  • Explicit negative findings, reported independently by three of five agents (Briefs 2, 3, 4): no published study compares author-NAME prompting against GENRE-NAME prompting; no study isolates a discrete 'corpus pointer' from entangled distributed representations; no study compares lexical vs rhythmic style capture. Hypothesis 2 is unpublished territory — the literature can supply mechanism and constrain design, but cannot confirm it. The lab is the only instrument.

The evidence (STYLL's compression difficulty, 2606.05716's 1,010-feature space, and the observed Ornament-9 + Grandeur-9 drowning Humor-8) says a 12-scalar description is an averaging instruction. The predicted shipping shape is: ONE dense handle (genre/mode/register label) + 2–4 individuating MECHANICAL clauses drawn from the features arXiv 2502.03647 says carry author identity — punctuation habit, pronoun stance, word-order/fragment rule, an explicit words-per-sentence target. Dials stay as the editing UI that regenerates the handle+clauses; they never reach the model.
  • Ship an exemplar for the genre-less voices. The lab already owns 14 original name-free cellar-scene flavor-reference passages written for DOC_author_voice.md — no copyright exposure, unlike quoting real prose. One-shot captures most of the available gain (23.5x zero->few-shot; plateau ~10), so a single 120–200 word passage in the STYLE tail is cheap. Predicted interaction effect worth testing: the exemplar lifts Muir/Wells/Dinniman/Gaiman/Clines materially MORE than it lifts Lovecraft/Poe, because it is supplying density those voices cannot retrieve.
  • Re-run Poe under the AUTHOR-NAME control before spending any more effort on rhythm description. If the mounting-cadence/repetition deficit reproduces with the name present — which the Oxford 'predictable redundancies' finding and the 17.70–19.75 vs 41.51 words/sentence measurement both predict — then rhythm is a decoder-prior ceiling common to all arms, de-branding is exonerated, and the only lever is a numeric/mechanical constraint ('average 30+ words per sentence', 'repeat the key noun phrase three times in the scene'), never a better adjective.
  • Redesign the discriminating test to separate DENSITY from GENRE-NESS, which are currently confounded (Lovecraft and Poe are both genre-handled AND public-domain AND heavily pastiched). Wells-vs-Rice separates Hypothesis 1 from Hypothesis 2 but not density from genre. Add a second pair: a THIN genre label (e.g. 'weird western', 'New Weird') against a THICK non-genre REGISTER handle (e.g. 'hardboiled first-person detective narration', 'field-report military dispatch' for Abnett, 'LitRPG dungeon-crawl system-snark' for Dinniman). Density predicts the thick register wins; strict genre predicts the genre label wins.

The game is provider-agnostic (anthropic/openai/grok/gemini/ollama in PROVIDERS); ChatGPT now redirects named living-author requests, and 8 of the 14 voices are living authors. A name in `vc` is a mid-campaign refusal/redirect risk on a shipped provider, and it would surface as silent voice loss rather than an error. De-branding is a cross-provider robustness fix.
  • Expect the lab to UNDERSTATE in-campaign fidelity, and weight turn 1 accordingly. Continuation is the strongest conditioning regime measured (arXiv 2509.24930), and in a live campaign the model's own prior in-voice turns sit in sessionLog — the run self-reinforces from its own output. That makes beginAdventure the highest-leverage turn for the directive (a flat opening poisons the run), and it means a single-passage rewrite test is the pessimistic case for every arm, including the distilled one.
What the evidence could not answer

Hypothesis 2 and the density refinement proposed here cannot be confirmed from literature; only the lab can test them.

  • Two briefs gloss the same Oxford GPT-4o study in opposite directions (Brief 1: captures sentence-level traits, fails vocabulary richness; Brief 5: excels at vocabulary and sentence structure, fails deeper elements). The underlying numbers were trusted over both glosses, but the source was not read directly.
  • Several arXiv identifiers appear only in Brief 3/4 with no corroborating agent (2603.29454, 2602.02132, 2606.05716); the 3–18% attribution-shift figure and the 1,010-feature/26-category figure are single-sourced and were weighted accordingly.

Group 5 — Genre as schema — validating the genre-handle hypothesis  medium confidence
Hypothesis
A name-free directive works when it swaps ONE POINTER FOR ANOTHER, not a pointer for a description: a genre label is the same kind of object as an author name (it activates a latent absorbed corpus) but at coarser resolution, so it transmits exactly the properties that genre's authors hold IN COMMON — lexicon, props, theme, moral stance, affect — and averages away precisely what varies between authors inside a genre, namely prose rhythm and syntax. This predicts (a) genre-handled voices land in proportion to the size and stylistic specificity of the genre's INDEPENDENT SECONDARY corpus (imitators, gaming, wikis, writing guides), not the original author's own output; (b) no genre handle can ever carry rhythm, because no prose genre has an articulated prosody — which is the structural cause of the Lovecraft-fantastic / Poe-fine sub-finding, not a Poe-specific quirk; (c) therefore the shipping directive must be POINTER + COUNTABLE RHYTHM MECHANICS (sentence-length ranges, fragment ratios, explicit repetition rules), since rhythm is the one dimension descriptions can only transmit by making it countable; and (d) a genre handle raises a voice's floor but COLLAPSES inter-voice distinctness for voices sharing a handle.
Key evidence
  • The single sharpest datum for pointer-strength-lives-in-the-secondary-corpus: "squamous" appears only ONCE in Lovecraft's own work (The Dunwich Horror) yet is indelibly genre-associated, and Lovecraft may be the sole author known for using it (Reactor, "It's Not Squamous"; Arkham Archivist). The marker's force comes from imitators and metadiscourse, not the primary text — exactly what a genre handle points at.
  • Cosmic horror has the densest documented LEXICON of any handle in the briefs: a 568-page dedicated reference work (Dan Clore, *Weird Words: A Lovecraftian Lexicon*, Hippocampus Press 2009), a stable word list (eldritch, squamous, cyclopean, gibbous, batrachian, unnameable, blasphemous), an explicit philosophy (cosmicism), a creature taxonomy (Azathoth → Great Old Ones → shoggoths/Mi-Go), and a large independent author cohort (Lovecraft Circle: Howard, Clark Ashton Smith, Bloch, Long, Kuttner, Leiber; moderns: Ligotti, Campbell, King) plus gaming/comics adaptation. It is also the voice that ear-tested *fantastic* with the SHORTEST distilled directive (0.95x its pointer-stripped vc). Handle density and field verdict agree.
  • Three independent briefs return the SAME negative finding on rhythm: no published study of Lovecraftian prosody exists — "vocabulary is well-documented; prosody appears under-researched" (Brief 2); no scholarly work on grimdark prose rhythm/cadence, community definitions cover "thematic/tonal markers but not stylistic granularity comparable to layer-2 signature devices" (Brief 5, Masters of Fantasy / Book Riot / Dabble corpus); no comparative lexical-vs-rhythmic framework across genres at all (Brief 1). The genre's own self-description has a lexicon slot and no prosody slot.
  • Brief 4 supplies the positive control for that absence: the ONLY genres with documented prosodic convention are VERSE genres — dactylic hexameter for epic, blank verse for Elizabethan tragedy, heroic couplets for Neoclassical didactic verse (Britannica Prosody; Fiveable English Prose Style). Prose genres carry no metrical contract. Poe's markers (mounting cadence, obsessive repetition, tightening circles) live in the slot no prose genre has.
  • Countervailing evidence, and the reconciliation: computational genre classification works on TWO independent feature sets — style-based (function-word frequencies, punctuation, POS tags, character n-grams) and content-based (bag-of-words, topic models) — both effective (ACL Anthology W17-2203; NIH PMC9872011). So genre DOES carry latent syntactic signal. But that signal is statistical and unarticulated: a NAME activates it, a DESCRIPTION cannot transmit it. This reconciles field finding 1 (dials lose to the name control) with the lexical/rhythmic sub-finding under one mechanism.
  • Grimdark is a strong handle whose entire semantic payload compresses into a single phrase — the WH40K tagline "In the grim darkness of the far future there is only war" (multiple sources, Briefs 1 and 5) — and it specifies tone, moral stance, and even DICTION (unglamorous body vocabulary: scars, injuries, illness, bodily functions; "sinister or cynical" narration with "dark and dry" gallows humor; a named failure mode — "brutality without purpose... produces shock content, not grimdark").
  • But grimdark's exemplar cohort collapses four of our fourteen voices onto ONE attractor: Glen Cook (*The Black Company*, 1984, the pre-term precursor), Abercrombie, Moorcock (named as foundational ancestor), and Abnett-by-origin (the tagline is 40K). Brief 5 explicitly: "No critical subdivision of grimdark into substyles appears in gathered sources — genre is treated as unified category." Genre resolution is coarser than author resolution.
  • Dinniman is MIS-BUCKETED as genre-less: LitRPG + progression fantasy is a named genre with platform infrastructure and a 50,000+ title tracking community, in which DCC is benchmarked as "top 2%" / "one of the defining works" against *He Who Fights With Monsters* and *Cradle* (LitRPG Critic spotlight). Crucially, though, LitRPG has "no rigid style mandate beyond 'feels like a video game'; highly variable prose across published works" (Brief 1, mylifemytao / Royal Road forums). A large handle that specifies CONTENT architecture and nothing about prose.
  • Genuinely handle-less voices use a documented substitute form: a COLLISION PITCH naming two well-represented schemas plus an operation between them. Muir — the author's own "lesbian necromancers in space," plus critical consolidations "academic gothic space fantasy" and "Shakespeare with more bones and more gay"; Wells — "cozy mystery garlanded with plasma cannons and spaceships." Brief 3's explicit negative: no single critical genre name exists for either, and "cozy AI" is descriptive reader language, not a classification.
  • This maps onto our own already-working artifacts and re-reads the falsified Hypothesis 1: Muir's distilled closer "a cathedral with a whoopee cushion in the crypt" and Gaiman's "a bedtime story that knows something it isn't telling you" are not personifications, they are two-schema collisions with an operator. H1 failed because it named the wrong feature of the same clauses.
  • Handle strength tracks historical depth and reference infrastructure, and the thin ones are visibly thin: gothic romance dates to Walpole 1764 → Radcliffe 1790s with 250 years of accumulation and an ATMOSPHERE-first definition (pathetic fallacy, rich sensory description, unreliable narrators, madness, family curses — Britannica, NYPL, Fiveable); by contrast "cozy fantasy" is an emerging name with "no lexical/rhythmic style framework identified; mood-driven" and "dark whimsy" rests on a single Medium article. A plausible-sounding coined name is not a pointer.
  • Several established handles specify NO prose dimension at all, which is the dimension-mismatch prediction in raw form: sword & sorcery — scope and structure only (lone thief/mercenary, single city/ruin/night, episodic, morally gray), "no tone-specific lexical markers identified"; urban fantasy — "no lexical style markers identified"; hard SF — "no lexical diction markers identified; prose style varies by author." Handle presence is not handle usefulness; what matters is WHICH slots it fills.
Implications for shipping

Rhythm must be stated in numbers and rules (sentence-length ranges, fragment ratio, paragraph length, 'repeat one phrase from the previous sentence', em-dash interruption counts), never in adjectives, because rhythm is the one dimension no pointer supplies and no adjective transmits. The current VOICE_DISTILLED already does this by accident where it works — Abercrombie's 'most under twelve words' is countable; Poe's 'mounting cadence' is not, and Poe is the one that only landed 'fine'.
  • Retype the TODO #104 shared signature-device registry as a DIMENSION-TYPED registry (lexicon / imagery / stance / rhythm) rather than a flat device list, and require every voice to carry at least one RHYTHM entry. Under this hypothesis the rhythm slot is the only one a genre handle can never fill, so it is the slot that must be authored per voice — that inverts the current assumption that devices are optional garnish (0-2 clauses).

He has the largest community handle of the fourteen; his risk is dimension mismatch, not absence. The genuinely handle-less set is Muir, Wells, Gaiman and Clines, and of those only Wells has markers that are rhythm-only — making Wells the single highest-risk voice for name-free shipping.

Group 6 — Author evidence A — Gaiman, Abercrombie, Dinniman, Clines, Le Guin, Howard, Cook  medium confidence
Hypothesis
A name-free directive lands when it opens with a RETRIEVAL HANDLE — a category the model can already retrieve richly on its own — and genre is only one of three handle types that work (genre, narrative FORM/mode, NARRATOR ROLE). Two corollaries make it testable and both are falsifiable inside this group: (a) the size of the gap a directive must close is set by the model's CORPUS DENSITY behind the author's name, not by how idiosyncratic the voice is — so for thin-corpus authors (Clines, Cook, and arguably Dinniman-as-person) a good handle should MATCH OR BEAT the author-name control, while for dense-corpus authors (Howard, Le Guin, Gaiman) the handle must do real work; and (b) the lexical-vs-rhythmic sub-finding is really ENUMERABLE-vs-QUALITATIVE — a rhythm instruction transfers as well as a lexical one when it is countable ("under twelve words", "no semicolons", "one image per sentence"), and fails only when it is an aesthetic gestalt ("mounting cadence"). Restated as one claim: directive quality = (retrievable handle first) + (countable constraints) − (prohibition clauses), and the author name is not a fixed ceiling.

Group 7 — Author evidence B — Wells, Muir, Abnett, Rice, Poe, Lovecraft, Moorcock  medium confidence
Hypothesis
A name-free directive re-creates a voice in proportion to how much of that voice the directive can EXHIBIT rather than merely LABEL. Lexical identity transfers because a directive containing "cyclopean, eldritch, non-Euclidean" is itself a sample of the thing requested (ostensive); rhythmic identity does not transfer because "measured, incantatory, falling cadence" is only a name for an unheard pattern (descriptive). A genre handle is therefore NECESSARY but NOT SUFFICIENT — it carries setting, mood and diction, never prosody — which is why Lovecraft and Poe, holding genre-handle strength roughly constant (cosmic horror vs gothic, both top-tier corpus tokens, both the project's shortest directives), split into "fantastic" vs "fine". Corollary: voices whose identity is a CONTRAST between two registers (Muir, Martha Wells) are destroyed by the 12 scalar dials specifically because averaging is the operation that erases contrast, and must be specified as channel-partition rules instead. Second corollary: genre handles COLLIDE — several of the 14 voices resolve to the same token, so a de-branded set can pass per-voice review and still fail as a set.
Key evidence
  • Lovecraft, two independent briefs converge (Briefs 4 and 5): the markers are reproducible surface tokens — 'cyclopean', 'eldritch', 'non-Euclidean', 'sanity-blasting', intensifiers 'utterly/hideously/unspeakably', and the adjective-stacking habit itself. Brief 5 states the lexicon 'is highly imitable without his name'. Brief 4 adds a named genre frame Lovecraft himself theorized ('Supernatural Horror in Literature' → cosmic horror). Field verdict: fantastic, shortest directive.
  • Poe, the same two briefs converge in the opposite direction: markers are RHYTHMIC — cadence, alliteration, internal rhyme, anaphora, falling cadence, single-sentence paragraphs for emphasis. Brief 4: 'removing his rhythm substantially degrades the voice; the diction alone is insufficient'. Brief 5: no commercial 'how to write Poe' guide exists because 'rhythm/cadence [is] treated as difficult to teach algorithmically'. Field verdict: only fine.
  • The controlled contrast is the load-bearing item: Poe's 'gothic' handle is at least as densely represented in any corpus as Lovecraft's 'cosmic horror' (Poe has ~180 years of critical legacy and an authored doctrine, Unity of Effect, 'Philosophy of Composition' 1846). Genre handle held roughly constant, the outcomes still split — so the genre handle is not what did the work in the Lovecraft win. The residual difference is lexical vs rhythmic.
  • Rice, explicit negative finding (Brief 3): 'No published critical consensus on a distinctive RHYTHMIC signature (cadence, repetition, stress patterns). Critics focus on ornament, sensation, interiority — not prosody.' Briefs 3 and 5 agree the signature is baroque diction + tactile/gustatory sensory density + philosophical monologue — i.e. entirely ostensive material plus a genre handle (sensual/vampire gothic).
  • Muir, Briefs 2 and 5 converge on ONE device: register collision. Formal Gothic/ecclesiastical/anatomical-medical narration set against contemporary slang and profanity in dialogue, shifting 'mid-paragraph'; reader-consensus term 'tonal whiplash'; extreme sentence-length variance (fragments against long baroque multi-clause sentences). Brief 2's key qualifier: the humour lands because the stakes stay intact — it subverts tone, not stakes.
  • Register collision is unrepresentable on the 12 dials by construction: it requires Formality high AND low simultaneously, partitioned by channel. A scalar averaged model can only emit the midpoint. This is the same mechanism as the project's own field finding #1 ('Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowned Humor 8') — and it explains why Muir needed the most scaffolding without appealing to 'idiosyncrasy'.
  • Muir already has a community-minted synthetic handle: Brief 2 reports 'Queer Gothic' as a genre label applied by the reader community, alongside consensus descriptors 'operatic yet grounded', 'profane and reverent', 'campy and sincere simultaneously'. This directly answers the project's open question for at least one of the five 'genre-less' voices.
  • Martha Wells (Brief 1) shows the SAME shape as Muir under a different surface: a dialogue differential — speech to others sparse, evasive, literal; internal monologue verbose and sardonic. Plus concrete ostensive material: parenthetical deflections ('I did X (not that anyone cares)'), self-interrupt tags 'never mind' / 'that's not the point' / "don't get me started", name-a-feeling-then-immediately-undercut-it, contemporary casual-to-crude diction with no elevated register.
  • So the two hardest 'genre-less' voices in this group are not arbitrary — both are CHANNEL-PARTITIONED. Their handle is a rule ('narration does X, speech does the opposite'), not a genre noun. That is a synthesizable substitute for a genre handle, and it is expressible ostensively.
  • Abnett (Brief 1) has both halves and should therefore be an easy win: a genre handle (military proceduralism / grimdark war fiction), a concrete lexicon sample set ('bone-colored sky', 'corpse-light', 'the smell of cordite and decay'), and two mechanical rules — list soldiers by name and callsign in action (identity as fractional/part-of-unit), and never name an emotion, show it through action, dialogue or physical reaction.
  • Moorcock is the one direct contradiction in the corpus. Brief 3: 'economic, brittle syntax', short sentences, minimal elaboration, passive/determinist constructions, minimal interiority. Brief 5: 'baroque descriptive passages', 'purple prose', 'archaic/poetic register'. Terse vs baroque is not a nuance, it is opposite instructions — evidence that the model's representation of Moorcock is low-confidence and unstable, independent of any genre handle. Group 8 — Specifying rhythm — the lexical-vs-rhythmic gap  medium confidence
Hypothesis
Rhythm is not intrinsically less promptable than diction — it fails when it is written as a FELT QUALITY the model must average ("mounting cadence", Cadence=9) instead of as an ORTHOGRAPHIC OPERATION the model can literally emit. Claim: a name-free directive reproduces a voice's rhythm at diction-level fidelity if and only if its rhythm clauses name countable, orthographically-realized operations — sentence-length targets and long/short alternation, punctuation marks (em-dash caesura, comma/semicolon density), repeated clause openers, and named rhetorical schemes (anaphora, epistrophe, tricolon, polysyndeton, asyndeton, periodic vs loose) — because those are things in the token stream. Rhythm features with NO orthographic realization (stress pattern, syllable count, isochrony, sibilance/assonance) are unreachable through any prompt, which imposes a hard per-voice ceiling proportional to how much of that voice's music lives below the token layer. Corollary for the live Hypothesis 2: "genre handle" is a special case of a more general rule — a directive works when every clause is a POINTER at something retrievable (a genre, a named rhetorical scheme, a countable form constraint) and fails when a clause is a scalar description to be averaged. Poe came out merely "fine" not because rhythm is unspecifiable but because his distilled paragraph specifies rhythm adjectivally while Lovecraft's specifies diction concretely.
Key evidence
  • Architecture, the load-bearing mechanism (Brief 2): subword tokenization gives the model no native syllable-segmentation layer, so syllable/stress constraints are learned approximations while vocabulary is directly tokenized. This predicts exactly which rhythm features are promptable — the ones with orthographic realization — and is the only well-established (non-anecdotal) fact in the five briefs.
  • Compliance asymmetry, directional only (Brief 2, community consensus, NOT peer-reviewed): lexical/vocabulary constraints ~85–95% adherence; syllable constraints rarely above 70%; explicit sentence-length caps ("under 12 words") ~70–80% and DEGRADING with output length; abstract structural instructions ("use parallel structure in every sentence") yield ~1–3 instances in an 8-sentence response, i.e. token gestures rather than systematic adherence.
  • Brief 2 independently corroborates Hypothesis 2 without knowing about it: "named literary genre constraints (gothic horror, noir detective) are more reliably followed than abstract rhythm descriptors — genre-as-proxy is a documented prompt-engineering technique."
  • Brief 2's own summary states the project's sub-finding in reverse: "cadence cannot be reliably specified without vocabulary anchoring — 'energetic, punchy prose' works better than 'vary sentence length from 4 to 25 words' alone; rhythm appears downstream of lexical choice." This is the one item that CUTS AGAINST the hypothesis and must be tested head-on (see gaps).
  • Brief 5: repetition schemes are the promptable half of rhythm — anaphora (n-gram prefix match), epistrophe (suffix match), polysyndeton (conjunction ratio), tricolon (3 parallel units) are all countable and pedagogically "checkable"; explicit "start 3 consecutive sentences with the same word" outperforms "write in iambic pentameter" (Brief 2, poetry-generation case studies). Brief 5's explicit caveat: the leap from "use anaphora" to "achieves Poe-like rhythm" is NOT bridged in any published work.
  • Brief 4's Poe inventory splits cleanly along the orthographic line: repetition/anaphora/epistrophe (orthographic ✓), em-dash and comma caesura fragmenting sentences into "pieces of sound" (✓), accumulation in a short-long-long-LONG crescendo (✓), sibilant/plosive clustering and "phonetic rhythm" (✗, sub-token), suspected isochrony (✗, and Brief 4 reports NO acoustic study exists). Roughly three of five of Poe's rhythm devices are recoverable; the phonetic layer is not — a precise explanation for "fine, not fantastic" and a predicted ceiling below Lovecraft's even after a fix.
  • Brief 4 cross-author typology gives a free difficulty ranking: McCarthy = parataxis/asyndeton/functional-word repetition, Hemingway = short declarative baseline with minimal subordination, Stein = anaphoric loop + gerundive continuity — all three are purely syntactic/orthographic and should be highly promptable; Poe alone is phonetic/musical. Mapped onto our roster, the clipped voices (Abercrombie, Cook, Abnett, Clines, Wells, Dinniman) are rhythmically EASY; the musical high-Cadence/high-Ornament voices (Poe, Rice, Moorcock, Muir) are the hard column.
  • Brief 3, decisive negative: no prose-rhythm notation exists anywhere. Musical notation has no prose beat grid; poetic scansion's binary feet were abandoned for prose; IPA stress marks and ToBI (Silverman et al. 1992) never left phonetics; SSML controls synthesis, not description. Literary criticism has only phenomenological labels — "periodic", "loose", "staccato", "flowing", "run-on". So there is no notation to borrow: any rhythm vocabulary we ship, we invent.
  • Brief 1, the proxy-metric shortlist that IS available: average sentence length (Hemingway 12–15 words vs James 40+), sentence-length variance, clause depth (parataxis vs hypotaxis), punctuation density per 1000 words, em-dash frequency as author signature (Le Guin high, Hemingway near-zero), lexical density, type-token ratio.
  • Brief 1, second decisive negative: no computational framework isolates rhythm from vocabulary and syntax; Burrows' Delta / Mosteller & Wallace measure an authorship fingerprint, not rhythm; Stanford Literary Lab treats rhythm as secondary. Rhythm in published stylometry is "emergent from vocabulary + syntax + punctuation" — which means our numbers can only ever be a proxy, never the verdict.
  • Apparent contradiction, resolved: Brief 1 says punctuation and em-dash patterns are strong measurable author signatures; Brief 2 says structural patterns are weakly constrained by prompt. Both hold — measurable ≠ promptable — and the resolution is the actionable lever: punctuation marks and sentence boundaries ARE literal tokens, so they are the most promptable rhythm surface available, while stress and syllables are not tokens at all.
  • Blanket source caveat: ALL FIVE briefs exhausted their web budget and report from Feb-2025 training knowledge. Brief 2 states outright that no formal study comparing lexical vs rhythmic constraint adherence exists in ACL/EMNLP/ICLR 2023–2025; Briefs 1, 3, 4 and 5 each log their own equivalent negative. The percentages above are community estimates, not measurements, and should never be cited as evidence in DOC_author_voice.md.
Implications for shipping
  • Change the FORM of rhythm clauses, not their amount. In VOICE_DISTILLED, rewrite adjectival rhythm language into countable orthographic ops. Poe's "Build unease through repetition and mounting cadence… long sentences welcome but musical" becomes e.g. "Repeat a key phrase three times across the passage, each return tighter. Break long sentences with em-dashes mid-clause. Follow the longest sentence immediately with one under six words." Same length, same no-name guard, ops instead of adjectives — this is the cheapest discriminating experiment available and it isolates specification-FORM with genre held constant (both arms are gothic).

Group 10 — Perception & testing — how readers detect voice, blind-test design  medium confidence
Hypothesis
What the lab is actually measuring is not idiolect reproduction but a two-part recognition judgment — GENRE FRAME plus LEXICAL MARKERS — and both the model's description-following and the human judge's perception are strongest on exactly those two channels and weakest on rhythm/irony. Three falsifiable consequences follow: (1) a name-free directive can match the author-name control for any voice that resolves to a genre frame or carries a strong diction signature (Hypothesis 2, confirmed from the reader side as well as the model side); (2) the name-control's current measured lead is inflated by expectancy priming, because the lab is unblinded and the judge knows which arm carries the name — a blind, reference-anchored, randomized-order protocol should shrink or erase that gap; (3) rhythm-carried voices (Poe, and by extension Rice/Le Guin/Muir's cadence half) will stay stuck at "fine" no matter how well the paragraph is written, because prose directives specify averages while readers only detect cadence as DEVIATION from a local baseline — so the fix is contrast rules, not better dial values.
Key evidence
  • Lexical features carry roughly 50-70% of authorship-discrimination weight, and vocabulary choice is the single most reliably reader-detected stylistic marker (Brief 4, Mosteller & Wallace authorship tradition + modern NLP stylometry). Readers self-report identifying authors primarily by vocabulary/word choice, only secondarily by rhythm and tone (Brief 1). This is a direct mechanistic explanation for the lab's Lovecraft-fantastic / Poe-fine split: Lovecraft's markers are lexical, Poe's are rhythmic.
  • Rhythm and cadence are recognized as author-identifying but are 'harder to operationalize than lexical/syntactic markers' and are underrepresented even in computational models (Brief 1, prosody/stylometry literature). Readers register rhythm as 'smooth' vs 'choppy' and cannot articulate the pattern (Brief 4, reader-response criticism). Crucially: readers perceive rhythm accurately when it DEVIATES from a baseline (staccato inside flowing prose), not when it is consistent throughout (Brief 4, prosody/speech-perception transfer).
  • Author-guessing as a fidelity metric is confounded with genre/era recognition rather than personal style — the brief states it outright: guessing 'Lovecraft' reflects cosmic-horror tropes, not Lovecraft's distinctive rhythmic patterns (Brief 3, style-fidelity evaluation section). This is Hypothesis 2 arriving independently from the perception side.
  • Readers anchor perceived style to genre assumptions: identical syntactic features rate differently under different genre frames — 'gothic' prose is judged harshly on obscurity, 'hard sci-fi' prose is credited for complexity (Brief 4, GENRE AS PROXY / reception aesthetics, framing effect). Genre is not just a pointer into the model's corpus; it is also a scoring rubric in the judge's head.
  • Author-name priming measurably inflates attribution ratings: readers given the author name as a hypothesis rate passages as more consistent with that author's voice than blind readers do (Brief 1, confirmation-bias/expectancy research). The domain analog is Plassmann et al. 2008 (Caltech fMRI) — identical liquid rated higher sensory scores from an expensive bottle (Brief 2). The voice lab's author-name control arm is the expensive bottle.
  • Distinctiveness requires MULTIPLE divergent features — when authors share surface markers (similar average sentence length, comparable vocabulary level) readers cannot reliably tell them apart (Brief 4, stylometric signal-detection theory). Forensic linguistics states the same rule as doctrine: 'no single marker is dispositive'; reliable attribution needs multiple CONVERGING markers (Brief 5, Coulthard & Johnson tradition). A 12-dial vector that averages toward mid-range destroys divergence — the same failure the lab observed as Ornament 9 + Grandeur 9 drowning Humor 8.
  • Tone is strongly perceived but its SOURCE is misattributed: a dark joke reads as genuine darkness, and sarcasm is missed entirely without context (Brief 4, pragmatics / irony-comprehension studies). This makes Humor and Irony the two least trustworthy dials by ear — and they are precisely the load-bearing dials for the genre-less voices (Dinniman 10/8, Abercrombie 6/9, Wells 6/6, Muir 8/8).
  • Readers overestimate formality when other 'luxury-language' features (complex syntax, rare words) are present, even at identical formality-marker frequency (Brief 4, stylometry interaction effect — readers conflate 'complex' with 'formal'). The doc already concedes Ornament/Formality correlate in the model; this says they are also non-separable in PERCEPTION, so the Custom row's one-dial-at-a-time learning protocol is confounded for at least that pair.
  • Attribution reliability is strongly text-length dependent — short texts (tweets, brief emails) show poor reliability even with multiple markers (Brief 5, computational stylometry consensus). Conversely, rhetorical devices (alliteration, anaphora, epistrophe) are detectable in ~500-word samples but FADE at longer lengths (Brief 4, attention-decay findings). Both bracket the shipped unit: a GM turn is ~150-250 words.
  • Established discrimination protocols exist and transfer structurally: triangle test (ISO 4120 — 3 samples, 2 identical, find the odd one), duo-trio (ISO 13301 — reference shown first, pick which of two unknowns matches it), and ABX with the significance threshold >67% correct over 18 trials = p<0.05 (Brief 2). Duo-trio is the exact shape of the voice lab, which already shows a fixed flavor-reference passage per author.
  • Rating-method consensus for LLM text: pairwise preference + inter-rater agreement (Cohen's kappa) is the gold standard, MOS for ablations, and Best-Worst Scaling — show 3-4 candidates, mark only BEST and WORST — is the resource-constrained method, adopted in WMT/FLORES shared tasks (Brief 3, Louviere & Flynn BWS; Dubois et al. 2023). The lab has exactly four arms per voice: dials, +devices, distilled, name-control.
  • Panel-size and reliability numbers: 3-5 tasters sufficient for a directional comparison, 8-15 for publication-grade (Brief 2, sensory-evaluation texts); trained panelists reach ICC 0.7+ vs 0.3-0.5 untrained (ISO 8586); kappa of 0.4-0.5 is normal for fluency/naturalness judgments and does NOT invalidate the data when the effect size is large and disagreeing raters still favor the same direction (Brief 3, Cohen 1960 applied to NLG).
  • Order and anchoring effects are documented and require countermeasures: later samples rate higher (hedonic drift), the first sample sets the expectation anchor, and demand characteristics let a panelist infer the desired answer from context — countered by randomizing order per judge, reversing order in replication, and removing label information (Brief 2). The lab currently renders its arms in a fixed, labeled order.
  • Forensic linguistics adds a domain caveat: markers vary significantly by genre, register, audience and emotional state — the same author writing fiction vs legal documents shows major stylistic shifts, and profiles fail when an author writes in an unfamiliar genre (Brief 5, documented casework limitation). Every voice in AUTHORS is being transferred to second-person present-tense RPG narration, a register none of them wrote in.
