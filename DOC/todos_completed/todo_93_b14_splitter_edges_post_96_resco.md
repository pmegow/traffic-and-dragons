✅ **SHIPPED v1.603 (2026-08-12).** TLDR: narration can no longer be read aloud in a character's voice, a tagged line can no longer be stolen by another speaker, and lone quote marks no longer reach synthesis as audio blips. **① (tts.js `splitSentences`):** a paragraph whose quote glyphs do not balance resolves in the SAFE direction — every unit in it narrates. It rewrites LABELS ONLY, so unit count and unit text are untouched and no persisted map trips the `sp.n` fuse on this account. Three exemptions, all sabotage-proven: the unterminated run STARTS the paragraph; the next paragraph opens on a quote (the continued-speech typography, B14c-pinned); or the paragraph is the response's LAST and ends with no terminal punctuation. **That third exemption is measured, not theorised** — a census of 23,858 real GM paragraphs (13 campaign exports + 6 harness corpora) found odd parity 27 times (0.113%; only 3 in 18,704 paragraphs of current-model prose), and in ALL 27 the unmatched glyph opened a character still SPEAKING when the response hit its output-token cap (23 ended with no terminal punctuation, several mid-word). The narration-after-a-stray-opener shape has ZERO field instances, so without the truncation exemption this rule's entire realized effect would have been flattening ~24 genuine cut-off speeches while preventing nothing. **①b (game.js `deriveSpeakerMapFromTags`):** each segment now carries a quoted-run MASK beside its quote-stripped text, and a key must LAND inside a quoted run. Quote state carries ACROSS segment boundaries — a `[SAY:]` tag does not reset parity, and `"[SAY:X]Ten out of ten,"` puts the opener in the PREVIOUS segment, so restarting per segment would narrate that correctly-authored shape flat forever AND nag the GM through `buildSayComplianceNudge` for prose it cannot improve — and RESETS at paragraph breaks, mirroring `splitSentences`' per-paragraph `_inQ`. **It caught a real one:** replaying the 23 `[SAY:]`-tagged responses held in the saves, t1667 had Frizwick's `"There,"` spoken in **Daeris's** voice — narration inside Daeris's segment (`...stays there, small, deliberate`) matched the key first and the forward-only cursor never reached Frizwick's segment. Now correct. **③:** punctuation-only units fold into a neighbour (backward, and the paragraph's FIRST unit forward, which the old `k > 0` bound could not reach) — text is moved, never dropped; a wholly-punctuation paragraph has no neighbour and is left exactly as it was. **② `speakerSpans` DELETED** (dead since the v1.451 rework); its surviving invariant is asserted directly on units in the B14b test. **Verification:** 1333 green, 14 new tests all written failing-first (6 red before the fix); a 14-mutation sabotage battery in which every guarded branch trips a check and both sources restore byte-identically — it also caught two branches the first pass left unproven (the forward fold, the paragraph reset) and four stale needles as no-op mutations. **Blast radius, measured old-vs-new over the 3,331-entry t1667 campaign (2.08M chars of GM prose):** junk units 87 → 13 (the 13 are punctuation-only paragraphs with no neighbour); 58 entries (1.74%) change unit count; 5 entries change labels, ALL of them flattening, ZERO gaining a dialogue label; 24 of 611 stored speaker maps degrade to mono-voice replay via the designed `sp.n` fuse (the v1.423 precedent); compliance-nudge firing unchanged (4 → 4). **Residuals, honestly:** curly quotes are absent from the real corpus (0 of 23,085 glyphs), so every curly branch is pinned by test only and has no field evidence behind it; the clause regex still drops a leading `,` when a piece opens on one (inaudible, a separate class, not fixed here); and the defect shape surviving inside a TRUNCATED final paragraph is deliberately unprotected, because there the truncation reading wins. 



⚠️ **v1.603 SHIPPED A REGRESSION; CORRECTED IN v1.604 (2026-08-12).** TLDR: the first cut built its quoted-run mask from the RAW response while the units it must match come from the CLEAN text, and that asymmetry could hand a tagged line to the WRONG CHARACTER — the exact harm this row exists to remove. Found by an adversarial review pass (3 census agents over 23,858 real paragraphs + 3 critique agents + an adjudicator that re-executed all 17 findings in isolated processes), reproduced by hand against v1.602 before any of it was believed. **The three v1.603 defects, all now fixed and sabotage-pinned:** ① `cleanTxt` CREATES paragraph breaks the raw does not have, by stripping a tag that sat alone on its own line; `splitSentences` reset its quote state there and the mask did not, so the correct in-quote hit was rejected and the forward-only cursor walked into a LATER speaker's segment — `[SAY:Daeris]"First part.\n[SAY:Frizwick]\n"Second part," he says.\n\n[SAY:Cyd]"Second part," she echoes.` gave Frizwick's own line to **Cyd**. ② a quote glyph inside ANY stripped tag payload (`[NPC_NOTE:…she keeps saying "ten out of ten]`) toggled the mask, so every later key landed unmasked and the WHOLE map went null — the entire response narrating flat. ③ flattening the whole paragraph killed correctly-tagged balanced quotes that merely shared a paragraph with a stray glyph. **The v1.604 rule is role-based, not parity-based:** `quoteFault()` classifies each glyph by typography (text-left + space-right closes; space-left + text-right opens; ambiguous falls back to parity) and returns the FIRST unmatched glyph, and only the quote runs at or after that fault are demoted — so an earlier closed quotation keeps its voice. The ambiguity fallback is load-bearing and was found by measurement: `normalizeForTTS` rewrites `...` to an ellipsis PLUS A SPACE, so a real closer in `And you..."` ends up with whitespace on both sides; without the fallback the rule misfired on 41 units across 22 corpus documents, with it, 4. **The next-paragraph exemption is RETIRED** — one ordinary quoted paragraph appended after a faulty one disarmed the whole rule (restoring the wrong-voice bug), it applied at 27% of paragraph positions, and the shape it protected has zero occurrences in the corpus; the genuine continued-speech fixture is protected by the opens-paragraph exemption, verified by ablation. **Deriver fixes:** cleanTxt's own regexes strip tags before the scan (silently — reusing its catch-all `console.warn` would be permanent console noise), a `tail` seam on the shared state detects a break that STRADDLES the `[SAY:]` cut (stripping alone does NOT fix that case — the tag creating the break is the SAY tag itself, which is in no slice; this was built and measured, not assumed), and a FLATTENED unit now consumes the forward-only cursor while taking no voice, so its text cannot be re-claimed by a later speaker's identical tagged line. Folds are voice-aware in layers — a hard gate at the piece level, preference-order at the paragraph level (a hard gate there resurrects the lone-quote blip on `"...," she said.`). **Measured v1.602 → v1.604 across 3,902 real GM documents:** junk units 164 → 26, B14c straddlers 1 → **0** (better than the pre-#93 baseline, which had one), dialogue units 20,872 → 20,720, 15 demotions across 100 changed documents, and **ZERO narration→dialogue promotions** — the safe direction holds absolutely. 1338 green; an 18-mutation sabotage battery, each mutation in its OWN PROCESS because the deriver's helpers are file-scope globals and in-process reloading silently contaminates every snapshot (that trap bit both me and one review agent). Sabotage also caught a worthless check of mine: the cursor-consumption guard is only exercised when the duplicate text sits INSIDE the flattened run, because an earlier closed quote advances the cursor by itself — the test now pins the shape that actually proves it. **Residual, deliberately left:** truncated speech whose cap happened to land on a period (`…Before the centuries.`) still flattens after its first closed quote — widening the truncation exemption to ANY trailing fault in a final paragraph would drop the corpus label changes to zero, but retires that protection for most GM dialogue, which sits in final paragraphs. **Filed separately, NOT #93:** `sayTagCoverage`/`paragraphGaps` precision defects (an `/i`-flagged attribution regex matching scare-quoted narration; `paragraphGaps` as an unconditional floor on `missing`; `qm` searched on raw text while `plain` is tag-stripped) make `buildSayComplianceNudge` fire on responses whose speaker map is 100% correct — identical on v1.602 and v1.604, so #93 neither caused nor worsens them, and folding a detector redesign into a regression fix would be wrong.
