## Task

**The antiquity ratchet — cap "how old is old" in the blueprint with a named era ladder (owner ruling 2026-08-23: "it's just lazy. Anytime a new scene is introduced it's 'older' than the last").** TLDR: the GM signals that a new scene matters by making it older than the last one, so age only ever climbs ("older than Thassilon, older than the mountain, older than the oldest of the old things that predate the concept of old"), and the blueprint should declare a fixed ladder of named eras so a thing IS of an era instead of being "older". **Mechanism:** every new scene has to feel bigger than the last, and age is the one dial in the fiction with no stop on it — it costs no invention, contradicts nothing already written, and reads as gravitas, so the model turns that one every time. Same *safety* reflex as the banned ledger habit (DEFAULT_RULES "NEVER ADMINISTRATIVE", data.js:110): clerical detail is safe because it is verifiable, unbounded antiquity is safe because it is unfalsifiable. **Why a bare ceiling is not enough (owner ruled past it):** a wall at the anchor gets climbed in three scenes and then strained against ("nearly as old as Thassilon, and in some ways older"), or the laziness relocates to the next free dial — more cursed, more forbidden, deeper. Removing an escape hatch without supplying a destination just moves the vagueness. **Ruled shape — era LADDER, both consumers:** ① the blueprint declares 3-4 NAMED eras with the anchor on top (Runelords: Thassilon, fell ~10,000 years ago → the Chelish colonial layer → living memory and Sandpoint's founding), turning age from an unbounded axis into a closed ENUM: "older than Thassilon" has no rung to land on, so the GM must pick a rung, and picking is information; ② `campaign_generator.js` also GENERATES eras for freeform starts (the #59 two-consumer pattern), so the failure mode is not left live wherever no author wrote a ceiling; ③ a companion clause redirecting escalation to proximity and consequence (it is closer, it is awake, it knows your name, it costs something now) so the enum is a destination and not merely a wall. **Already half-authored:** samples/rise_of_the_runelords.blueprint names Thassilon 24 times and its premise already pins "fell ten thousand years ago" — the ceiling exists in prose and is enforced nowhere. **Placement:** campaign-CONSTANT text, so unlike buildSkeletonBlock (which carries arc lifecycle state and must stay volatile) this is a STABLE-half candidate beside buildNamingClause — cached at 0.1x on every re-read, in the region that carries authority; per-campaign variation is already precedented there by the tone and adult blocks. **Back-compat:** ""-clean when the field is absent, so the five sample blueprints, freeform campaigns, and every legacy save produce a byte-identical prompt. **Testability splits, and say so honestly:** the pure block builder is engine-testable (stable-half byte-identity, ""-clean-when-absent, legacy blueprints unchanged); whether the ratchet actually STOPS is playtest-audit judging only, alongside the existing prose-length dimension. **Touches:** blueprint schema + blueprint-designer.html, campaign_generator.js, api.js buildSysPrompt. **Possible sibling, NOT ruled:** a STYLE clause for escalation *in place* below the ceiling (an intensifier is not information — either spend one committing detail or say it plainly and move on), deliberately held back because "be specific about ancient things" naively applied turns the GM into a lore-dump engine whose inventions the memory tiers then file as canon.
