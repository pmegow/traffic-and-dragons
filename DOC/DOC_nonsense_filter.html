Extracting meaning from nonsense and "sense-adjacent" sources — research findings and how they apply to Traffic and Dragons

/fable-review subject pass · 2026-08-06 · TODO #77 widened by the user beyond STT to a general nonsense filter. Evidence gathered by four parallel briefs (A: input-path inventory · B: external research · C: #77 design dossier · D: house tolerant-parser census); load-bearing probe results re-verified by hand against the live engine at v1.546. Findings only — no code shipped from this pass.

TLDR: The research is unusually unanimous: every published technique that works on garbled text is a form of restriction — restrict the candidate vocabulary (phonetic shortlist), restrict the spans touched (verify before correcting), or restrict when you dare act (confidence bands: act silently when sure, confirm only in the suspicion band, reject below it). Our sttCorrectNames already IS the validated shortlist pattern — but a live probe shows its missing piece is exactly the piece the literature says matters: with no confidence gate, it altered ordinary, correct English at a 47–60% rate on synthetic rosters ("there is"→Daeris, "sets"→Zethran), and in Car Mode those corruptions auto-send with zero human glance. Meanwhile the confidence signal the #77 gate needs is free on the model we already ship (include:["logprobs"] on gpt-4o-mini-transcribe) — we just never ask for it. The path: capture confidence → gate the corrector and the read-back on it → extend the same "restricted extraction" pattern to the typed path and the GM prompt. An LLM proofread layer is affordable (~$0.0004/utterance) but is the LAST layer, not the first.

1b · The one law everything obeys: restrict, or make it worse

The clearest quantified result in the sweep (EDM 2026, GPT-4o-mini as corrector): a corrector handed a phonetically pre-filtered, type-matched candidate shortlist cut named-entity WER 32.3%→22.7% while leaving correct text untouched (non-NE WER flat at 7.0%). The SAME corrector handed the full context did worse than no correction at all (43.4% — hallucination). Every published mitigation is a restriction: candidate-set restriction (their Double-Metaphone + entity-type filter), span restriction (detect → verify → correct, arXiv 2505.24347), or output-grammar restriction (constrained decoding; over an API, that means structured outputs / function-calling with enums drawn from the live roster). No source recommends unconditional LLM rewriting. The game-domain paper (arXiv 2509.23630 — RAG over game vocabulary + confidence gate for character/location names) is our exact problem shape.

1c · When to ask the player: the three-band consensus

High confidenceAct silently (implicit confirmation at most — restate the result in the response)VUI practice (85%/40% bands — practitioner heuristic, not empirical law); Alexa design docs draw the same line by STAKES ("protecting against… game progress lost"); parser-IF tradition (Inform's "Does the player mean" rulebook; Zarf 2024: "the author mostly shouldn't have to think about disambig")
Suspicion bandExplicit confirm — "I heard X — send it?"

The fatigue finding: people fail to read routine confirmations over 50% of the time — over-confirmation degrades into a rubber stamp. Brief confirmations are strongly preferred (median 1.13s vs 4.03s responses, CHI 2022). No study establishes a quantified irritation threshold (UNDETERMINED) — but every tradition converges on: ask only when suspicion is earned. This independently validates §5's confidence-gated design and the #77 row's own "needs to be played a bit" worry.

1e · Typed-text noise (the non-STT half of the widened scope)

  • Transfers to ES5/no-deps as pure code + a static table: Levenshtein/Damerau, keyboard-adjacency-weighted edit costs (a 26-key coordinate table). SymSpell's 1000×+ speedups are scale optimizations — irrelevant at roster size (~10–50 names).
  • The hard class is autocorrect-induced REAL-WORD errors ("computer flies" for "computer files") — provably undetectable by any dictionary/lexical method, because the observed token IS a valid word. Detection requires context: an LM, n-gram statistics, or a domain prior ("a roster name was expected here"). This is the typed-path twin of the "physics"→Frizwick problem, and the same restricted-shortlist machinery applies.

2 · What stands in our code today (Briefs A + C — internal evidence)

2a · The inventory: one net, twelve doors

Twelve player-text entry points reach the engine (typed action, Table Talk, STT native, STT cloud, Car Mode voice, suggestion taps, Sync modal fields, char-creation prose, custom rules, custom tone, .tnd/.char imports). Exactly ONE content transform exists in the whole pipeline — sttCorrectNames — and it runs only inside stt.js. The typed path's entire validation is if(!txt)return; (game.js:1354). The GM prompt contains zero instruction about incoherent input — the nearest rule (PLAYER ACTIONS ARE INTENT, data.js:124) pushes the opposite way: adjudicate what was typed, don't question it. Two inventory findings worth flagging on their own:

  • Custom narrative rules are player prose injected into the CACHED STABLE prompt half (ui-modals.js:44 → getRulesBlock) with no validation — out of #77's scope but noted for the record.
  • A garbage turn is irreversible in three stores at once — transcript (sacred, append-only), sessionLog (feeds summarize → permanent memory), turn counter — plus RAG can re-serve the player's nonsense verbatim as a past-scene excerpt later. One wasted turn costs two API calls (the turn + the actions call).

2b · The correction net: catches its class, and bites clean speech

sttCorrectNames (helpers.js:631) is the literature's validated shape — phonetic-skeleton shortlist, first-sound gate, two-layer distance, 374-word common-word stoplist, tie-refusal ("never guess between people"). Probed against the live engine (Brief A; the load-bearing rows re-run by hand at v1.546):

ClassResult
The designed garble class ("physics"→Frizwick, "more when"→Morwen, "sand point"→Sandpoint, "dairies"→Daeris)CAUGHT — all field examples repair correctly
The #77 row's structural example ("Shalelu Korunn symbol" ← "Show Corynn the symbol")PARTIAL — Korunn→Corynn repairs; the swallowed verb/article are structurally unrecoverable by token-aligned substitution (can merge two tokens into a name; can never delete, insert, or reorder)

Hand-re-verified ✓. Field rate UNDETERMINED (synthetic corpus) — but the mechanism is exact and the trigger conditions are common words.

The structural tension this exposes: catching "physics"→Frizwick REQUIRES substituting valid English words — which is exactly the operation that corrupts clean sentences. No stoplist can resolve this (it would need to contain English). The discriminator the literature prescribes is confidence + context: only correct aggressively when the transcriber itself was unsure, or when the token sits where a name is expected. That is precisely the #77 gate — which means the gate is not just a read-back UX feature; it is the missing half of the correction net we already shipped.

2c · The confidence gap and the zero-glance surface

  • Zero confidence signal is captured on either path today. Cloud: the request sends only file/model/prompt — no response_format, no include; only json.text is read (stt.js:503,518-522). Native: maxAlternatives=1, confidence never read (stt.js:71,91-92). Every downstream decision runs on the bare string.
  • The zero-glance surface, precisely: Car Mode forces auto-send regardless of the pref (autoOn = isAutoSend() || carModeOn, stt.js) — the only checks between transcript and sendAction are length ≥ 3 and not-a-#78-command. Android/Chrome Car Mode is a fully hands-off loop (auto-listen default ON); iOS Car Mode is tap-to-start but auto-sends on 1.5s silence — v1.536's endpointing made it MORE hands-off. Desktop with the auto-send pref on has no glance and no length gate.
  • §4 shipped status (v1.536): bias prompt ✓, model swap + fallback ✓, VAD endpointing ✓, mic telemetry ✓ — but the getUserMedia constraints audit (§4d2) did NOT ship ({audio:true} bare), and no §4 item captured confidence.
  • No measurement channel exists. §5's own instruction is "ship §4, measure, then tune" — but sttCorrectNames corrections are console-only (nothing persists), the garble corpus on record is tiny (one structural example, anecdotal), and the #16 crumbs record mic settings, never transcripts. The "measure" step has nothing to read.
  • #78 grammar collisions for the confirm flow: spoken yes/no doesn't exist in parseCarCommand; "again" already means "re-read the options menu"; a spoken "no" (2 chars) would be eaten by the Car Mode <3-char gate unless the confirm hook sits ABOVE the gates (the same ordering lesson #78 learned, stt.js:206-209).

The GM model's own output is the app's OTHER nonsense-adjacent source, and the codebase already contains ~17 tolerant parsers (loosened numeric tags, duration parsing, JSON repair with named-refusal gates, empty-slot NPC parsing, truncated-tag amputation) and ~18 name-resolution mechanisms (alias resolution, variant scanning, foe matching, capability/class/skill lookups), built one incident at a time. 4 · Application to Traffic and Dragons — the layered design (verdict)

Every finding assembles into one architecture: layers of restriction, with confidence deciding how far down the stack an utterance travels. This is simultaneously what the literature prescribes (§1b/§1c) and what the codebase already practices on the GM-output side (§3) — the design extends the house pattern to the player side rather than inventing anything.
