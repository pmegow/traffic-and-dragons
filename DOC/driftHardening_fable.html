Drift Hardening — the t1667 five-lane field audit

Filed 2026-08-11 · engine v1.595 · subject: the live Rise of the Runelords save at t1667 (3,331 transcript entries, 42 roster NPCs, 71 map nodes, 10 in-play days) · method: five parallel Opus investigators on sealed lanes (NPC identity · geography & presence · time/quests/schedules · sheets & possessions · transcript-vs-state forensics), every load-bearing claim re-verified against the save by Fable before filing · the user's prompt: "there's some nasty drift going on — track it down"

0. Executive verdict

The drift is real, and it is not one bug. Thirty-two adjudicated findings cluster into four systemic classes, and the most important discovery of the audit is not any single rotten record — it is that two of the guards shipped this very week have verified blind spots at their input boundary, and one incident this morning was mis-diagnosed because of one of them. The guard code was tested; the recognizer feeding it was not, and hostile real prose walked straight through.

The meta-finding. The house discipline says "no guard is trusted until sabotage proves it" — and every sabotage run to date has proven the plumbing: latches, cooldowns, note delivery, contract clauses. What it has never done is feed the recognizer hostile real prose. #158's sentence splitter orphans closing quotes onto the next sentence and then rejects it as speech; #61's weighty-bond regex matches four English words that contain its alternatives as substrings; #137's stay-behind detector reads a foot resting on a calf as a party separation, and missed the one real separation it existed for (t1591). Three different guards, one failure shape: the recognizer is the untested half. Section 5 proposes making recognizer fixtures — drawn from real campaign prose — a standing requirement beside sabotage.

1. Class A — shipped guards with blind recognizers

A1 · critical fable-verified WEIGHTY_REL_RE is unanchored — "Owed" is weighty, the #61 guard never armed, and a false ★ defining moment landed on all four sheets

WEIGHTY_REL_RE = /(married|wed(ded)?|wife|husband|…)/i (globals.js:22) has no word boundaries. Executed against the t1666 overwrite descriptors: "Owed a favor — reckless theft…" matches via the substring "wed"; so does Frizwick's mirrored "Owed favor collected, warming". Probes show the class is broad: midwife (via "wife"), loathes (via "oath"), even showed all test weighty.

Two independent consequences, both verified in the save:

  • The #61 downgrade guard never armed. Its condition is WEIGHTY(prev) && !WEIGHTY(next) — and "Owed a favor" false-tested weighty, so !WEIGHTY(next) was false. No queue entry, no toast, no nudge. This overturns the same-day #167 diagnosis, which concluded from an empty relDowngrades that the nudge had fired once and been ignored. It had never fired. The #167 work (persistence, pre-filled restore tag) is sound machinery and stays — but it cannot fire on this class either until the regex is word-bounded. The corrected mechanism is recorded on the #167 row. Methodology lesson, stated plainly: the regex was eyeballed against the descriptor during the morning diagnosis instead of executed. The audit executed it.
  • A false permanent memory. detectCoreMoments uses the same regex to decide bond-weight, so t1666 minted 'The bond between Ammut and Frizwick became "Owed a favor — reckless theft, for the fun of it"' as a ★ defining moment, fanned witnessed-by-all onto Ammut, Frizwick, Daeris and Morwen — five entries after the genuine "Wife — beloved family, reunited" moment (t1661). It re-injects under DEFINING MOMENTS every turn, and no tag can remove a core memory.

Fix: word-bound every alternative (\bwed(ded)?\b, \bwife\b, \boath\b…), with a failing-first fixture on the literal "Owed a favor" string; then hand-purge the t1666 coreMemories entry from all four sheets (console — no tag exists) and re-verify the #167 persistence path can now arm on this exact overwrite.

A2 · critical fable-verified The #158 phase-mismatch detector cannot see narration that follows dialogue — including t1605, the case it was built for

clockPhaseAssertion splits prose with /[^.!?]+[.!?]*/g. A sentence ending …lly is." breaks at the period inside the closing quote, so the orphaned " leads the next split — which is pure narration — and the very next rule (any quote in sentence = speech territory) rejects it. In this campaign's house style (dialogue block, then a scene-setting narration paragraph), the sentence after dialogue is exactly where the GM writes its time cue. Verified at string level: the t1605 sentence handed to the quote test is literally "\n\nDusk catches you on the descent…", and the shipped detector returns null on the flagship response. 24 phase recognitions survive over 367 recent GM turns before band-filtering; the investigator's instrumented counterfactual (trimming a leading orphan quote inside the splitter, parity preserved) roughly quintuples post-band arms.

Fix: keep a closing quote attached to the sentence it terminates (split on /[^.!?]+[.!?]+["”]*/-style) or trim a leading /^[\s"”]+/ per sentence while preserving the parity count; freeze t1605's exact text as a regression fixture; then re-run the dev/clock-phase-audit.js precision gate, expecting new true positives and at least one new false-positive class (t1643's future-reference "escape before dawn finds you" needs a \bbefore\b rejection in the same commit). The v1.584 enable gate's "1 alert = the true positive" precision was partly precision-by-blindness.

A3 · major The #137 stay-behind recognizer: one false positive that leaked, one true separation it missed by 39 turns

Both directions failed in one week of play. False positive: t1665's "Frizwick's foot … stays there" armed the ping (the possessive is invisible to the window regex), producing the t1666 note the GM then answered aloud. False negative: the one real unrecorded separation — Frizwick sent ahead alone at t1590-91 ("Then she's gone…") — matched nothing, so the engine asserted her presence for ~39 turns until the player corrected it out-of-character at t1593 and a split tag finally landed at t1630. Fix: skip possessive mentions (Name's), add send-ahead/farewell shapes to the trigger set, and let an OOC player correction ("X is not with us") arm the ping directly.

A4 · major CONSUMABLE_RE is a drifting second copy of the item bible — five phantom Alchemist's fire flasks prove it

The GM's own canon closes the stock at t1351 ("That's it, that's the last of it"); the sheet still reads Alchemist's fire x5. The #60 ghost-consumable nudge never fired because CONSUMABLE_RE has no fire alternative — while ITEM_BIBLE["alchemist's fire"] already says category:"consumable", uses:"single use — thrown". The authoritative answer exists; the detector consults a hand-rolled copy instead. Fix: make itemLookup(base).category==="consumable" the primary leg with the regex as fallback — one authority, per the #81/#157 TYPE rulings. (The blasting-charge stack — ceiling 14, ≥5 narrated detonations, one decrement — is the other half of #60's loop failing: the nudge fired and was ignored turn after turn; see D-class.)

2. Class B — untagged narration with no detector on its axis

B1 · critical fable-verified Jorgenfist: 57 consecutive turns, nine named interiors, zero location tags — the climax of the campaign is filed on a ridge outside

From t1593 to t1649 every transcript entry is stamped e.l="Fogscar Mountains - Ridge Line". The descent, the breach, the giants' hall, the burial chamber, the forge, the silk colony, and Mokmurian's ritual chamber — where the party killed the act's villain — produced no [LOCATION:] and (one t1623 exception) no [SUBLOCATION:]. The blueprint-seeded Jorgenfist node still reads firstVisit:null, visits:0; Mokmurian has no lastSeenAt; no stateNote records his death or the library fire; no edge connects the Kodars to anywhere; and RAG keys 57 turns of fortress scenes to the ridge, so locSame will happily serve the death chamber as ridge history. The same window filed taverns and bathhouses correctly — the GM simply stops tagging position when the story goes underground, because nothing ever asks. Aggravating: prose calls the range "the Kodars" and "the Fogscars" interchangeably while the seeded node says "Iron Peaks" — three names, no filed visit to reconcile them.

Fix (engine): a location-filing nudge in the buildArcStagingNudge mold — N committed turns (≈8) with neither location tag while clean prose names a new enclosed place (chamber/hall/tunnel/passage/vault) arms a one-shot GM-decides note. Never auto-file. Fix (data): file the Jorgenfist visit, reparent/describe the interiors worth keeping, stamp the two stateNotes, via map_cleanup + GM: lines.

B2 · major The travel axis has no teeth: "Sandpoint still two days south" delivered in 205 clock-minutes, and a live deadline is being held open by the shortfall

The GM's own committed narration priced the return leg at two days (t1655); the arrival landed three turns later on a +180m advance. The player challenged it in-fiction at t1658 ("how did we not even glimpse the giant army?"); the GM rationalized the route and moved nothing. The outbound leg shows the same ~5× compression. This is invisible to every clock guard by construction — #142 and #158 both key on phase, and every phase word in the window sits in-band; the failure is elapsed distance. Materially: the Collector's warehouse deadline (dueMin:11235 vs clock 10505) survives on 730 minutes of headroom that an honestly-charged journey would have blown by ~2,675 minutes. Fix: a day-count assertion detector — committed prose matching \bN days?\b-class journey pricing within K turns of an arrival [LOCATION:] whose cumulative advance is under a day arms the one-shot "emit [TIME_ADVANCE:Nd] or correct the reference" fork. Same never-auto-advance discipline as #158.

B3 · major Dated commitments evaporate: a 1,200-gold blood-sealed commission exists in zero stores, and futureEvents expire in turns while promises are made in days

Wyla Ashvane's warded-rings deal — "Twelve hundred gold… Call it five days", blood taken, re-confirmed at t1562 — produced no [QUEST:], [FUTURE_EVENT:], [SCHEDULE:], or even an [NPC_NOTE:]; Wyla's record has events:[]. Shalelu's "map lands tomorrow" likewise. Nothing in the prompt stack can ever raise either thread again. The one store that did catch a commitment (the Collector schedule entry) is the exception that proves the design: [SCHEDULE:] works when used — the GM just doesn't volunteer it. Compounding: FUTURE_EXPIRE_TURNS=40 is a turn budget, and this campaign's measured pacing makes 40 turns ≈ 14.6 in-fiction hours — so any promise on a multi-day horizon that is filed as a futureEvent is guaranteed to age out silently (the rings did exactly that in the archive; two other clock-horizon threads followed). Meanwhile two already-resolved events (the black-water shape, resolved on screen four turns after filing; the bathhouse trip, happened at t1664) survived ten summarize passes un-echoed. Fixes: ① a dated-commitment nudge — currency-or-obligation + delivery-interval in one committed sentence with no lifecycle tag in the response arms the one-shot "file it or say it was only talk"; ② clock-aware expiry — when when parses to a duration, stamp setMin and expire on the clock, or promote to schedule at file time (#150's machinery, run earlier); ③ a deterministic resolve-assist — score pending events' feTokens against each fresh chapter summary and surface strong overlaps for [FUTURE_EVENT_RESOLVED:].

B4 · minor world.time is a frozen free-text twin of the clock

ws.world.time has read "dawn" since t1652 while the clock reads Day 8, 1:05 pm — and both are injected into the same prompt ("Time: dawn" beside "CAMPAIGN CLOCK: Day 8, 07h05m"). 5.3 Process — the standing rule this audit earns

Recognizer fixtures beside sabotage. Every guard that begins with a recognizer (regex, splitter, window-match) gets a fixture file of real campaign prose — the hostile cases: dialogue-adjacent narration, possessives, substring traps ("Owed", "midwife", "showed"), orphaned quotes — and the failing-first test runs the recognizer over them, not a hand-seeded queue. Sabotage proves the plumbing; fixtures prove the eyes. Three of this audit's four criticals were recognizer failures in guarded, sabotage-proven code.
