Title
The key, seal states and promised ending procedures are undefined
Kind
content / causal rules
Severity
high
Basis
5.3 (DMG preface A / Dragon B:26, primary); 7.2 (EN World B:29, secondary).
Blueprint evidence
  • premise: "bargain with anyone carrying the key"
  • acts[0].turningPoint: "The reliquary cracks during an ambush"
  • acts[1].turningPoint: "a rite that partially wakes the Coffered God"
  • acts[2].turningPoint: "reseal the Coffered God, deliver it to whichever faction the company has thrown in with, or break it open themselves"
  • npcs[4].notes: "speaks in fragments through cracks, dreams, and borrowed mouths"
Reason
The premise introduces a key without assigning its custody or function. Cracking, partial waking, resealing and opening are distinct story milestones but have no defined triggers, limits or achievable procedures. The narrator must invent whether a precaution or attempted ending works and can change the box's powers to force the next milestone. This is missing operational information, not a demand to identify the entity's cosmic nature.
Recommendation
Add a compact common rule for intact/cracked/open states, the key's initial custody and exact use, what contact or possession permits, and at least one feasible resealing method with discoverable prerequisites and price. State the immediate practical difference between resealing, delivering an intact or damaged box, opening it and abandoning the job; keep later politics uncertain.
Mitigation
Ren knows sealing fragments, Sister Ash-Tongue has read sealing texts, and Two Thrones has actual reliquary vaults. These are credible existing anchors for the procedures. The final menu already offers multiple outcomes; it needs workings rather than additional endings.
Confidence
High

Limits and dissent

Do not turn the salt crossing into a detailed simulation or require a taxonomy for the God. No observation of travel resource tracking, import behavior or generated adjudication is claimed.

Title
All magic can receive an undefined permanent surcharge after use
Kind
content / playable cost boundary
Severity
high
Basis
1.2 and 1.3 (S:action-roll / S:setting-position-effect, primary); 1.7 (S:action-roll, primary); 7.4 (LF, primary).
Blueprint evidence
  • rules[0]: "Sorcery in this world always extracts a cost paid in the caster's body, memory, or years"
  • rules[0]: "the GM should make that cost concrete and immediate whenever magic is used."
  • npcs[5].notes: "magic lets her read weather and water in the Flats at a cost paid in her own memories"
Reason
The campaign explicitly mandates an extra-seeming body, memory or lifespan payment on every magic use without defining whether ordinary spell-resource costs count, which effects require a further payment, how large it is or when the player learns it. A familiar healing or utility action can therefore acquire an invented irreversible loss after the player has acted. Ren demonstrates the intended harsh texture but does not establish playable boundaries for anyone else.
Recommendation
Define the scope of the sorcery price relative to normal character abilities, plus a small consistent method for pricing it. State the concrete loss or bounded risk before a player commits, allow them to decline, and record the paid cost consistently. If ordinary magic also exacts a bodily price, make that rule explicit from entry rather than silently exempting it. Memory loss should identify the agreed memory, not authorize retroactive identity rewriting.
Mitigation
The price is deliberate genre design, and the text does require a concrete immediate consequence. Preserve that cost and Ren's memory erosion; the correction is scope, amount and disclosure rather than free sorcery.
Confidence
High

Reason
