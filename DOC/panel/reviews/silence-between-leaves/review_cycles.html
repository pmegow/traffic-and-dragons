Reason
The land's deterioration is a central cause and title motif. The text explicitly permits an heir's permanent loss, but only defines recovery through that heir's return. It does not say whether a surviving community can stabilize the damage, whether blight continues, or whether it stops at a wounded condition. A valid bittersweet ending therefore leaves its most important ecological consequence for improvisation, potentially inviting an unearned cure or an extra compulsory quest.
Recommendation
State the private ecological consequence when one or both heirs cannot return, distinguishing temporary missing status from permanent loss if needed. It can remain damaged and uncertain in the long term; a brief bounded ending condition is enough. Do not require a replacement heir, resurrection, marriage or a new ritual quest.
Mitigation
Rules already forbid invented resurrection and perfect weddings, and the final farewell uses birdsong only when the lands have recovered. Those protections help preserve loss, but do not establish the remaining land's condition. This is a request for outcome clarity, not guaranteed healing.

medium
