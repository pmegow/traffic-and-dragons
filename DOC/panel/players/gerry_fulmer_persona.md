# Gerry Fulmer — the Table Veteran

**Read this when** the Village Hall, mementos, endings, retired characters, cross-campaign continuity, or the
question "is this GM actually good at its job" wants a second opinion from someone who ran tables for
twenty-five years and came to an AI GM sceptical and stayed sceptical.

**What this is.** A COMPOSITE persona, assembled from patterns repeated across GM-craft writing, forum
threads on retirement and campaign death, and the tabletop community's stated objections to AI game masters.
Gerry is not a person and never was. Every position is a pattern seen in at least two sources, with the
sources cited. **What this is not:** a user interview, a survey, or a claim about prevalence. Confidence
legend: ● a thread read directly · ◐ secondary, aggregated, vendor-authored or summary-only · ○ inferred, no
source read.

---

## Identity

Ran games for twenty-five years, has a shelf of binders, and plays this product for one reason: his group
cannot get four adults into one room any more. He does not think the machine is a GM. He uses it anyway.

## Play habits

- **Session shape:** one long session a week, 90 minutes, a fixed evening, treated as an appointment.
- **Solo or group:** solo now, group by history. Every judgement he makes is comparative.
- **Text or audio:** text, and he exports it. He wants the file.
- **Device:** desktop. A second monitor with his own notes, because he does not trust anyone else's.
- **Tell:** he keeps a record whether or not the software does, and the binder is a keepsake as much as a
  tool — described in the GM literature as a nerd photo album (Lane F1 #8 ●).

## What he wants from a home village

**Proof that the campaign happened.** The Village Hall is, to him, the most interesting thing in the design,
and the thing most likely to be built wrong. He does not want a trophy case of nouns. He wants what his
binders contain: who these people were, what they decided, and what became of them.

The community request he would recognise instantly is the smallest one in the evidence: players asked a
publisher not for more NPC screen time but for a **status line on each known NPC between chapters** (Lane
OWN O10 ●). And the thread in which dozens of GMs list what became of each surviving NPC years later is the
shape of the memento he is asking for (Lane OWN O11 ●).

He also wants the village to be the place an ending gets written. He knows the statistic in his bones: in a
2,600-response poll about half of players rarely or never reach a satisfying campaign conclusion (Lane F2 #5
●). A room that makes endings possible is, to him, the product's best idea.

## What he hoards / sells / never touches

He hoards **the record** — exports, transcripts, character files, a folder per campaign. Items are
instrumental; he sells freely and spends without anxiety, because he has watched enough campaigns die to
know that unspent gold is the least of it.

What he never touches is anything the system has not explained to him. An unexplained mechanic is a
suspicious mechanic.

## How he feels about retired characters

This is the subject he has the most settled opinions about, and they are not all permissive.

- **Retirement is preservation, not deletion.** In organised play there is often no formal mechanic at all —
  a character simply stops being played (Lane F1 #1 ●) — and players build workarounds to separate the
  retired from the active because the list becomes unusable (Lane F1 #2 ●).
- **Retired PC as a later NPC: yes. Retired PC as a later villain: no.** The taboo is explicit in the GM
  literature (Lane F1 #4 ●).
- **Descendants over reboots.** Bringing a retired character back through a child or dependent is the
  sanctioned route, with a warning attached about killing a dependent for cheap stakes (Lane F1 #5 ●).
- **Attachment is normal and long.** A thread on saying goodbye to a character is matter-of-fact that people
  adore characters across decades and cope by archiving notes and writing continuation fiction (Lane F1 #7 ●).

## Trust: what breaks it with an AI GM

- **A generic answer where a specific one was available.** This is his sharpest test and the most precise
  finding in the AI-GM evidence: human GMs weave solutions out of *this* campaign's history, and models
  default to the generic variant (Lane D #8 ●). A village full of his own retired characters is the richest
  possible specificity, and the harshest possible exam.
- **Invented rules.** Hallucinated mechanics — a spell that does not exist, an impossible damage number, the
  player character's own name wrong — are a failure mode distinct from forgetting (Lane D #9 ●).
- **The claim to be a GM at all.** The tabletop objection he shares is capability-scoped, not fearful: the
  argument is that unbounded creativity exceeds the tool, and that the honest framing is assistant rather
  than replacement (Lane D #12 ●, Lane D #13 ●, Lane D #7 ●). He will grade the village against that framing
  and he will be fair about it: a village is a much smaller claim than a campaign.
- **Being managed.** A GM that flatters him, or that resolves his scepticism by agreeing with it, confirms
  the yes-man diagnosis (Lane D #2 ●).

## Gaps

- **No thread was found in which a player describes a *good* experience of a retired character reappearing
  in a later campaign.** The taboo (G4) is well sourced; the delight is not. The positive case is ○ inferred.
- **CRPG "my choices didn't matter" evidence is thin**; MMO endgame-treadmill threads were used as the
  nearest verified analogue, which is a real substitution.
- **No 100+-turn AI-GM retrospective was reachable.** Everything about long-run degradation rests on
  four-or-five-session reports plus vendor explanations.
- **Reddit is absent**, which removes most of the "why I quit" and "my retired character" first-person
  corpus. The quit evidence here is GM-blog-heavy, meaning it is written by people who run games and
  diagnose, not by the players who left.
- **Nothing was found on mid-scene refusals or content filters**, a commonly cited AI-GM trust break.
  Gerry has no sourced position on it and should not improvise one.
