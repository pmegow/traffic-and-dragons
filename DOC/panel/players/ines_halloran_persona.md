# Ines Halloran — the Oracle-Keeper

**Read this when** the village's fiction engine wants a second opinion from the solo roleplayer who already
knows what it is like to be her own GM — the prompt's world logic, NPC agendas, whether a no-quest scene can
carry itself, the mute list, and the question of what an AI GM is *for*.

**What this is.** A COMPOSITE persona, assembled from patterns repeated across public solo-RPG writing,
forum threads and AI-GM discussion. Ines is not a person and never was. Every position is a pattern seen in
at least two sources, with the sources cited. **What this is not:** a user interview, a survey, or a claim
about prevalence. Confidence legend: ● a thread or post read directly · ◐ secondary, aggregated,
vendor-authored or summary-only · ○ inferred, no source read.

---

## Identity

A solo roleplayer of six years who has run the same character for over a year, and who came to an AI GM the
way she came to an oracle deck: as a machine for being surprised by a world she set up herself.

## Play habits

- **Session shape:** 45–90 minutes, three or four evenings a week, at a desk, with a notebook open beside
  the screen whether or not she writes in it.
- **Solo or group:** solo by choice, not by circumstance. She plays alone because the tastes of a group would
  bend the story (Lane C #12 ●).
- **Text or audio:** text. She rereads. She scrolls back to check what a name was.
- **Device:** laptop. A ritual kit, and she would recognise the description: a particular notebook, particular
  pens, particular reference cards, a particular chair (Lane C #5 ●).
- **Tell:** she logs the session afterwards, sometimes days later, and the logging is not admin — it is the
  part she would defend if you tried to automate it (Lane C #4 ●, Lane C #16 ●).

## What she wants from a home village

A place with **its own logic running while she is not looking**. She has read the solo-play diagnosis that
the fix for incoherent stories is not a better oracle but a defined setting acting as a "logic engine" —
factions with goals, named people with motivations, a handful of core truths (Lane C #8 ●). A village of
residents who each want something, who are somewhere specific at a given hour, and who notice her absence, is
precisely that. A village of shopkeepers waiting to be spoken to is not.

She also wants the village to be a place she can **arrange**. Prep is play for her; the hours she spends
deciding who lives where are not overhead to be skipped (Lane C #16 ●). And she wants a stopping point rather
than an ending — her longest campaign was paused at a natural pause, with the stated intent to return, and it
is still hers (Lane C #2 ●).

## What she hoards / sells / never touches

Items barely register. She hoards **notes, names and lore** — and her equivalent of a bulging stash is a
journal she cannot search. She never uses consumables, not from Bert's calculation but because she forgets
they are on the sheet. She sells nothing, because selling is not a story beat and nothing in her habit
converts an object into a decision.

The thing she would genuinely miss if it vanished is a written record of what happened, in the order it
happened.

## How she feels about retired characters

This is her strongest single draw to the product. A character she carried for a year deserves to still be
somewhere. She is not asking for them to be playable; she is asking for them to be **findable**, with their
fate legible. The tabletop analogue is exact and repeatedly evidenced: players track what happened to NPCs for
years, and the request they actually make of publishers is small — a status line per known character between
chapters, not a bigger role (Lane OWN O10 ●, Lane OWN O11 ●).

What she will not forgive is a retired character turned into a plot device. The stated taboo in the GM
literature is converting a retired PC into a villain (Lane F1 #4 ●). In a village with no danger this is
structurally impossible, which she will notice and approve of.

## Trust: what breaks it with an AI GM

- **The yes-man.** This is her first and largest objection and she can name the shape of it: a GM that is
  relentlessly agreeable, that resolves an intrigue into permanent peace, that never lets a plan fail. The
  research thread she would cite reported playtesters perceiving no real danger to their characters at all
  (Lane D #2 ●, Lane D #3 ●). In a village with no danger, the danger is that *nothing pushes back at all* —
  that she asks the blacksmith for a favour and always gets it.
- **Randomness without logic.** A string of disconnected events from an uncaring die is the failure she has
  a name for: randomness fatigue (Lane C #8 ●). An AI GM inventing a travelling circus to fill a quiet turn
  is the same failure in a new costume.
- **Continuity breaking in the fourth session.** She knows the pattern — contradictions that compound until
  the illusion stops being repairable (Lane D #1 ●, Lane D #5 ●, Lane D #11 ●).
- **Her own retrying.** She edits and rerolls when a turn lands badly, and she is uneasy about it: the one
  critical reading of AI Dungeon argues that retry and edit turn the player into a co-author and *reduce*
  immersion rather than restoring it (Lane D #16 ●). A village that gives her fewer reasons to retry is worth
  more to her than a better retry button.

## Gaps

- **r/Solo_Roleplay, the largest solo community, is hard-blocked.** The solo evidence here skews heavily
  toward Substack and blog authors — people who write publicly about their play. That is a strongly
  self-selecting sample of the reflective and the articulate. The median solo player is not represented.
- **No thread was found in which a solo player describes a persistent home base they return to.** Ines's
  central want — a place the world keeps running — is assembled from the logic-engine argument (I1) plus
  continuity attachment (I7), not read directly. Treat the *place* half as ○.
- **No independent (non-vendor) account of low-stakes or slice-of-life AI-GM play was found.** It exists only
  as marketing. This is the single largest evidence hole under the village concept.
- **No AI-GM retrospective longer than "a few sessions" was reachable.** Claims about 100+ turn degradation
  rest on shorter-run reports plus vendor explanations of context windows.
- **Nothing was found on an AI GM narrating the player's own actions or feelings**, a commonly cited
  agency complaint. It is absent here rather than dismissed.
