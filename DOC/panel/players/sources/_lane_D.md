## PATTERNS

1. **The "goldfish problem" is the single most cited trust-breaker.** Independently voiced on Hacker News (#1, #5, #6), itch.io (#10), the vendor's own help docs (#17), and named explicitly by aggregator terminology (#18): the AI forgets inventory, HP, dead NPCs, prior favors, and physical details once they fall out of the context window, and campaigns either quietly stop mentioning things or actively contradict them.
2. **"Yes-man" / no-stakes GMing shows up as a named, specific complaint, not vague dissatisfaction** (#2, #3): testers can point to a moment stakes evaporated (a diplomatic crisis resolving to permanent peace; a death being walked back) rather than describing it only as "boring."
3. **The counter-camp's objection is capability-scoped, not fear-of-technology.** Both EN World threads (#12, #13) explicitly reject the "Skynet" framing and instead argue TTRPGs require unbounded creativity that pattern-completion can't match, or that job displacement (not superintelligence) is the real concern.
4. **Practitioners converge on "assistant, not replacement" as the stable resting point** (#7, #8, #12): scoping the LLM to encounter tables, flavor text, NPC voice, and rule lookups while keeping a human (or literal code) in charge of adjudication and specific-solution creativity.
5. **Availability and infinite patience are the delight side's load-bearing claims**, repeated with near-identical language across sources (#4): no scheduling, 2am play, and tone/style customization on demand.
6. **The retry/undo/edit loop is a double-edged design choice.** It's what makes AI GMing usable when output goes wrong (#10, itch.io reviewers regenerate bad lines routinely), but the one independent critical essay found (#16) argues that very convenience is why immersion suffers — it smooths over the AI's failures instead of forcing engagement with them.
7. **Hallucinated mechanics (not just narrative drift) are a separate, mechanical trust problem** (#9): invented spells, impossible damage numbers, and rules that shift to keep the story "fun" rather than staying fixed, distinct from memory loss.
8. **Vendor and community framing of the same failure differ mainly in blame, not description.** Players describe the goldfish problem as a betrayal of the world's persistence; vendors describe it as a solvable architecture problem and pitch a fix — but the underlying behavior described is consistent across both source types (#1, #6, #17, #18).
