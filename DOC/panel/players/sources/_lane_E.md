## PATTERNS

1. **Verbosity/redundancy tolerance is razor-thin.** Repeated control reminders (#3), literal reading of stray punctuation/formatting artifacts (#1), and a game's own voice ignoring the user's preferred speech rate (#4) are all called out as friction, not charm — audio-first users want the minimum necessary restatement, not maximal narration.
2. **Blind/audiogame players are starved for depth, not just access.** The recurring ask across #2, #5, #6 isn't "make something audio-only exists" — it's "make something as rich as a real RPG," with explicit fatigue at shallow "choose-the-link" formats.
3. **Interaction model matters as much as narration quality.** Parser/command-line text beats GUI or choice-link interfaces for screen-reader usability (#5, #6) — the two IF threads independently converge on this.
4. **Voice-per-character has no consensus answer.** Full-cast vs. single-narrator-doing-all-voices is a live, unresolved preference split (#7) — relevant directly to any GM/NPC voice-switching design.
5. **Real-world hands-free voice control still routinely fails.** Misheard commands, forced screen glances to confirm, and proprietary phrasing requirements show up concretely in the car-voice-assistant domain (#9) — the closest available analog to "had to pull over."
6. **The interruption/pause problem is real enough that platforms are now building for it.** Spotify's Recaps feature (#11) is essentially an industry admission that listeners lose narrative continuity after a break, framed in the show's own words as a TV-style "previously on."
7. **One audio stream stepping on another is a distinct, separate annoyance from "losing the plot."** #12 and #8's interrupt/skip feature both point at users wanting explicit control over interruption — either preventing an unwanted interruption (directions cutting into a podcast) or actively invoking one on purpose (skipping a wall of TTS-read ASCII art).
8. **Trust in synthetic voice is conditional on purpose.** The HN thread (#10) shows the blind/screen-reader community drawing a hard line between "good enough to extract information" and "good enough to enjoy a story performance" — cheap AI narration clears the first bar but is resented when it's substituted for the second.
