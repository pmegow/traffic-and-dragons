## PATTERNS

1. **Retirement-as-preservation, not deletion** — across D&D Beyond and Gnome Stew, the recurring framing is that a retired character isn't gone, it's archived/memorialized (folders, binders, continuation fiction, "we keep them as memories") (#F1-2, #F1-7, #F1-8).
2. **Retired PC → NPC is a well-worn, GM-endorsed move, but with one hard boundary** — becoming a patron, shopkeeper, or background legend is welcomed; becoming a villain/nemesis is specifically warned against as likely to cause hard feelings (#F1-4, #F1-7).
3. **Character concept recycling across unrelated campaigns is common and normalized**, provided there's no claimed narrative continuity between the worlds and the GM is informed (#F1-3).
4. **Organized play (PFS/AL) treats "retirement" as an administrative non-event, not a story beat** — no reward, no special status, and same-level replacement is explicitly banned in PFS to protect continuity/pacing (#F1-1, #F1-6).
5. **Generational/legacy play (playing a retired PC's descendant) is a named, deliberate technique**, distinct from a full campaign reboot, used specifically to pull veteran players back in via inherited stakes (#F1-5, #F1-7).
6. **Attachment to old characters is treated as normal and validated, not a quirk to justify** — multiple threads independently reach "this is normal after decades of play" (#F1-7, #F1-8).
7. **Scheduling/logistics is repeatedly named the single largest killer of campaigns**, ranked above narrative or system complaints in both a large-scale poll and multiple threads (#F2-2, #F2-5, #F2-7, #F2-11).
8. **"Fizzling," not a dramatic ending, is described as the modal outcome** for TTRPG campaigns — open-ended systems have no built-in stopping point, so most campaigns just stop rather than conclude (#F2-3, #F2-5, #F2-6).
9. **Losing the narrative thread between sessions (poor continuity/notes) is cited as a first-order cause of campaign death**, not a cosmetic issue — echoing the retirement/attachment theme from the opposite direction: readable continuity is what keeps investment alive (#F2-10).
10. **The MMO "endgame treadmill" complaint is structurally the same complaint TTRPG players make about grind/bookkeeping** — progress that resets or doesn't durably change the world reads as meaningless regardless of medium (#F2-8, #F2-9).
