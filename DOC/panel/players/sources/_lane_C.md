## PATTERNS

1. **Solo campaigns run long, then pause rather than end.** Multi-month to year-plus engagement with one character, closed not by finishing the story but by finding a "stopping point," with an explicit intent to return (#2, #3).
2. **Short, slotted sessions tucked into daily routine.** Coffee, bedtime, gaps between meetings — solo play is repeatedly framed as fitting into found minutes rather than scheduled blocks (#18).
3. **"Not knowing what to do next" is the named killer of solo campaigns**, more than lack of time or interest (#6), and system structure (move lists, procedures) is the cited antidote (#6, #15).
4. **Oracle/"randomness" fatigue is a recognized, named failure mode** — outcomes feel arbitrary and the player is forced to invent justification for every roll — with the fix being world truths established up front, not more/better random tables (#8, #9).
5. **Prep and journaling are reframed as the actual play, not overhead before it.** Worldbuilding, mapmaking, and log-writing are described as legitimate solo-RPG activity in their own right (#16, #12, #4).
6. **A minority but visible ritual/kit culture**: dedicated small-format journals, specific pens, laminated reference cards, particular cafés or outdoor spots that recur across sessions (#5).
7. **What's lost playing solo is consistently the same short list**: a GM who pushes back, genuine surprise, and other players' unpredictable input; oracles and deliberately ambiguous answer-tables are the DIY substitute people build for this (#9, #10, #14, #15).
8. **Cozy, no-combat, low-stakes journaling RPGs are a distinct and marketed subgenre**, explicitly positioned against danger/stakes as calming rather than dull (#17).
9. **Commitment to one system over hopping between new releases is cited by experienced solo players as the single biggest driver of satisfaction and skill** (#3).

## GAPS

- **Reddit (r/Solo_Roleplay), almost certainly the largest solo-RPG community, was hard-blocked for this lane** — every finding here comes from smaller blogs/forums/Substacks whose authors chose to write reflectively, which skews the sample toward committed hobbyist-writers over the median casual/lurking solo player.
- No thread was found that explicitly frames "I have to do all the work myself" as a bare complaint; the closest matches are the more specific "randomness fatigue" (#8) and "don't know what to do next" (#6) framings — the rawer decision-fatigue complaint may live specifically on the blocked Reddit community.
- **No genuine player testimony found on reusing the same character across different solo game systems** — search only surfaced marketing copy (a card-deck product) and an unrelated RPG Maker forum thread; this angle is effectively unconfirmed from public evidence.
- **No thread explicitly described a persistent "home base" place a solo player returns to** (tavern, camp, etc.) as a named pattern; the closest is general continuing-world/continuing-character attachment (#2, #12) — inferred only, not directly evidenced.
- Numeric session-length/frequency data is thin; only #18 gives fetched, dated specifics — most other "session shape" claims came from general search-result snippets rather than a full fetched thread, so they were excluded from the citation table per the strict-citation rule.
- D&D Beyond and odd74 threads (#13, #14, #15, #19) skew toward "where do I find solo content," a weaker fit for habit/ritual questions than the Ironsworn/Mythic-adjacent Substack blogs, which are self-selected reflective writers.
