## PATTERNS

1. **A base becomes valued when it's treated like a character, not a stat block or storage locker** — posters explicitly wanted a home base to "feel like almost another member of the party" worth being angry about losing (#6), and BG3 players' actual complaint was about losing a place they'd invested feeling into (#15).
2. **Persistence failures kill investment fast.** Losing decorated space/items when the game moves you on (#15) and disconnecting/instanced housing that "isn't memorable" (#7) are named directly as what breaks the payoff of building something up.
3. **Downtime left unstructured degenerates into either boredom or admin.** GM threads repeatedly describe "nothing happens, people get bored waiting for their turn" from unbounded shopping/meal/exploration scenes (#4, #11), while too much bookkeeping (marble countertops, kingdom spreadsheets) is the opposite failure (#5, #14).
4. **The fix GMs converge on is a clock, a cost, or a hook — never open-ended freedom.** Time-boxed scenes (#4), an energy/resource economy (#12), and deliberately-hidden downtime length driving real prioritization (#13) all appear independently as the same solution shape.
5. **Displaying/curating what you've collected is a draw on its own, no mechanics required.** Stardew's decorating thread (#16) and BG3's camp-decorating thread (#15) both show unprompted, unrewarded personalization as something players actively pursue and mourn losing.
6. **What makes a hub matter is the people in it, not the building.** EN World repliers converged on NPC relationships and "defacto defenders of the town" status as the actual source of attachment, more than room count or fortification (#6).
7. **A base/stronghold risks siphoning the community it's meant to serve.** The oldest MMO housing thread already named this in 2005 — housing that's "too useful" drains shared hub towns into ghost towns, while housing that's "useless" gets ignored (#7); the 2025 WoW thread shows the same argument still running twenty years later, this time framed as travel/acquisition tedium rather than sprawl (#8).
8. **Players want to build a base up incrementally through their own choices/quests, not receive a finished one.** Buying rules threads keep landing on quest-based or narrative acquisition over a flat price tag (#1, #3), and the EN World OP explicitly wanted room to add walls/library/stables over time (#6).
