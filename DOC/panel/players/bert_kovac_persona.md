# Bert Kovac — the Quartermaster

**Read this when** the village's stashes, shops, prices, wares, inventory permanence, gold sinks or item
canon want a second opinion from the player who treats the inventory screen as the game.

**What this is.** A COMPOSITE persona, assembled from patterns repeated across public forum threads. Bert is
not a person and never was. Nothing below is one poster's view; every position is a pattern seen in at least
two threads, or one thread plus a design source, with the threads cited. **What this is not:** a user
interview, a survey, or a claim about how many players are like this. Confidence legend: ● a forum thread
read directly · ◐ secondary, aggregated, vendor-authored or summary-only · ○ inferred, no source read.

---

## Identity

A methodical accumulator who plays RPGs the way other people keep a workshop: everything sorted, nothing
wasted, nothing spent. He has finished games with two hundred unused potions and considers that a clean run.

## Play habits

- **Session shape:** long, two to three hours, weekends and one weeknight. He does not play in gaps.
- **Solo or group:** solo by preference. He has been in groups; he was always the one who tracked the party
  loot (the elected-treasurer pattern, Lane B #14 ●).
- **Text or audio:** text, always. He reads the item description. Audio would hide the numbers.
- **Device:** desktop, large screen, second window open with a wiki or a spreadsheet.
- **Tell:** the first thing he does on entering a settlement is open every shop's stock list, then sell, then
  leave. The last thing he does before a hard fight is *not* drink the potion.

## What he wants from a home village

A place his stuff is safe and legible. He does not need the house to be pretty; he needs to be able to put
four hundred items somewhere and find one of them again in eight weeks. He wants the shop stock to be worth
reading — a stock list that never changes is a stock list he stops opening after the second visit (Lane A
#8 ●, the vendor-hopping complaint; Lane B #13 ●, the curated-random-stock remedy). He wants somewhere to
*put* gold that is not a number: property, an upgrade, a commission, a standing order at the blacksmith
(Lane OWN O6 ●).

## What he hoards / sells / never touches

- **Hoards:** consumables, above all. The mechanism is documented and he would recognise it in himself:
  scarce plus powerful equals too precious to spend (Lane B #8 ●). He hoards past the point of cost — over
  encumbered, unable to run, still carrying (Lane B #4 ●).
- **Sells:** obvious vendor trash, and only when friction forces it. The trigger to finally sell is almost
  never a plan; it is the inventory getting slow to scroll (Lane B #12 ●).
- **Never touches:** anything with a risk clause attached. A backfire chance is enough to freeze an item
  permanently (Lane B #1 ●).
- **Keeps forever, irrationally:** the one item that grew with him. He wants the sword he has carried since
  turn forty to get better rather than be replaced by a better sword (Lane OWN O8 ●).

## How he feels about retired characters

Retired characters are *inventory* to him before they are people — but that is not cynicism, it is how his
attachment expresses itself. He wants the retired character's gear to still exist, on the retired character,
where he left it. His nightmare is opening an old save and finding the equipment list empty. The community
analogue is exact: a stash that appears to be gone reads as betrayal even when nothing was actually lost, and
only an explicit promise from the developers restored trust (Lane B #11 ●).

## Trust: what breaks it with an AI GM

One thing, mostly: **the GM inventing a change to his stuff.** An item that vanishes, an item that appears, a
count that is wrong, a merchant who sells him something he already owns. He will test it deliberately — leave
a marked item, come back, check. A GM that narrates an object permanence failure has failed at the only thing
he was watching (the "if the farmer is back at his plough, immersion is broken" objection, Lane OWN O1 ●;
the memory complaint as the single most-cited AI-GM grievance, Lane D).

Second: **prices that move without a reason.** He will notice that the same axe cost 12 and then 40, and he
will not read it as inflation; he will read it as the model making things up.

| B4 | Gold starts being spent when it buys identity, not power. | D&D Beyond, "How to get players to spend money?", 2021-02-22 — the fixes posters converge on are appearance, status, property, pets, daily living; the thread's conclusion ties spending to character identity ●. | B8 | Bookkeeping is the thing that kills the activity he likes. Two overlapping economies double it. | Paizo, "Kingmaker and Downtime", 2017-06 — the objection is not boredom but that a second economy destabilises the first and doubles the tracking ●. | B9 | He will not spend what he cannot price. | B10 | Encumbrance and friction are the only reliable forces that make him let go. | Lemmy.world consumables thread, ~Jul 2026 — over-encumbrance does not stop hoarding, it just makes people miserable ●. Paizo, "Dealing with Encumbrance", 2014-08 — every proposed fix removes the constraint rather than carrying less ●. | A permanent house stash removes the last friction he has. Is that a gift or the end of his economy? Worth designing the answer deliberately rather than discovering it. |

## Gaps

- **No direct evidence of sentimental, non-mechanical item attachment from a forum thread** beyond the legacy-item
  discussion (B6). The "I kept it because of where it came from" pattern is widely assumed and I could not
  read a thread that states it plainly. Treat B7's item half as ○ inferred; the *display* half is sourced.
- **"Limited-time offer" as a spending trigger is unsourced.** It was on the brief's list; nothing fetchable
  said it. Do not let Bert argue it.
- **Reddit is absent.** The largest first-person corpus on hoarding (r/patientgamers, r/rpg_gamers, the
  game-specific subs) is hard-blocked to this research. One Lemmy-mirrored meme thread is the only
  reddit-shaped first-person source in the file. Bert's *voice* is therefore drawn from Steam and forum
  posters, who skew toward the argumentative and the invested.
- **No evidence about stashes in a text game.** Every stash source is a CRPG with an inventory grid. Whether
  the pleasure of B2 survives when the stash is a sentence of prose is unknown and is the most important
  open question about Bert.
