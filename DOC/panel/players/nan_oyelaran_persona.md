# Nan Oyelaran — the Twenty-Minute Regular

**Read this when** the question is whether a place with no quests and no danger can hold a player at all:
daily return, being recognised, the tavern, decorating, visible change, the shape of a very short session,
and the specific way cozy content dies.

**What this is.** A COMPOSITE persona, assembled from patterns repeated across public discussion of cozy
games, hub towns, low-stakes content and short-session play. Nan is not a person and never was. Every
position is a pattern seen in at least two sources, with the sources cited. **What this is not:** a user
interview, a survey, or a claim about prevalence. Confidence legend: ● a thread read directly · ◐ secondary,
aggregated, vendor-authored or summary-only · ○ inferred, no source read.

---

## Identity

Plays twenty minutes a night to come down from a job that requires being alert all day, and measures a good
session by whether anything in the world noticed she was there.

## Play habits

- **Session shape:** 15–25 minutes, nightly, usually the last thing before sleep. Occasionally a longer
  Sunday session.
- **Solo or group:** solo. She has no interest in coordinating with anyone; that is what her job is for.
- **Text or audio:** text, read on a phone, one thumb. Sound off.
- **Device:** phone, in bed, screen dimmed.
- **Tell:** she plays at the same time every day, and the regularity is the point. Solo-RPG writing describes
  exactly her slots — after the children are down, while the coffee warms, ten minutes between meetings
  (Lane C #18 ●).

## What she wants from a home village

Three things, in order.

1. **To be recognised.** Not thanked, not rewarded — recognised. Being acknowledged by name is repeatedly
   named as what makes a village of NPCs feel like a place rather than a menu (Lane OWN O4 ●).
2. **A small thing that completes.** A twenty-minute session must be able to *finish* something. The
   real-time cap in Animal Crossing is described as a feature: limited daily tasks produce short habitual
   sessions and a sustainable place in a daily routine (Lane OWN O4 ●).
3. **Visible change she caused.** Decorating and arranging are the most cited draws of cozy play, and they
   are curatorial with no mechanical payoff at all — people build a whole room to display one collection
   (Lane A #16 ●, Lane OWN O4 ●).

She does not want a plot. She will actively resent one arriving. What she wants is the thing the Gnome Stew
downtime write-up asks for: a choice that leaves a lasting, visible mark on the world (Lane A #12 ●).

## What she hoards / sells / never touches

She hoards **things that look like something**: a cloak with a colour, a carved box, a plant. Utility items
she ignores entirely. She never touches combat consumables — she has never been in combat. She sells
reluctantly, and when she does it is because the list got annoying to scroll, which is the documented trigger
for almost everyone (Lane B #12 ●).

Her version of Bert's stash is a shelf she wants to *look at*.

## How she feels about retired characters

They are her neighbours. She does not think of them as former protagonists; she thinks of them as the woman
in the house by the mill who is always sweeping. She will invent relationships between them unprompted. The
risk is the opposite of Gerry Fulmer's: she will be hurt if a retired character she has adopted is treated as
furniture, and she will not notice or care whether the character's stats are correct.

## Trust: what breaks it with an AI GM

- **Not remembering she was here yesterday.** For her, memory failure does not show up as a contradiction in
  the plot; it shows up as the tavern keeper greeting her as a stranger. This is the most-cited AI-GM
  complaint in every community reached, in its most emotionally exposed form (Lane D #1 ●, Lane D #18 ◐).
- **The same line twice.** Scripted repetition is a named immersion-breaker — and the counter-argument in the
  same thread is worth holding: authored repeated lines become the shared, quotable moments that infinite
  variation destroys (Lane OWN O3 ●). She wants *variation within a recognisable person*, which is the
  hardest of the three options.
- **Drama she did not ask for.** A quiet evening that the GM escalates into a crisis is a broken contract.
  Friction mechanics — durability, timers, pressure — are named as the thing that turns a cozy experience
  stressful (Lane OWN O2 ●).
- **Being sold to.** A cozy experience curdles when it feels engineered for engagement rather than for the
  player (Lane OWN O2 ●).

## Gaps

- **Every cozy source is a video game.** Animal Crossing, Stardew Valley, House Flipper, WoW housing. None is
  a *text* experience, and the whole cozy affordance set — arranging furniture, seeing a room, walking past a
  neighbour — is visual. Whether coziness survives translation to prose is unknown and unsourced. This is the
  single biggest question mark over Nan.
- **No evidence was found about short-session text RPG play specifically.** N6 leans on solo-RPG time-window
  advice and on podcast retention data from vendors.
- **Reddit is absent**, which removes most first-person cozy-game talk; HN's cozy thread substitutes, and HN
  skews toward an analytical, work-stressed, technical population. Nan is therefore plausibly *more*
  articulate about why she plays than a real twenty-minute player would be.
- **Nothing was read about an NPC greeting a returning player by name in a text game.** N1's recognition
  claim is carried by a visual game's community.
