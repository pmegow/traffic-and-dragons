Sol drift-hardening — Fable review & action plan

Reviews DOC/workdone_sol.html (the W1–W7 handoff, commit c8c37f1, v1.601) · prior reviews folded in: entry-13 (84d34fc, v1.602) and tonight's #175 incident work (v1.607) · reviewer: Fable · 2026-08-13
TLDR: The W1–W7 program is real, large, and mostly good — the designs are conservative in the right direction and the verification culture (failing-first tests, sabotage batteries, receipts) is genuine. But the field has now delivered the verdict its own gates could not: every one of Sol's W2 gates was green while the quest-credit blackout shipped inside them, ran 40 turns in the live campaign, destroyed ~2,600 XP and 1,700 gp of recorded rewards plus two quest completions, and pushed the GM into narrating a boss back to life. Entry-13 — a Fable review — held the exact defective line and preserved it. The lesson is not "test more"; both efforts tested plenty. The lesson is that every gate was asked whether it correctly refuses bad writes, and no gate was ever asked what the campaign looks like while it is refusing. This plan's spine is that missing question, applied to every guard the program shipped: the guard failure-mode audit. Tonight's #175 fix closed the W2 instances; the remaining workstreams get the same lens below, prioritized.

1 · What tonight proved about the verification itself

Sol's handoff closed with "the implementation is green; the most valuable review is adversarial scrutiny of the boundaries rather than another broad feature pass." That was the right instinct, and entry-13 did it — eight boundary defects. Both still missed the defect that mattered most in play, because both interrogated the guards' decisions (does it refuse what it should? accept what it should?) and neither interrogated the guards' consequences (when it refuses, what does the player lose, is the loss visible, can the GM ever satisfy it, does it end?). The blackout passed every decision-test and failed every consequence-test:

  • A natural, instruction-following GM emission tripped it (the doc said "enclose the death and every caused consequence"; a combat kill's consequence includes the combat ending).
  • The loss was silent — no toast, no visible marker; the first player-visible symptom was broken fiction 40 turns later.
  • The refusal was unsatisfiable — the nudge's own advice ("re-emit with a NEW id") was blocked by a second gate that refused any envelope whose body named the conflicted subject.
  • It never expired — quarantined receipts never retire, the conflict had no attempt cap, and the strip keyed on a proper noun the campaign could not stop saying.

Hence the lens for everything below — for each fail-closed guard, four questions: ① Can a natural emission trip it? ② When it fires, what exactly is lost, and is the loss loud? ③ Can the GM's next response satisfy it? ④ Does it expire? A guard that fails ②–④ can be worse than the drift it prevents, because drift degrades canon while a wedged guard degrades canon and the prose and the player's trust in the bookkeeping.
