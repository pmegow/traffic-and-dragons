TTS options for Traffic and Dragons

Trial SpeechifyAI Simba 3.2 first, then Inworld Realtime TTS-2. Speechify is the strongest balance of a larger English cast, quality and cost. Inworld is particularly attractive for directing and designing recurring characters. ElevenLabs remains the best place to shop for actors, but its qualifying model needs a different streaming adapter and sits close to the price ceiling.

Ten distinct provider/model routes qualify at the reference workload: 900 billable text units per generated minute and 20 generated hours per month. None exceeds $3/hour under those assumptions. Cartesia and Speechify require monthly plans; qualification depends on utilization. This is a shortlist for auditions, not a claim that any provider has been proven to avoid the hangs experienced with Google.

  1. First audition: SpeechifyAI. Its September 8 catalog expansion changes the comparison with a fixed 13-voice reader. Validate the broader English voices against its purpose-trained eight.[3]
  2. Second audition: Inworld. Use stable delivery for narration and voice design for NPCs the stock catalog cannot cover.[8] [9]
  3. Cast-first alternative: ElevenLabs. Its community library is the deepest documented option here. Audition v3 Conversational specifically; full v3 exceeds the cap.[17] [16]
  4. Keep Cartesia conditional. It leads the benchmark snapshot, but returning to the $5 plan would recreate the small-allowance problem. Its qualifying Startup plan starts at $49/month.[1] [11]

Snapshot: September 11, 2026. Prices are USD before tax, for synthesis only. Free trials, annual discounts and enterprise negotiations are not used to qualify the ten routes. App baseline: v1.896, commit ea1bf79. No paid synthesis or comparative listening test was performed.

Ten candidate assessments

Read these alongside the dynamic ranking. A low aggregate position can reflect an unverified actor count or a constrained API rather than unacceptable audio quality.

1. SpeechifyAI — Simba 3.2

Reference aggregate: 8.69/10 · Small adapter

API selection: simba-3.2

Best first trial. A large ready-made cast and inexpensive synthesis closely match the immediate need. The service advertises 900+ voices, but that is a multilingual total. Its September 8 update removed Simba 3.2’s eight-voice allow-list: all English catalog voices and workspace clones now work. The English subtotal remains unverified, and the expanded catalog may not match the purpose-trained eight in quality.[3] [4]

Select simba-3.2 explicitly; omission selects 3.0. The streaming endpoint returns raw bytes, and PCM matches the app’s 24 kHz mono path. SSML provides delivery controls. An initial buffered adapter should be small; incremental playback can follow through the shared player.[5]

Starter makes the reference 20-hour scenario cost $10. The ranking also considers its ongoing rate so cheap included usage cannot conceal expensive overflow. Monthly balances expire, and exhausted top-up funds can still halt narration. A visible balance warning belongs in the integration.[2]

2. Inworld — Realtime TTS-2

Reference aggregate: 8.48/10 · Small–medium adapter

API selection: inworld-tts-2

Best character-direction trial. Inworld can create a persistent voice from a description, useful when an NPC’s age, accent and texture do not match stock actors. On-demand’s 100-custom-voice allowance is account capacity, not evidence of 100 ready-made English performers. Its stock library therefore receives a conservative cast score.[6] [9]

Start narration in STABLE delivery. Use BALANCED or CREATIVE only where variation improves the performance. Natural-language steering and nonverbal cues provide more direction than voice-and-speed selection, but the adapter must keep instructions out of spoken prose.[8]

Streaming HTTP returns newline-delimited JSON with base64 audio. This fits the PCM conveyor conceptually, with incremental parsing as additional work. Fetch actors through /voices/v1/voices, since the older TTS voice-list route is deprecated. Public pages mix preview and GA terminology; verify access to the selected model on the intended account.[10]

3. Cartesia — Sonic 3.6 · Startup

Reference aggregate: 8.08/10 · Small adapter; monthly commitment

API selection: sonic-3.6-2026-08-27

Quality contender with a budget condition. The selected route is Startup, not $5 Pro. Pro’s $65/M overage fails the ceiling at the reference pace. Startup includes 1.25M credits for $49 and has $45/M overage. It requires at least 16 hours 20 minutes of monthly generated speech to amortize the fee below $3/hour. At ten hours, the average is $4.90/hour.[11]

The earlier experience of exhausting credits is consistent with these mechanics: characters consume credits and requests stop when the balance is exhausted unless overage is enabled. A small subscription is not unlimited narration. The calculator exposes that failure mode rather than relying on the vendor’s slower speaking-rate estimate.[11]

Sonic supports cloning and pronunciation controls. The catalog supplies IDs, gender presentation and locale suitable for NPC casting. Pin the dated 3.6 model snapshot so a moving alias does not quietly change familiar characters between sessions.[13] [14] [12]

4. ElevenLabs — v3 Conversational

Reference aggregate: 8.05/10 · Medium: dialogue WebSocket

API selection: eleven_v3_conversational

Best actor-shopping destination. Its community library offers character-oriented searches and far more choice than a fixed narrator list. Favor tested English voices with long withdrawal notice periods. Owners can remove actors; moderation can add latency. The headline count therefore proves neither permanent campaign availability nor equal performance. Free-tier API access excludes the community library.[17] [18]

The qualifying model is v3 Conversational, not full v3. Its base rate leaves only $0.30/hour headroom at the reference pace; above 1,000 billable characters/minute it fails the cap. Use paid API billing and check each selected actor’s applicable rate and entitlement before pinning it.[16]

A standalone dialogue WebSocket avoids buying the bundled Agents service. Conversational permits one voice per connection; the multi-context endpoint permits five contexts with one voice each. Map the two prefetched groups to bounded contexts and close them on Stop/Skip. The older non-v3 TTS WebSocket is the wrong route for this model.[19] [20]

5. Murf — Falcon 2

Reference aggregate: 7.66/10 · Small–medium adapter

API selection: Falcon streaming route

Strong value challenger. Falcon 2 combines a curated roster with a low character rate. Its 150+ voices span 35+ languages; this is not 150 English actors. Custom cloning is enterprise-only, so this affordable route depends on stock voices.[35] [34]

Use Falcon streaming and verify its current format before connecting it to the PCM player. Testing the older Gen2 workflow does not establish compatibility with the selected model. A bounded whole-group adapter can precede incremental playback.[37]

Current self-serve limits matter more than enterprise-scale claims: five concurrent generations in US-East, two with global/regional routing. Two-prefetch narration can occupy that entire regional allowance before an audition or another tab starts. Use one shared concurrency governor. Its median latency does not establish a tail-latency or hang advantage over Google.[36] [35]

6. Alibaba Cloud — Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS-Plus

Reference aggregate: 7.60/10 · Medium–large: regional protocol

API selection: qwen-audio-3.0-tts-plus

High upside if retooling is acceptable. Plus has two named system voices and more than 500 pre-generated base voices. Counting only the system pair understates its potential; calling all 500 native English actors overstates it. Check language and model compatibility in the downloadable catalog before choosing an NPC bench.[23]

The selected price is International/Singapore: $0.20 per 10,000 input characters. It is neither the mainland-China rate nor a price for the similarly named speech-to-speech model. This synthesis route has no separate output-audio charge.[22]

Natural-language instructions and cloning are supported; voice design is not. Regional WebSocket routing and authentication make a small server adapter preferable to assuming browser compatibility. Cancellation is supported, while CosyVoice’s hot_fix pronunciation facility is explicitly unavailable on this model. US-to-Singapore first-audio time remains an audition question.[24] [25] [26]

7. Fish Audio — S2.1 Pro · paid API

Reference aggregate: 7.57/10 · Small–medium adapter

API selection: s2.1-pro

Inexpensive custom-cast experiment. Free-form performance cues and reference-based voices suit unusual NPC delivery. Its discovery surface is a community/reference catalog, not a verified roster of contracted English actors. Build a reviewed reusable list; a public upload alone is not evidence that the speaker authorized its use.[28] [29]

The paid model costs $15/M UTF-8 bytes with no monthly minimum. ASCII English bytes roughly equal characters; accented and non-Latin text can cost more at the same character count. The paid route is evaluated so the recommendation does not depend on the free developer offer. Initial paid concurrency is five.[27]

Use the explicit model header and saved reference ID. Preserve speaker-group boundaries even though native multi-speaker output is available, to keep cancellation and identity manageable. An August APAC routing incident shows how failover can alter latency; it is not evidence of a current outage or a worse overall reliability rate.[28] [30]

8. Smallest.ai — Lightning v3.1 Pro

Reference aggregate: 7.43/10 · Medium: chunk-size changes

API selection: lightning_v3.1_pro

A manageable, documented English bench. The Pro list has 30 British/American rows but 29 distinct IDs because Henry repeats; thirteen Indian-English/Hindi rows add another accent group. These are catalog counts, not a listening-based diversity measure. The standard model’s 217 voices are not interchangeable with Pro.[32]

The 250-character maximum requires a real grouping change. Split longer speech at natural boundaries and retain one pinned actor across sub-chunks. The current 220-character opener fits, but does not establish compatibility for the rest of a turn. Select Pro and English explicitly.[32] [33]

Sources disagree on concurrency (10 versus 15) and cloning (model capability versus plan entitlement). Budget stock actors and use the lower concurrency assumption until the account confirms otherwise. This lowers flexibility; it does not justify borrowing standard-pool voices or assuming enterprise cloning is included.[31]

9. Soniox — TTS Real-Time v2

Reference aggregate: 7.01/10 · Small–medium adapter

API selection: tts-rt-v2

Cheap audition with a cast-size gate. TTS v2 became generally available in August, with expressive audio tags, cloning and multilingual delivery. Actor metadata supplies useful presentation and descriptions. Public documentation does not establish the stock English bench’s size, so it ranks conservatively for the primary casting requirement.[39] [40]

Billing combines text and audio tokens. Applying both vendor token conversions to the reference density gives approximately $0.71/hour. This is an estimate from both sides of the invoice, not a flat hourly tariff or text-only charge.[38]

Streaming signed 16-bit little-endian PCM at 24 kHz closely fits the audio path. Voice selection at stream creation aligns with speaker grouping. No comparable numeric startup benchmark was established. Advance it only if a catalog pull and network audition show sufficient diversity and prompt audible output.[41] [42]

10. Rime — Coda

Reference aggregate: 6.50/10 · Small–medium adapter

API selection: coda

A conversational specialist. Coda supplies curated actors with explicit descriptions, including youthful American voices and older, lower voices. That is useful for GM casting. Natural conversation may suit townspeople especially well; mythic narration and extreme character acting remain audition questions.[45]

Select modelId: coda. The model-specific table supports the 184-voice count; the broader 600+ marketing claim is not used. Custom pauses remain forthcoming, pronunciation support is automatic normalization, and cloning is enterprise. These limitations lower flexibility.[44] [43]

Coda narrowly fits the cap at the reference density and fails at 1,100 characters/minute. Its published latency uses one concurrent request, while the conveyor uses two. Test that actual workload before crediting its speed. Its lower benchmark score is relative; it does not mean every Coda voice sounds worse than every competitor.

Fit to the narration pipeline

The app separates synthesis from shared scheduling through CLOUD_READERS. It groups consecutive speech by actor, prefetches two OpenAI groups, converts 24 kHz PCM and preserves playback order. OpenAI requests have a 20-second deadline covering headers and body. Stop/Skip abort work; errors are surfaced before a degradation window and local fallback. These are valuable constraints for a new provider, not reasons to replace the GM’s story protocol.[L1–L3]

The current bottleneck is whole-group readiness. A provider can return first audio in 100 ms while the app still waits for the remaining group and startup conditions. Swapping the provider does not make that number the audible onset. Capturing streaming gains requires bounded incremental PCM scheduling, backpressure and rejection of late audio from an obsolete read.[L1–L2]

One adapter interface

Keep one registry for model, voice catalog, input limit, concurrency, billing unit, synthesis and cancellation. Normalize format, sample rate, channels and byte stream at the boundary. HTTP/raw-PCM providers can initially use buffered playback; NDJSON and WebSockets require small transport parsers.

Use a proxy where browser authentication or CORS requires it. A server SDK’s authorization headers are not proof that native browser WebSockets can send them. The existing server is a plausible location, but its incremental operating cost is not included in vendor rates.

A persistent cast registry

The GM already identifies speakers with [SAY:Name], and the app casts by gender/pronouns. OpenAI maps this into a smaller actor bank. A larger bank will still collide or change identities if selection is only a hash into a changing list.[L1–L2]

Persist provider, voice ID and compatible model per NPC. Keep presentation, accent, age range, texture and approved styles as metadata; exclude the narrator, retain manual overrides and visibly flag retired actors. Familiar characters should not silently change voices.

Audition and failure test

Audition eight contrasting English actors: young and old, masculine and feminine presentation, soft and forceful delivery, and multiple accents. Use identical quiet narration, NPC dialogue, confrontation, whisper, fantasy names, numbers and a 1,500-character passage. Compare blind, at matched loudness, against the existing OpenAI reader. The target is a usable cast, not one impressive demo.

Then exercise two concurrent groups, rapid actor switches, 30 minutes of continuous narration, a stalled body after successful response headers, quota exhaustion, throttling, mid-generation Stop/Skip, reconnect and resumed playback. Record first audible sample and completion p50/p95, gaps, discarded generations, speaker mistakes and error causes. Test the deployed origin and a real mobile network. A benign first request cannot prove the reported failure is resolved.

Retain bounded prefetch, one account-level concurrency governor, explicit retry budgets and local fallback. Cancellation can arrive after generation has been charged. At $2.70/generated hour, 25% extra generation costs $3.375 per useful hour before delivery overhead. Log generated versus played seconds and use the calculator’s waste setting.

Reproducible cost arithmetic
Character/byte models: hourly synthesis = rate per million × billable units/minute × 60 ÷ 1,000,000.
Monthly bill = monthly fee + max(0, generated billable units − included units) × overage rate, plus selected delivery overhead.
Soniox hourly estimate = 30,000 × $21.50/M + (characters/minute × 60 × 0.3) × $4/M. At 900: $0.645 + $0.0648 = $0.7098.
A 25% waste setting means 1.25 generated hours per useful hour, not a 25% share of total generation.
Both ongoing useful-hour rate and monthly average must be ≤ $3.

The reference density approximates English at 150 words/minute and six characters per word including spaces. This is an assumption, not a measured campaign average. Speechify’s whitespace exclusion makes this conservative relative to raw text. Fish requires actual UTF-8 byte counts. Soniox’s audio-token conversion is approximate. Audition logs should replace these assumptions with billable units and generated duration.

Prices use direct APIs and published self-serve routes. The calculator does not optimize every subscription tier, assume free allowances continue indefinitely, or subtract promotional credits. A monthly plan can fail at low utilization despite a cheap marginal rate. Optional voice creation, paid retries, cancelled requests and a new proxy can change the invoice. Required subscriptions are included; elective casting and delivery expenses are separate.

Model-specific documentation takes precedence over older marketing pages. Material conflicts are explicit: Smallest’s cloning/concurrency entitlements, mixed Inworld launch labels, Rime’s broader marketing counts and Speechify’s recent catalog expansion. Exact English subtotals for several providers and a common p95 latency dataset remain unresolved. The scores expose these limitations rather than replacing them with guessed measurements.
