Drift Risks — where the anti-drift stack can still leak

Fable audit · 2026-08-08 · engine v1.563 · written for the hardening-pass triage; a parallel audit by Sol lands beside this file, and each will rebut the other.
Instruments: code trace of the drift surface · the live save Rise_of_the_Runelords_Ammut_t1549.tnd (t1549, 3,096 transcript entries, exported 2026-08-08) · a longitudinal diff against the t1265 export (2026-07-30, 284 turns earlier) · the real system prompt built headlessly from the t1549 state via dev/load-engine.js → buildSysPrompt().

Method, and the discipline against over-finding

Every candidate had to survive three validation angles before it became a finding:

  1. Mechanism — the exact code path that produces the drift, quoted by file and line. No mechanism, no finding.
  2. Field evidence — the behavior demonstrated in the real t1549 save (or proven longitudinally against t1265). A mechanism that has never fired in 1,549 turns of real play is a theory, not a vulnerability.
  3. Steelman against — the strongest argument that this is not drift (deliberate design, another system already covers it, the loss is harmless). The finding ships only if the steelman loses, and the steelman's surviving points bound the stated severity.

Ten candidates were rejected under this discipline; they are listed at the end with the evidence that killed them, so the hardening pass doesn't re-litigate them.

Findings

Finding 1 — "Knowledge evicts to the void": per-NPC knowledge is the only memory tier that silently destroys canon, and NPC merges bulk-destroy it severe

The facts an NPC knows — the tier the whole alive-feeling-NPCs validation rests on — roll off a 12-item window straight into nothing, with no archive, no warning, and no relevance test; and the #128 duplicate merges, built to heal NPC memory, truncate the united history to 12 in the same silent way.

Evidence. Longitudinal diff t1265 → t1549 (284 turns): 83 knowledge facts on file at t1265 are gone at t1549 — not superseded, not archived, not merged elsewhere; simply destroyed. Over the same window, lore lost 0 facts and NPC events lost 0 (both follow eviction-to-archive discipline). Among the destroyed: Morwen Zethran's "Two carved entities exist a thousand miles apart, same design, older than Karzoug" — a fact about the currently active Jorgenfist arc, evicted to make room for "First time in days her hands and nerves have properly settled, in the bathhouse." The merge amplification is starker: at t1265 Morwen's history was split across three keys holding ~36 facts total; after the #128 merges, exactly 12 survive — the [NPC_MERGE:] handler concats the duplicate's knowledge then re-slices to 12 (tag_table.js:318), archive-less.

Angle 1 · mechanism: memory.js:976 (summarize extraction) and memory.js:940 (supersede) cap via knowledge.shift() — the evicted fact goes nowhere. fileNpcEvent (memory.js:174) does the same to events at cap 8 via slice(-8). Contrast memory.js:291–298: lore/decisions/chapters were explicitly converted from delete-the-oldest to evict-into-memory.archive ("P12: the caps used to DELETE… never vanishes"). NPC knowledge was left out of that pass. The merge site even documents the adjacent hazard in its own comment (the TODO#69 note about shift-based caps shedding 1/write) without touching the destruction itself.
Angle 2 · field: the 83-fact longitudinal loss above, on the highest-value NPCs (the party's inner circle peg the cap first: Frizwick, Daeris, Morwen, Shalelu, Hemlock, Ameiko all at 12+). Frizwick's 18-entry list (over-cap since at least t1265, seeded above the cap by an import/blueprint path) shows the cap is also not an invariant — an over-cap list stays over-cap forever, shedding one real-play fact per new fact.
Angle 3 · steelman against: "The GM still gets 12 facts per NPC, RAG can re-serve the origin scenes, and oldest-first eviction approximates least-relevant." The first two points bound severity (this is degradation, not blackout). The third is false on the evidence: extraction files trivia and plot-canon into the same FIFO window, so a bathhouse mood-beat evicts the campaign's central mystery. And RAG cannot compensate reliably — it re-serves scene excerpts when entity/location overlap triggers, not the distilled fact; the fact was distilled precisely because the scene alone doesn't carry it. Steelman loses; severity stays severe because the loss is silent, permanent, targets the most-developed NPCs by construction, and the healing tool (merge) makes it worse.

Remedies, ranked:

  1. 1. P12 parity: evict to archive, everywhere knowledge/events shrink. memArchive().npcKnowledge.push({npc,fact,turn}) at all three shrink sites (both shift caps + the merge re-slice), with the merge case warning loudly ("merge truncated N facts — archived"). Cheap, mechanical, honest, and it makes remedy 2/3 and any future archive-mining tier possible. Nothing is lost ever again; injection behavior unchanged.
  2. 2. Importance-aware retention instead of FIFO. Before shifting, prefer evicting facts with zero token overlap against active quests/arcs/schedule labels (deterministic, no model call — the #29 feTokens stemmer already exists); party members get a higher cap (e.g. 25, matching CORE_MEMORY_CAP's per-sheet precedent). Fixes the bathhouse-evicts-the-mystery failure directly, at the cost of a new deterministic heuristic on the drift surface.
  3. 3. Summarize-time compaction. When a list hits cap, hand the extractor the 12 facts and ask it to fold near-duplicates and demote stale ones (model judgment, one extra instruction in an existing call). Highest ceiling — the window gets denser, not just bigger — but it puts an LLM's judgment inside canon retention, the exact class the engine avoids; only worth it layered on top of 1.

Finding 2 — "The ten-chapter horizon": always-on narrative memory covers ~35 turns of a 1,549-turn campaign, and the archive that holds the rest is write-only moderate-high

The injected story spine is the last 8 chapter summaries (~t1524–t1545 at export time); the other 206 chapters live in an archive nothing ever reads, so the GM's only routes to Act 1 are capped per-NPC facts (Finding 1), 12 lore lines, 16 core memories, and RAG excerpts that fire only on entity overlap — arc-level history with no entity handle is unreachable and will be confabulated on demand.

Evidence. fileChapter keeps 10 chapters (memory.js:914, overflow → archive.chapters, now 206) and eventHistory keeps 8 (memory.js:916) — that is the whole of STORY SO FAR (api.js:856, measured 1,952 tokens). Under the RAG diet the TOC's CHAPTER SUMMARIES section is deliberately dropped as duplicate. A repo-wide grep for archive. readers finds tests, the merge tool's rename fix-ups, and export — no injection or retrieval path; memory.js:295's own comment says a future tier "can mine the archive," i.e. aspirational. RAG's document set is GM transcript entries only — chapters and archives are not indexed.

Angle 1 · mechanism: confirmed above — three caps (8/10/write-only archive) compose into a hard horizon. Nothing is deleted (P12 held), but nothing behind the horizon is servable.
Angle 2 · field: at t1549 the spine spans 21 in-fiction turns of summaries. The campaign's Act 1 (Sandpoint, ~500 turns, the arc that produced 10 of the 16 defining moments) has zero chapter presence in any prompt. A player asking the GM (or an NPC) "how did we get here" gets an answer synthesized from 12 lore lines + whatever RAG happens to trigger — the confabulated-recall class that tail retention (#28) was built to kill at session scale, reappearing at campaign scale.
Angle 3 · steelman against: "The t308 validation proved NPCs recall 100+-turn-old events; core memories, quests, and the skeleton carry the big beats; maybe chapters are redundant." Partly right — and it bounds severity below severe: character-level continuity genuinely does ride NPC facts + RAG + core memories, and act boundaries ride the skeleton. But the t308 validation ran at one-fifth this scale with 20× fewer archived chapters, and every carrier named is itself capped (knowledge 12 — Finding 1; lore 30; moments 25/sheet). The un-carried class is specific and real: arc-shaped history ("what did the trafficking network do across Act 2") has no entity key for RAG and no single fact-line home. The steelman survives as a scope limit, not a refutation.

Remedies, ranked:

  1. 1. Era compression — a third summary tier. When the archive accrues N chapters (say 20), one summarize-class call compresses them into a single era summary (~150 tokens) kept in a new always-injected ERAS block, recursively bounded (~10 eras even at t5000). This is the same move summarize() already makes at session→chapter scale, applied at chapter→era scale; cost is one extra API call per ~20 chapters. Bounded, always-on, answers the arc-shaped question directly.
  2. 2. Index archived chapters into RAG. Stamp each chapter with the entity scan at archive time (the ragEntitiesFromRaw machinery exists) and let ragRetrieve score them alongside transcript entries, serving as "PAST CHAPTER" excerpts under the existing stale-chunk header. Cheap and read-side-only — but stays trigger-dependent, so it helps entity questions, not arc questions.
  3. 3. Raise the caps (chapters 10→25, eventHistory 8→12). Trivial, buys ~2× horizon at ~1,500 extra volatile tokens/turn, and merely postpones the same wall — worth doing only as a stopgap alongside 1 or 2.

Finding 3 — "The unfed channel": [LOCATION_STATE:] infrastructure is complete and the GM essentially never emits it, so durable world changes still live only in evaporating prose moderate

The #105/B17 machinery (stateNotes, geo-block override, the always-present CHANGED LOCATIONS roll-up) is built, tested, and starving: zero emissions in the last 40 responses and 3 nodes carrying notes out of 77 in the whole campaign — so a place the story burned, sealed, or emptied is still re-served intact from its frozen description once the prose scrolls out of the summarize window.

Evidence. The tagLog provenance ring (last 40 responses): SAY 39, TIME_ADVANCE 19, DICE 10 … LOCATION_STATE 0. Campaign-lifetime: 3 nodes with stateNotes across 77 map nodes and 3,096 transcript entries — a campaign that includes a workshop assault, sealed Spire doors, and a trafficking network dismantled across two cities. The measured CHANGED LOCATIONS block: 72 tokens, near-empty. Adjacent context: sub-location descriptions exist for 10 of 56 nodes (#134's nudge shipped 2026-08-04 and heals go-forward only), so for most interiors the frozen-description override has nothing to override or be overridden by.

Angle 1 · mechanism: the tag's only driver is its STATE TAGS doc line. This is the exact documented failure class — prompt-channel-beats-position — proven twice on this project: [SAY:] emission was ZERO until buildSayComplianceNudge gave it an engine-note channel (v1.449), and [LOCATION_DESC:] was 8% before #134's nudge. Every tag that got a nudge channel complies (SAY now 39/40); every tag left doc-only starves. LOCATION_STATE is the highest-stakes tag still doc-only.
Angle 2 · field: the 0/40 and 3-nodes-lifetime numbers above; and the consequence is already on the incident record — the t1431 multiplying-beds forensics showed re-imagined interiors, and the #134 close note itself rated "furniture drift fully stops" only MODERATE confidence with this exact dependency open.
Angle 3 · steelman against: "Maybe nothing durable changed in those 40 turns — city intrigue, shopping, planning — and 3 notes might be the honest count." For the 40-turn window this is plausible and softens the ring evidence. It cannot explain the lifetime rate: the campaign demonstrably changed places durably (the sealed seam-door at the Spire is in the #137 forensics record) and those changes have no notes. Severity stays moderate rather than severe because the failure needs a return visit after summarize-window decay to manifest, and the campaign currently revisits few changed sites.

Remedies, ranked:

  1. 1. Post-combat one-shot nudge. When an encounter closes at a node (COMBAT_END or auto-close), arm a one-shot engine note next turn: "if that fight left durable marks on this place, file [LOCATION_STATE:]; if not, stay silent." Deterministic trigger, targets the highest-yield class of change (violence), same one-shot-shelf pattern as buildStayBehindNudge, zero noise when nothing burned.
  2. 2. Fold into the #134 settle nudge. The existing buildLocationDescNudge fires when settled at an undescribed node; extend it to also fire (with different text) at a described node the party has dwelt at N turns: "does the frozen description still match what the story has done here?" Reuses a proven builder and covers non-combat change (looting, construction), at the cost of periodic-noise risk the #134 cooldown already manages.
  3. 3. Destruction-verb watcher. A detectStayBehind-style scanner for burn/collapse/seal/shatter verbs near the current location name in GM prose, arming the same one-shot nudge. Broadest coverage, but open-vocabulary detection is false-positive-prone (the sttCorrectNames lesson: designed catches are inseparable from FP classes) — third, not first.

Finding 4 — "One hundred and seven strings": the inventory is the largest block in the prompt and the only major state class with zero canon behind it moderate tracked · #81

107 uncanonized item strings cost ~2,691 tokens every turn — the single largest volatile block, 8.5× the quest block — and because no item has recorded mechanics anywhere, what a potion, wand, or ring actually does is re-derived from its name on every use: the exact class the capability bible killed for spells, still fully open for items.

Evidence. Measured from the real prompt build: Inventory ≈ 2,691 tokens vs ACTIVE QUESTS 314, CAMPAIGN CLOCK 71, CHANGED LOCATIONS 72 — the flat string list outweighs the three most load-bearing canon blocks combined by ~6×. 14 items carry parenthetical provenance ("(from Sheriff Hemlock)") as their only semantics. No duplicates by base name (the #50d fold discipline holds). #81 (item bible) is filed with its display-vs-GM-injection fork undecided; #82's tooltip surface already ships pointing at a data source that doesn't exist.

Erratum (2026-08-08, post-rebuttal — conceded to Sol): the 2,691-token figure was a measurement error — the instrument read from "Inventory:" to the next blank line, overrunning into the adjacent Relationships/Languages sheet lines. The true inventory line is 5,026 characters ≈ 1,257 tokens: the third-largest volatile block, behind STORY SO FAR and ACTIVE NPC DETAILS. The "largest block / 8.5×" claims in this finding are withdrawn; the schema claim (zero item canon anywhere) and the tracked-under-#81 disposition stand, at the softened priority Sol's comments argue.

Angle 1 · mechanism: items are plain strings in character.inventory; nothing anywhere records what one does. Anti-drift for spells/abilities works via per-turn canon re-injection (buildSpellBibleBlock); items have no equivalent, so the GM's belief about an item's power lives only in conversation momentum — the compressible, drift-prone place.
Angle 2 · field: the token measurement above is the new, hard evidence; 107 items is also an attention hazard — load-bearing one-line canon (clock, changed-locations, party-size) shares a context window with a 10,700-character shopping list. (Direct field proof of an item contradicting itself across turns was not hunted in the transcript for this audit — the mechanism-plus-scale is asserted, the specific incident is not.)
Angle 3 · steelman against: "Most of the 107 are narrative color; flavor items can't meaningfully drift; #81 already tracks the fix, so this is a duplicate." Fair on both edges — this finding deliberately ships as evidence changing #81's priority and scope, not as a new row. What the steelman can't dismiss: the measured cost is new information (nobody knew inventory was the largest block), and the consumable-class items (potions, oils, the alchemist's charges) are mechanics-bearing today with zero canon. The existing consumable nudge machinery (buildConsumableNudge) polices quantity, not effect — adjacent, not covering.

Remedies, ranked:

  1. 1. Decide #81's fork as GM-injection, scoped to carried items. Item bible + emergent overlay (the [SPELL_DEF:] write-once pattern as [ITEM_DEF:]), with a buildItemBibleBlock injecting canon for mechanics-bearing carried items only (consumables/wearables with effects — not every trophy). Kills the drift class at its root, the proven spell-bible shape; it's the drift-surface option, which is exactly why it's worth the Fable dance.
  2. 2. Shrink the surface: structured inventory. Categorize on write (weapon/consumable/quest/treasure/mundane) and inject mundane/treasure as counts ("assorted travel gear ×23") with the full list available on demand via the sheet — reclaims ~2k tokens/turn and reduces the attention hazard without touching canon. Pairs naturally with the #6 stash when it lands.
  3. 3. Display-only bible (#81's other fork): tooltips get real, prompt unchanged. Cheapest, off the drift surface — and it hardens nothing; the GM still re-derives effects. Only worth choosing if the fork ruling goes against injection.

Finding 5 — "Two calendars, one future": anticipated events live in two stores with different lifecycle rules, and the age-based expiry sweep is blind to whether a thread is still live low-moderate

The same anticipated fact can sit in memory.futureEvents (auto-expires at 40 turns, by design) and worldState.clock.schedule (expires by in-fiction due time, with escalation) — at t1549 "Sable checks the workshop" is in both, one copy 0 turns from silent expiry while its twin is 1.6 in-fiction days from due and the Intercept-Sable quest is active; threads that land only in the expiring store die quietly at age 40 regardless of liveness.

Evidence. From the save: futureEvent t1509 "Sable or an agent of the network arrives to check on the silent workshop" at age 40 (the FUTURE_EXPIRE_TURNS threshold — it expires at the next summarize) while clock.schedule holds "Sable checks the workshop" due at min 8930 and the quest is active. Eleven other pending events, ages 4–37, include slow-burn threads with no schedule twin and no quest anchor ("Determine the significance of the empty child's glove", age 29) that will hit the same age wall mid-life.

Angle 1 · mechanism: expireFutureEvents() sweeps on age alone — no check against active quests, arcs, or schedule labels. The two stores have separate writers (the summarize extractor / [FUTURE_EVENT:] vs scheduleAdd) and no cross-reference; which store a thread lands in is an accident of how the GM phrased it.
Angle 2 · field: the live Sable pair above — a real, active-arc thread whose futureEvents copy dies this week while its schedule copy escalates. Expiry itself is provably firing in the field (the engine warns and archives to expiredSchedules/sweeps pending events; 12 pending is a healthy count, so the #29 hygiene is working as designed).
Angle 3 · steelman against: "Expiry at 40 was a deliberate ruling (#29) against pile-up; the Sable instance loses nothing because the schedule twin and the quest both keep the thread in front of the GM; a stale mirror dying is hygiene, not drift." Mostly right — which is why this is the audit's lowest-severity finding. What survives: the ruling predates the campaign clock (#73); threads that exist only as a futureEvent (no schedule, no quest — the glove) get killed by age while genuinely unresolved, silently from the player's view, and the GM never gets to disagree. That is thread-drop drift, in a bounded class.

Remedies, ranked:

  1. 1. Liveness guard on expiry. Before expiring, token-overlap the event against active quest titles/objectives and live schedule labels (the feTokens stemmer again); a hit refreshes setTurn instead of expiring, loudly. Deterministic, no model, ~15 lines, kills the only harmful case while preserving the anti-pile-up ruling for genuinely orphaned events.
  2. 2. Expiry demotes to a one-shot GM question instead of silence. An expiring event rides one engine note ("this pending thread aged out — resolve it, re-file it, or let it die"), then expires if unanswered. GM judgment where the engine can't know; costs one nudge per expiry and adds to the note-builder stack.
  3. 3. Unify the stores — fold futureEvents into the clock schedule (every anticipated event gets a due time, possibly "unscheduled"). Architecturally cleanest end-state and the most disruptive: two writers, the extractor contract, and the TOC section all move; not worth it ahead of remedies 1–2 unless the clock work reopens anyway.

Sol's Comments

Independent rebuttal · 2026-08-08 · checked against both cited saves, the current source, and a fresh headless buildSysPrompt() reconstruction. Fable's original text above is unchanged.

Overall judgment

Finding 1 is strong and urgent. Findings 2, 3, and 5 identify real mechanisms, although their recommended first remedies need tighter safety constraints. Finding 4's underlying item-canon concern is valid, but its size/priority argument does not reproduce. The rejected clock candidate should be reinstated as a separate repair-safety vulnerability.

FindingValiditySol's disposition
1 · NPC knowledge evictionconfirmedSevere. Archive parity is necessary, but an unread archive alone does not harden live continuity.
2 · Chapter horizonconfirmed, boundedA real accessibility horizon; “will be confabulated” is stronger than the evidence. Moderate rather than clearly moderate-high.
3 · LOCATION_STATE starvationconfirmedStrong field signal. The proposed post-combat nudge can write to the wrong node unless it is location-anchored.

Finding 1 · NPC knowledge evicts to the void

Validity. Confirmed. The two ordinary write sites use shift(); [NPC_MERGE:] concatenates then keeps only slice(-12); none of those losses has an archive route. My simple exact-text longitudinal diff found 115 t1265 knowledge strings absent from all live t1549 NPC knowledge and every existing archive array. Fable's more conservative 83-fact count is therefore directionally credible, and the cited carved-entity fact is genuinely absent. The over-cap Frizwick list independently proves that 12 is not a load invariant.

Solution concerns.

  1. Archive-on-eviction is the right first safety net, but not a complete drift fix. The archive is currently write-only, so this prevents irreversible loss without restoring the fact to a future prompt. It also needs a complete schema path: ui-files.js's import whitelist currently preserves only archive.lore, decisions, and chapters; it already drops superseded, and would silently drop a new npcKnowledge collection unless import, migration, merge/rename handling, and tests change together. Bound or compact the storage tier so a “never delete” rule does not become unbounded sync ballast.
  2. Quest-token retention is too brittle to decide canon value. A central fact phrased with synonyms can score zero, while trivia repeating a quest name can become immortal. Raising party-member caps also enlarges ACTIVE NPC DETAILS and worsens the stale-detail conflict documented in Sol R1 and R2. Type facts first (durable canon versus transient scene state), then use relevance only as a secondary eviction hint.
  3. LLM compaction must never become the sole copy. Folding twelve assertions into new prose can merge subjects, erase qualifiers, or turn beliefs into objective truth. If used, retain immutable source facts with turn/provenance, make the compacted statement a derived index, and require a visible replacement receipt.

Finding 2 · The chapter horizon

Validity. The storage and injection trace is correct: ten live chapters, eight eventHistory lines, 206 archived chapters, and no archive reader. The risk statement needs one qualification. RAG is not gated only by NPC entity overlap; current location and active-quest overlap also open candidacy, after which lexical terms rank candidates. Nevertheless, lexical similarity alone cannot open the gate, and archived chapter summaries are not candidates at all. An arc-shaped question with no currently matching person, place, or quest can therefore fall through. Existing skeleton, quest, lore, core-memory, and transcript carriers make confabulation possible rather than inevitable.

Solution concerns.

  1. Era compression has the highest coverage but also compounds model loss. “Every 20 chapters” is a storage cadence, not a narrative era boundary; an arc can straddle two batches. Recursive summaries can launder an early mistake into permanent canon. Each era needs covered-turn ranges, source chapter IDs, correction/supersession semantics, and a rebuild path from immutable chapters. Keep the always-on budget explicit rather than assuming ten eras remains enough at every campaign length.
  2. Archived-chapter retrieval may deserve the safer first experiment. It is read-side and reversible, but simply adding chapters to the current RAG corpus will not answer handle-free arc questions because the entity/location/quest gate remains. A separate topical chapter gate or an era/arc index is required; entity stamps alone reproduce the existing blind spot.
  3. Raising caps is correctly ranked last. It spends recurring prompt budget and delays rather than removes the horizon. Use it only with measured recall tests and a fixed budget.

Finding 3 · The unfed LOCATION_STATE channel

Validity. Confirmed, and the save is slightly worse than the headline: three nodes have stateNotes, but Sandpoint's only note is literally "none". Only two nodes carry meaningful durable changes. Seventy-five of 77 map nodes are marked visited, so blueprint-only nodes do not explain the starvation. The lack of a demonstrated return-visit contradiction appropriately keeps this at moderate severity.

Solution concerns.

  1. A next-turn post-combat nudge must capture the changed node. [LOCATION_STATE:] always writes to currentNodeKey(). If combat closes and the same response moves the party, a next-turn reminder can file the old battlefield's damage onto the new location. Store the trigger node and suppress the note after departure, or introduce an explicitly targeted, strictly validated write path. Also do not reuse the current build-time latch semantics without a delivery/receipt check: a failed provider call or ignored note otherwise burns the one shot.
  2. Feed-side hardening should not outrun sink-side hardening. fileLocationState keeps only LOC_STATE_CAP notes and evicts the oldest with a console warning. That contradicts the prompt's “permanent change record” promise once a place changes more than the cap.   3. The settle nudge and verb watcher remain secondary. Periodic “does this still match?” prompting can encourage the model to invent a change merely to satisfy the note. Verb scans also confuse hypothetical, negated, and remote destruction with a current-location fact. Both need telemetry and exact-node gating before promotion.

Validity. The schema point is valid: the active inventory is plain strings and the engine has no canonical effect record for mechanics-bearing items. The quantitative premise does not reproduce. Solution concerns.

  1. A model-authored write-once [ITEM_DEF:] can canonize the first hallucination. Prefer curated definitions for known items and an explicit player-confirmed definition for emergent objects. Finding 5 · Two calendars, one future

Validity. The bifurcation is real. At age 40, the Sable future event survives because the predicate is age <= 40; it will disappear at the first summarize after its age exceeds 40, with no warning or archive. Its schedule twin persists by clock time. The particular countdown is currently distorted by the clock defect discussed below, but that does not remove the duplicated-lifecycle problem.

Solution concerns.

  1. Token-overlap refresh should not be the primary fix. It has false negatives under paraphrase and false positives on generic words such as “network” or “workshop.” Rewriting setTurn also destroys the event's original age and can make a stale event immortal while leaving both stores active. At most, use overlap to flag a review candidate.
  2. Prefer explicit promotion/linkage. When a fuzzy anticipated event becomes a clock schedule, record a shared ID or retire the future-event copy as “promoted to schedule.” Active quests can reference that ID. This preserves provenance, eliminates duplicate prompt pressure, and gives one lifecycle authority without requiring a full store rewrite.
  3. The one-shot GM question is safer for ambiguous legacy entries, but inherits note-stack and latch risks. Archive anything it finally drops. Full store unification remains the clean end-state if clock work reopens, and may deserve a higher rank than a heuristic refresh despite its effort.
