Settlement tiers — what a town opens on arrival

#385 design note · Fable, 2026-09-24 · for the owner's signature · nothing here is built. File: C:\Projects\traffic-and-dragons\DOC\Research\DOC_settlement_tiers.html

TLDR. Today a settlement carries one physical size word and a wares cap; the market ask fires once per place when nothing is on record, so The Long Walk's High Reach sat at two wares for forty turns. The proposal: every settlement has a tier — outpost, village, town, city, metropolis — and a tier declares a roster of establishments (its inn with its tavern, its shops). The roster is introduced in prose on first arrival, never as a shopping list; each establishment is filed as a sub-location as the party finds it, and each filed shop gets its own market ask on the village model. The tier is derived from what the GM already writes (the size word, the place's own name), so no new tag is needed and no save migrates. Five rows follow the signature.

0. The owner's invariants (2026-09-08)

  • Every settlement has at least one inn, and every inn has a tavern.
  • Opening proposal: outpost — the one inn (with its tavern), no shops; village — 2 shops; town — 5 shops; city — 10 shops and a second inn; metropolis — 20 shops.

The counts are the owner's numbers unchanged. The word lists are the derivation's whole vocabulary — deliberately short, one tier per word, no substring pairs (the opposing-values rule: "town" and "township" share a stem but resolve to the SAME tier, so the pair cannot misfile).

3. ① Vocabulary and mapping — recommendation: derive, never tag go

Keep [LOCATION_SIZE:] as the physical scale. It already carries travelMins, every existing save has it, and the frozen tag doc is money-tested. The tier is a read-time derivation: settlementTierFor(node) (pure, helpers.js) reads the node's size and the leaf words of its name and LOCATION_DESC; a name word outranks the size word ("Greyhaven, a sprawling village" is a village). Unknown → null: a node with no tier is not a settlement and nothing below applies to it — wilderness and ruins stay exactly as they are.

  • No new tag, no doc-line change, no strip-regex change: the stable prompt half is byte-identical.
  • No migration: an existing save derives its tiers the first time a reader asks; nothing is stored until the roster is minted (§5).
  • waresSizeTier and WARES_CAP_BY_SIZE keep serving the legacy world-node market until row R3 retires it (§4).
  • Rejected: a [SETTLEMENT:tier] tag. It is a parser + doc + strip change on the drift surface, and the GM would emit it as unreliably as it emits [LOCATION_SIZE:] today — the derivation reads what the GM already writes. If a campaign ever needs an explicit override, map_cleanup.html can set node.tier by hand and the derivation yields to a stored value.

4. ② What a shop is on the map — recommendation: the village model, everywhere go

A shop is a sub-location node under the settlement, exactly as the village files them: leaf name matches shopWords or carries shop:true, a keeper (the seller's name, from the WARES note or the first [NPC:] seen inside), and its own wares[] capped at WARES_CAP_SHOP. Inns and taverns are shops with a role — role:"inn" / role:"tavern" — not a separate node kind: one shape, one carrier through merge/split (the audit C1/C2 fix already carries wares, hours, wanted). What a role adds is an affordance, not a schema: the inn is where [REST:long] reads as safe and paid; the tavern is where whispers already gather and where a board could hang later (§7).

  • The settlement node keeps the roster: node.roster = {tier, inns:N, shops:N, found:[…leaf keys]}, minted once on first arrival (§5). The roster is a count of what exists, not a list of names — the GM names each place as the party meets it.
  • The legacy world-node market (wares on the town itself) is retired for new settlements: once a node has a roster, the per-shop ask replaces the per-town ask. A settlement that already carries world-node wares from the old ask keeps them until they expire (7 days) — no rewrite of existing data.
  • The village kind is exempt from rosters: its shops are whatever the library's residents run (populated from the library, not from a tier), and the owner's "village" tier count would fight that. The village keeps its own model unchanged.

5. ③ How the opportunities are introduced — the arrival note go

On the party's first arrival at a node that derives a tier (the [LOCATION:] filing of a new settlement, or the first visit to an existing one after this ships), the engine mints the roster and arms a one-shot engine note for the next turn. It names the tier's roster in prose terms and forbids the shopping list; the GM files establishments as the party finds them, and each filed shop gets its own market ask the first time the party stands in it (the village mechanism, buildMarketNote's per-shop branch, gated on the roster instead of the kind). Draft wording:

[ENGINE NOTE — ARRIVAL (not a player action): High Reach is a TOWN. What a town of this world has: one inn with its own tavern, and five shops or trades — a smith, a healer, a provisioner, whatever this place would plausibly keep. Do not list them. Let the party SEE them as they move: name an establishment when it comes into view or someone points to it, and file it with [SUBLOCATION:name] when the party enters. Wares are asked for inside each shop, never here. The inn is where a paid night is safe; the tavern is where the town talks.]
  • Once per settlement, ever — latched on the roster (roster.introduced), restored by the note-latch registry on a dead provider turn like every other one-shot.
  • Silent in combat (the fight outranks the tour); silent in the village kind.
  • Tier words the note uses are the tier table's own; the counts are the roster's, so a hand-set tier in map_cleanup speaks the same sentence.
  • The #431 HERE line may add "Establishments: 2 of 5 found" at the settlement node — UI only, no prompt text.

6. ④ Ceilings and restock — recommendation: no new constants go

The tier sets the number of shops; the shelf per shop stays WARES_CAP_SHOP (6) and the restock rhythm stays WARES_RESTOCK_DAYS (7). The settlement's ceiling is therefore shops × 6 (table in §2) and it is a consequence, not a setting. An outpost's inn may still answer its ask with a bed and a meal; "no shops" means no trades, not no commerce. WARES_CAP_BY_SIZE retires with the world-node market (R3).

7. ⑤ The fourth button and the board

  • Buy: the village rule generalises — "Buy the X from <keeper>" fires only when the hero stands in a shop node with live wares and the keeper in the scene. The adventure branch's looser "seller somewhere in the scene" rule retires with the world-node market. One branch, not two.
  • A quest board does not exist today and this spec does not design one. If wanted, it is an affordance of the tavern role (a [QUEST:…|offered] the GM raises when the party sits in the tavern) — a later row, after the roster has been played.

8. ⑥ Tests and viewers

  • Derivation table: every size word and every name word resolves to exactly one tier; an unknown yields null; a stored node.tier wins.
  • Roster minting: first arrival mints once (idempotent on revisit), the note fires once, a dead turn restores the latch (the #151 registry census).
  • Found shops: a filed sub-location whose leaf matches shopWords marks the roster; the per-shop ask fires inside it; the settlement's own market ask no longer fires once a roster exists; a village campaign is byte-identical.
  • Carriers: merge/split carry roster, shop, keeper, role (the C1/C2 class — a test per field, not per carrier).
  • Prompt: the stable half unchanged (frozen goldens); the volatile half gains the note only on the arrival turn.
  • Viewers: map_viewer shows the tier as a badge on settlement nodes and shades found/unfound roster counts; map_cleanup lets the operator set node.tier and shop/role on a sub-location (the only explicit override).

9. Questions the signature settles

  2. Derive the tier from the GM's own words (recommended) rather than teach a new tag?

  5. The village kind stays on its library-driven shops, exempt from rosters?
