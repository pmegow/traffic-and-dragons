01 / Foundation and scope

The working pass-the-phone game supplies the fiction and round structure: each PC declares an action, the declarations form an ordered block, and the group receives a common result. Remote play should preserve that shared resolution while allowing separate reading, drafting and character control. Four independently generated campaigns cannot be merged into one trustworthy story.

02 / The table experience

The central playability risk is waiting and lost agency. Let people draft in parallel, then make the commitment point clear. Network arrival order must not become initiative or permission to speak for another player.

  1. Create and agree. The owner selects a campaign copy and room spending ceiling. Each participating player agrees to the equal usage split and sets their own mandatory ceiling; room ownership does not authorize spending another account’s allowance. The group agrees on tone, content boundaries, history visibility, absence and removal policies, and the selected model provider. In A, disclose the host’s access and powers before admission.
  2. Join independently. Each player signs into their own account, accepts an invitation, and is admitted to a seat/character. Show the public history that joining will reveal. No shared account or provider key.
  3. Read and draft. Everyone sees the same committed scene and authorized sheets. Unsent typing stays on that device. Sheet focus, scrolling and audio playback are personal presentation choices.
  4. Post and acknowledge. Posted intentions are shared. Ready acknowledges the exact current planRev and displayed cost share/maximum under the current funding terms; editing an intention, changing participants or changing those terms clears readiness. Help and Pass are explicit choices. Resolve conflicting intentions before locking the plan.
  5. Resolve deliberately. Only the authorized facilitator may resolve, after every participating seat acknowledges the same plan and all players’ shares are reserved atomically. The last Ready click never buys a turn automatically.
  6. Read one result. Publish after canonical commit. Show common third-person narration, attributable PC outcomes and resource receipts. Equal agency does not require four equal-length speeches. Advance fictional time once for the adjudicated events; network waiting does not advance the campaign clock.
  7. Pause or finish. Any member can request a safety pause. End with a factual recap, outstanding choices and export options. Return from a disconnect using recorded declarations and receipts, not reconstructed guesses.

Interruption and consent

Before lock, a required member’s absence blocks resolution and clears that member’s readiness; reconnect requires acknowledgment. After lock, disconnect does not revoke the already-given consent: that round may finish, but the next waits. A safety pause holds publication and new work. Resume requires the facilitator and acknowledgment from the person who requested the pause, or the group’s previously agreed withdrawal procedure; kicking someone does not retroactively supply their consent.

No timer plays an absent PC. After a proposed 90-second idle reminder, offer pause/reconnect, not punishment. A defensive/pass delegation may be added only as a narrow, revocable player agreement. Taking another PC’s possessions, PvP or acting as them needs explicit affected-player consent and table rules. Plain text chat is bounded, separate from paid Table Talk, and excluded from GM prompts unless a player deliberately submits their own message as an action.

Audio and accessibility

Use text and an optional external group call for the prototype. In-game narration, if later enabled, is individually controlled and never required for Ready. A shared event ID prevents automatic replay after reconnect. Microphone capture requires an explicit action and review before sending; it cannot infer which seat spoke in a shared call. Do not assume browser speech is processed locally—disclose the actual audio provider before enabling it.

Require keyboard access, visible focus, meaningful screen-reader status, 200% zoom, retained drafts and silent play. As an illustration, 180 words at 180 words/minute takes a minute to hear; with 30 seconds planning, ten seconds review and twelve seconds inference, a round takes about 112 seconds before interruptions. These are assumptions, not measurements. Test a concise ensemble result and a satisfying twenty-minute stopping point.

03 / Method A — trusted host, durable relay

A

The host computes the game.

A dedicated host shell runs the shared engine in an isolated candidate context. The room service owns admission, command ordering, durable checkpoints and accepted commits. All model calls go through the service gateway.

Useful if it arrives meaningfully sooner.

Fits trusted friends who accept host dependence. Guests still get independent sheets, drafts and controls. The amount of engine extraction needed must be established before calling this the smaller project.

Players ↔ room serviceIdentity, plan, revisions and public events
↔
Host candidate engineFull private checkpoint; isolated computation
↔
Gateway ↔ providerAuthorized attempts, reservations and durable responses

The commit boundary

  1. Freeze. The service binds the exact participant IDs, posted content and agreed funding allocation to an immutable round, base revision and fenced host epoch. It reserves every participant’s share of the authorized job’s budget atomically.
  2. Compute in isolation. Load the complete acknowledged checkpoint: world state including transcript, memory, session log, canonical configuration and engine/schema identity. Credentials are excluded. Every upstream attempt has a purpose and ID; its response/usage is persisted before candidate mutations use it.
  3. Accept atomically. Validate phase, attempt, membership policy, epoch, base revision and batch identity. Store the complete next checkpoint, projections, receipt, usage attribution and broadcast outbox together. The service validates the envelope, not the honesty of host adjudication.
  4. Adopt. Host and guests adopt the accepted checkpoint/projections. Do not run commitGmTurn() again or reapply tags. Discard rejected candidates; recover a lost acknowledgment by looking up the receipt, never by regenerating.

Cloning only worldState is insufficient: memory, session history, prompt latches, asynchronous callbacks, storage and UI effects also participate. Use isolated execution with explicit adapters and one engine source. Do not loop over sendAction() or temporarily swap globals around an asynchronous call. Before acceptance, the candidate may not touch live saves, legacy cloud state, narration, audio or canonical UI.

In room mode, disable old Retry/reroll, direct Sync edits, free Rest, local restore/import-overwrite and other unmediated writers. Rest becomes an agreed action; required progression uses validated commands. Later administrative corrections must be visible, revisioned operations, not unrestricted state blobs. Suppress legacy autosync at the storage boundary as well as the UI. Cache only room/revision-keyed mirrors; resume from service authority.

Trust and availability

The host, service operator and provider can see private canon. A malicious host can alter candidate prompts/results, inspect secrets, withhold delivery or invent adjudication. A gateway ledger reveals authorized billed attempts, not truthful computation. Promise one accepted commit per round, not fair dice or one generation. No guest receives the owner’s session, provider credentials, raw prompt or complete save.

One host execution lease is valid. A second tab is read-only until an explicit takeover fences the old epoch. Host loss pauses new resolution; the same owner resumes from the acknowledged checkpoint and attempt record. No automatic guest promotion or private-canon transfer. A records engine/schema identity and refuses incompatible resume; today’s static delivery cannot supply a pinned older bundle on demand. An already-open compatible tab need not stop merely because a new deployment exists.

The deciding spike: force a relay rejection after takeover and prove every live/save/cloud artifact remains at the old checkpoint. Then accept, lose the acknowledgment, reload and recover without another generation or mutation. If this requires most of B’s extraction, A has lost its main scheduling advantage.

04 / Method B — authoritative server rooms

B

The service computes the game.

Every browser submits authorized commands and reads projections. A room worker builds prompts, calls the gateway, validates mutations and commits the canonical result.

The planned destination.

Follows the ratified staged-authority direction: shadow verification, canonical server turns, then multiplayer. This direction also addresses solo consistency and gateway trust; its value does not depend only on host disconnects.

↔

↔
Isolated workerVersioned shared engine; canonical computation
↔
Gateway + providerBounded, recorded upstream attempts

The service freezes the plan and funding allocation, reserves every participant’s share atomically and creates a durable job. A fenced worker loads the pinned engine and exact base revision, builds the prompt and calls the provider outside a database transaction. Persist the raw response and usage; apply valid mutations in an isolated candidate; atomically commit state, transcript, memory, events, receipt, usage attribution and outbox. Broadcast only after commit. Duplicate deliveries return existing results.

Use one shared engine source or immutable byte-verified bundle, not independently maintained browser/server parsers. Prove prompt, memory, clock and mutation parity on real transcripts and mature saves. A headless test loader demonstrates portability, not production isolation. Bound workers and isolate concurrent rooms; never swap global state in a shared asynchronous execution context. Imported content is data, never executable code.

Finish stable entity identity, legacy-name reconciliation and schema migration. An account/seat/PC binding survives renaming, deaths and roster changes without giving another client control. Ambiguous imports are held for reconciliation. Rooms pin engine artifacts; upgrade at a committed boundary with a checkpoint and a tested migration. Use shadow comparison before cutover, respecting the existing protected-code review process.

Campaign owners can manage admission and room ceilings, but cannot raise another player’s personal ceiling or post arbitrary canonical state. Legacy whole-save routes must reject room writes even from the owner. A permitted export creates a separate solo fork, never an upload path back into room authority. Guest character-library export is an explicit previewed copy with provenance, not automatic overwrite of a personal character.

B can continue without the original owner’s PC only if a replacement facilitator and valid funding were authorized beforehand. Service availability does not authorize play as an absent PC. The operator and model provider remain trust parties, and generated prose may still reveal a secret included in its context. B can keep full canon off the owner’s client; private scenes between players require a later retrieval, prompt and projection design.

06 / Identity, access and hostile input

Protect credentials, private canon, character resources, player consent, availability and every participating player’s budget. Consider an uninvited link-holder, malicious admitted player, compromised browser, dishonest host in A, stale executor and client bypassing the UI. The model never grants identity, roles, money or tool permissions.

Admission and session recovery

Each player uses their own supported Google/GitHub identity. The owner admits that identity to a seat and unclaimed PC. Use a random invitation with at least 128 bits of entropy, stored hashed, with proposed 15-minute expiry, bounded redemptions and rate-limited lookup. Enforce the four-seat cap atomically. An invitation is an admission opportunity, not a long-lived account credential. Reject the shared dev_local login identity for production rooms.

Serve the dedicated room UI and API under one HTTPS origin. Proposed room session: Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie; 30-minute idle and eight-hour absolute expiry; explicit CSRF protection and exact Origin checks. Renewal rotates the session secret and rechecks membership/version. After session expiry, reauthentication restores a still-valid membership without another invitation. A revoke invalidates future reads, commands, renewal, sockets and media access. Account logout revokes the associated room sessions as well.

Use a one-use socket ticket, proposed 60-second lifetime, bound to room/member/origin and sent in the first authentication frame. Until authentication succeeds, no room data is sent; expire idle unauthenticated connections. Browser WebSocket cannot simply reuse an arbitrary HTTP Authorization header. Check membership throughout the session, not only at handshake. Tokens and invitation URLs must not appear in query strings or operational logs. Exact Origin is defense in depth, not proof of identity against nonbrowser clients.

Harden the OAuth handoff before reuse: checked expiry at redemption, one-use consumption, safe serialization, exact recipient origin, expected popup source and redacted request logging. Existing origin validation is present; the source-window check is the missing sibling. Legacy file:// solo use remains separate; room endpoints reject null/hostile origins. The existing wildcard CORS middleware must not override room policy.

Controls that the UI cannot supply

  • Block legacy bypasses. Put rooms in an explicit namespace/mode. Audit state, checkpoints, portraits, imports, library writes and deletion. Room capabilities cannot authorize account-wide endpoints; owners in B cannot bypass command authority with a blob save.
  • Validate every boundary. Schema/type/size/depth allowlists, safe object keys, explicit IDs and exact ownership. Render chat/names/actions as text, sanitize limited narrative markup, validate image bytes/dimensions/URLs, and reject active imported HTML/SVG/scripts. Do not fetch arbitrary player-supplied URLs from the server.
  • Protect host and guests. A’s host shell is a hostile-input surface. Exercise narration, DICE, imports and transcript replay there too. Give room surfaces a restrictive CSP and reviewed script/event bindings; CSP supplements safe rendering. HttpOnly cookies reduce token reading, not all XSS abuse.
  • Treat model content as data. Separate player/lore text from policy, validate candidate mutations deterministically, and minimize unrevealed context. Never send a full save and hide secret fields with CSS. A prompt instruction alone cannot prevent prose spoilers.
  • Bound abuse. Proposed starting limits: four seats, one writing connection/seat, 2 KiB action, 16 KiB command, ten mutations/minute/seat with a small burst, and bounded sockets/queues. Apply per-member, room, funding-account and IP limits; shared-household players must not unfairly exhaust one another’s allowance.

Controls are proposed requirements, not shipped claims. Reference baselines: OWASP authorization, WebSocket security, and prompt-injection prevention.

07 / Commands, phases and recovery

The client contract should be independent of whether a host adapter or server worker computes the result. Keep one typed command registry with schema, role, phase, resource and cost policy for each kind. Only the execution adapter differs.

Command: { protocolVersion, roomId, commandId, planId,
           expectedStateRev, expectedPlanRev, kind, pcId?, payload }
Authenticated session supplies principalId and membership/version.
Server validates PC ownership; a supplied actor or role is never proof.

stateRev advances for every canonical change, including progression and approved corrections. planRev advances when posted content, participants or funding terms change. The event sequence also orders noncanonical status notices. Account, membership, seat, PC and display name are distinct. Bind command IDs to principal/room/payload: repeat identical commands return the durable receipt; changed content under an old ID is rejected. All phase/revision checks and state transitions happen atomically.

A frozen round never changes its plan or base revision. Explicit retry of an unchanged failed round creates a new attempt ID and reservation after renewed agreement, retaining its original funding roster; changing that roster requires a new round. Editing its plan or changing its base state abandons that frozen round and creates a new round at the next Resolve. Keep a tombstone so delayed responses cannot become a new result. A transport retry of a commit resubmits the same candidate and identity; it never authorizes another generation.

Pause is a recorded hold over the underlying phase, not a way to forget an in-flight request. A call may finish while paused; retain its candidate without publishing. Resume uses the consent rule in §02. Membership changes cannot quietly reduce the voters or supply an absent person’s action. Phase and consent are checked again at finalization where required.

Attempts, takeover and deadlines

Proposed prototype configuration: one generation attempt per explicit Resolve, 180-second upstream limit and 240-second end-to-end resolution deadline. Disable nested solo retries and fallback calls for room operations. These are experimental bounds, not demonstrated support for every model. A timeout, lost connection or upstream 5xx does not prove the provider did no work; only positive evidence of no dispatch permits releasing that reservation as unused.

Renew a short execution lease by heartbeat, bounded by the job deadline. On takeover, atomically change the executor epoch without changing the frozen plan. The successor inherits the attempt’s known/uncertain status; it may recover a durable response under its new lease. A stale executor cannot commit, although its late response may be retained as recovery evidence. Expiring a lease never authorizes a second paid call.

Summarization and era compilation are separately recorded prerequisite jobs with budgets and deadlines. Planning may continue while they run; a dependent round waits for their canonical commit and renewed base-state acknowledgment. Do not hide several paid maintenance/retry calls inside a nominally single room turn.

Dice, progression and reconnects

The initial networking prototype explicitly disables player-roll mode. Its DICE tags are model-reported outcomes, not independently generated fair dice. If player rolls are required for the campaign pilot, add immutable check IDs, checked pcId, an awaiting-roll phase and one durable result per check. Only the owning seat may request the roll; server-generated results in B must be recorded and reused. Another seat’s intention cannot lapse the check. A still cannot prove a dishonest host used the roll faithfully.

For a real campaign, replace guest auto-picks with the same owned levelChoice protocol as the hero: bind to offered choices, validate legality and reject reuse. Hold further resolution while choices are owed. Imported characters must pass the admission policy before entering the canonical roster.

Reconnect from an authorized event cursor; if history was compacted, return a current projection plus paginated narrative. Bound slow-consumer buffers and close stalled sockets with a resumable status. One seat has one writing lease; a second tab reads or explicitly takes over while preserving its unsent draft. Exports, errors and replay use the same privacy projection. The WebSocket standard exposes buffered bytes; the application must still impose bounds.

08 / Privacy, retention and ownership

In A, the host, service operator and provider can read private canon. In B, the operator and provider can, while the owner’s browser need not. Neither is end-to-end encrypted. Both first releases share scenes with all admitted players; strong secrets between players need a later context/retrieval/projection design. A model given unrevealed information may still disclose it in prose even when raw state filtering is correct.

Before admission, identify the selected GM provider, enabled narration vendors, operator/backup recipients and history being shared. Provider or canonical-configuration changes occur at a committed boundary, advance the configuration revision, and require renewed participant acknowledgment before new paid work. Never change provider under a frozen attempt. Guests control their intentions, not a power to edit gold or rewrite game consequences. Full owner exports and public/own-character guest exports are different permissions. Exporting progression into a personal library is explicit and previewed.

09 / Funding, operations and recovery

Both product methods use the service gateway. The operator pays providers; participating players share the room’s usage charge through their individual account allowances or credits. BYOK and a new server key vault are not part of this pilot.

Owner decision · split the shared cost

18 September 2026: divide shared usage equally among participating players. The intended incentive is lower usage cost per person when playing with friends. The room owner pays the same share as another participant. This replaces the earlier owner-funds-the-room proposal; equal sharing is settled, while plan prices and the conversion from measured usage to allowance units remain open.

An ordinary round combines the party’s actions into one GM request and distributes one result to every screen. Charge for the shared work once, then allocate it: each player’s share = total room usage charge / participating players. Joining, reading, reconnecting, duplicate delivery and replay add no AI usage charge. Four participants do not each consume a full solo-turn allowance for the same result.

Consent and membership

Before paid work, show each player the shared purpose, participant count, equal share, reservation maximum and failure/refund terms. Bind consent to a versioned funding plan and personal ceiling. Freeze the distinct participating accounts at round lock; Help or Pass still participates and shares the cost. NPCs, duplicate tabs and additional characters on the same account do not add payers or dilute the charge. A roster or funding change before lock clears readiness and requires fresh agreement.

A disconnect or removal after lock does not erase an already authorized share or move it to the remaining players. Joining or leaving changes only future work after the roster and consent are updated; newcomers never inherit earlier charges. If anyone cannot reserve their share, dispatch nothing, release any provisional holds and show a funding-blocked status without exposing their balance. No automatic overdraft, redistribution or fallback to charging the host. Reconnect and recovery retain the original allocation.

Shared maintenance and any later shared audio use the same allocation rule, with their own purpose, consenting participant snapshot and ceiling before dispatch. Prerequisite jobs may use the agreed planning roster before a round is locked; that roster remains attached to the job. An uncertain retry needs renewed acknowledgment from every affected payer and a new bounded reservation. Historical charges and refunds always use the original job allocation. Personal paid extras, if later introduced, require separate explicit opt-in and cannot silently charge the table.

Two linked ledgers

Keep one room/job charge with per-account reservations, fractional allocations, settlement and refund entries, linked to the operator record for each upstream request: room, round/job/attempt, funding-plan ID, purpose, provider/model, token/cache usage, status and measured cost. The provider request is recorded once, not copied as a separate full-cost request for each player. Unknown cost stays unknown. The server assigns purpose; X-TND-Kind supplied by a client cannot choose which quota applies.

Reserve all shares before dispatch in an all-or-nothing transaction; settle or refund each allocation idempotently by job/attempt and account. Preserve fractional usage internally and carry unavoidable settlement remainders fairly so allocations sum exactly to the room charge; never round each player up to a whole turn. Each player sees the room total, their own share and settlement, not another account’s balance. Final debits cannot exceed accepted personal or room ceilings; excess provider exposure stays with the operator.

Enforce atomic account-wide and room-wide limits across every paid route, including auxiliary work and the legacy gateway. Start with one active paid job per room and funding account, finite tokens and mandatory ceilings; concurrent solo/room jobs must not spend the same allowance twice. No guest Table Talk, per-seat suggestion call or paid room art/narration in the initial networking pilot. Use deterministic suggestions or those already in the committed response. Later audio reuse must avoid synthesizing the same paragraph independently for every listener.

Hosting/participation eligibility, room usage weights, reservation pricing and failed-attempt refund policy remain decisions; equal division is not an open pricing fork. A model refusal can consume provider cost without advancing canon; the player charge follows the disclosed settlement policy. Unset entitlement/budget configuration disables paid room creation. The existing per-IP HTTP limiter is useful abuse protection, not a substitute for these controls.

Measure a session as sum(actual GM + maintenance + auxiliary + audio attempts) + compute + storage/egress. Record shared totals and each player’s allocation, failed and ambiguous attempts, cache usage and per-round totals. Separate actual operator cost from the configured player allowance charge. Measure the per-person saving rather than inferring it from player count. There is no defensible A/B cost ranking or calendar estimate yet. Estimate work after the isolation spike, separating work shared with B from A-only work.

Runtime and rollback

A single coordinator and transactional SQLite store may fit a measured pilot. Keep transactions short and outside inference; WAL still permits one writer at a time. Multiple instances need shared authority and fencing, not divergent database files. SQLite transactions · WAL.

The inspected Fly configuration permits auto-stop with zero minimum machines and a 256 MB VM. Keep the job executor running while work exists; for a single primary-region worker, set a suitable minimum or disable its auto-stop. Test all clients leaving during inference and measure memory before adopting this footprint. Fly’s minimum applies to the primary region; it does not protect against crashes or replace durable jobs.

Feature-flag creation and writes separately from recovery. Keep a versioned read/export service, including old-schema support and authorization, available when room writes/UI are rolled back. The recovery unit is complete checkpoint, committed event cursor, receipts and engine/schema identity. Restore backups into isolated staging; compare history and resources, reapply deletions/revocations, and only then permit writes. Never silently downgrade a save or bypass room controls through solo import.

10 / Expert-panel synthesis

The nine existing rubrics provide grounded design questions, not interviews or endorsements by the named people. Numbered citations refer to the project’s source entries. Applying a craft principle to a networked game is the document author’s interpretation; it does not turn that principle into technical certification or an architecture vote.

Gygax · consistent procedure

1.1, 1.2, 2.4, 5.2, 5.3, 7.3

Require attributable declarations, consistent consequences and one canonical resource/clock change. A relies on the host honoring that procedure; B can enforce the commit boundary against browsers. Test repeated commands and distinct class contributions. The lens’s recorded reservations about computer storytelling remain; no blanket AI-GM endorsement follows.

Laws · the needs of the table

1.1, 4.3, 8.1–8.4; 6.1–6.2

Evaluate who gets meaningful attention. A’s host dependence and B’s durable waiting are different interruptions, neither automatically enjoyable. Parallel drafts, public recaps and separate clarification are proposed applications, not mechanisms prescribed by these sources. Measure the quiet/slow player’s experience and first-ready waiting time.

Harper · agency and less preparation

1.2, 1.7, 5.1, 5.5, 7.8, 7.9, 9.4

Agreed intent and stakes matter in both methods; paying or hosting does not authorize another PC’s goal. The planning-cutting principle is an explicit dissent against board overhead. Ready-on-plan is this design’s proposed consent mechanism, to be rejected or simplified if it burdens play. Test opposed intentions, bargains and time spent planning.

Baker · a conversation among people

2.3, 3.4, 4.3, 6.3, 7.1, 7.4 · Apocalypse World principles credit Meguey and Vincent Baker.

Neither host control nor server availability grants absent-player consent. Keep posted, ready, passed, paused and disconnected distinct; allow players to answer one another. The precise presence/withdrawal protocol is an engineering application. Test disconnect before lock, after lock and during generation.

Moran · different ways to contribute

3.3, 5.3, 8.1; 2.6

Quiet acts and relationships should remain consequential under either architecture. Helping and explicit Pass are proposed options, not rules established by the cited reward system, which is not imported. Avoid compulsory busywork, emotional performance rankings or equal-prose quotas. Test a supporting character who chooses not to lead.

Le Guin · a coherent ensemble

1.1, 1.2, 1.8; 4.1–4.5, 5.1, 6.1, 6.3

Both methods need readable attribution and rhythm. Keep the existing D12 third-person multiplayer voice and consequential quiet actions; put network status outside fiction. Read and listen to two- and four-PC scenes, especially with similar names. The literary lens offers no preference about where the engine runs.

Leiber · companionship that develops

3.1–3.5, 4.1–4.2, 10.1–10.2 · secondary critical readings.

A must avoid making guests the host’s sidekicks; B must preserve more than inventories. Let PCs converse, remember consequential cooperation/friction, and reach a stopping beat. Test a disagreement followed by a joint choice and later callback. These are applications of criticism, not imagined opinions from Leiber on online games.

Abercrombie · distinct people and consequences

1.2, 1.5, 5.1, 5.5, 7.3, 8.1, 8.4

Both methods should preserve contrasting intentions and legible success, failure and costs. Do not invent a silent player’s agreement, confession or emotional reaction. Compare accepted declarations with resulting actions. Craft principles do not grant authorship over a human player’s inner life or require darker content.

Jemisin · discovery and consequential choice

1.8, 7.1–7.4, 9.3–9.4, 10.1, 10.3

Distinguish revealed knowledge from engine truth. A exposes canon to its host; B still risks spoilers through prose or recaps. Disclose shared split scenes and test canaries across every read/export surface, alongside visibly different consequences for different choices. Literary discovery is a motivation for privacy tests, not proof of secrecy.

Result: preserve agency, attribution, conversation and continuity while testing the cost of procedural consent. The rubrics do not decide A versus B. The architecture recommendation is an engineering judgment; the real tension between quick entry and explicit plan review remains a playtest question.

12 / Delivery and acceptance

Sequence work around evidence

  1. Finish solo security verification. Confirm the intended #422/#423 builds and the honest two-account import path independently of multiplayer. Harden the remaining auth/rendering surfaces before admitting untrusted room content.
  2. Test the social loop cheaply. Use an existing group call with disposable fixed-level hot-seat play, or a fake-GM four-browser board. Observe attribution, consent and waiting. This does not validate production security or real-model group quality; a subsequent real-GM trial is necessary.
  3. Prove candidate isolation and recovery. Run A’s reject/accept/reload spike under the protected-code review process. Break down shared engine/room work and A-only work before estimating delivery.
  4. Select the implementation sequence. Build A only if it delivers useful earlier evidence at justified extra cost. Otherwise apply the same UI, authorization and protocol work directly to staged B. Prove browser/server parity before canonical cutover.
  5. Run a limited campaign pilot. Use separate campaign copies, mandatory budgets, agreed dice/progression scope and trusted groups. Expand only after security, recovery, long-session and real-player gates pass.

Decisions before a paid campaign pilot

  • Four total humans, or a larger party requiring cap and dependent-system changes?
  • Does measured demand and the isolation spike justify an A pilot before B?
  • Is disclosed model-reported dice acceptable, or are owned checks required before the pilot?
  • What imported-character admission and equal-progression rules apply?
  • Which subscriptions may host or participate, how is the shared room usage charge measured, and what ceilings and failed-attempt refund policy apply? Equal division among participating players is already decided.
  • What history-sharing, absence, safety-pause, moderation, deletion and backup policies does the table accept?
  • Does the posted-plan consent mechanism pass the actual playability test?

Owner-decided funding is equal shared usage across participating players. Proposed defaults are account-backed seats, gateway funding, four humans, assigned characters, text-first presentation, no guest Table Talk and no secret subscenes. A real campaign pilot adds equal progression and explicitly chooses its dice contract. These decisions define the next implementation brief; this research changes no game code.
